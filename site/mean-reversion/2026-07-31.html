7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the method — what each structure is and when it fits. The actual setups and their specific levels are in the tables above.

RSI (14-day) — A 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling pressure may be exhausting). Above 70 = overbought (buying pressure may be exhausting). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme.
Bollinger %B (20,2) — Shows where price sits within its volatility bands. Below 0 = price below the lower band (extremely stretched down). Above 1 = above the upper band (extremely stretched up).
Z-score — How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day mean. Beyond ±2 is statistically uncommon; beyond ±3 is rare and often marks exhaustion.
% from 50d / 200d SMA — How far price has moved from its average. Large gaps tend to close over time — that's the mean-reversion thesis.
IV (Implied Volatility) — The market's "fear gauge" on a specific stock. Elevated IV means options are expensive, so selling premium (credit spreads) is more attractive than buying it.
Bull Put Credit Spread — Sell a put at a higher strike, buy a put at a lower strike. Collect premium if price stays above the short strike. Max loss is the width of the spread minus premium received. Best for: oversold + high IV.
Call Credit Spread — Sell a call at a lower strike, buy a call at a higher strike. Collect premium if price stays below the short strike. Best for: overbought + high IV.
Bull Call Spread — Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Pay a net debit; profit if price rises to the short strike. Best for: oversold + low/moderate IV.
Bear Put Spread — Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. Pay a net debit; profit if price falls to the short strike. Best for: overbought + low/moderate IV.
Cash-Secured Put — Sell a put and hold enough cash to buy the stock if assigned. Income strategy; you own the stock at your strike price if assigned. Best for: oversold + high IV, when you'd be comfortable buying the stock at that price.
Iron Condor — Sell both an OTM call spread and an OTM put spread simultaneously. Profit if price stays range-bound. Best for: high IV + expectation of sideways/consolidation.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter — the most important screen: An oversold reading alone is not a buy signal. Before acting on any setup, ask: is the move driven by (a) sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, or (b) a structural break? Structural breaks — fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, −50%+ collapse with no stabilization — are not reversion setups. They are falling knives. Stocks like STX (Seagate, dropped ~20%+ and already up 1,085% over 5 years with extreme valuation vs. GF Value) were reviewed and excluded from the oversold table because the valuation re-rating risk outweighs a pure technical bounce thesis.

Special Rules — Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs:

Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Only trade when stretched UP — fade the spike. Never buy them as "oversold" — structural contango decay grinds them lower over time. A low RSI on VXX is not a setup; it's the drift.

Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, TBF, 2x/3x anything): Volatility decay + roll costs mean they cannot be held for weeks without significant drag. Use for short-term tactical reversion only; for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered version or an equity-sector proxy.

Futures-based ETFs (USO, UNG, UGA): These track futures, not spot prices. Contango roll drag erodes value over time. USO, UNG, and UGA issue Schedule K-1 tax forms — prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB for broad commodity; BNO for Brent).

Equity proxies (GDX, GDXJ, XLE, XOP, COPX, URA, MOO, SIL): These track companies, not the commodity. They carry stock-market beta and can diverge from the underlying commodity (e.g., energy stocks up 40% YTD while oil was more volatile on geopolitics).

The 5-question pre-trade checklist for any reversion setup:

  1. Is the stretch on 3+ measures, or just RSI alone?
  2. Is the "why it moved" catalyst fading (reversion) or permanent (regime change)?
  3. Is there an earnings/FDA/binary event in the next ~30 days that could override the technical setup?
  4. Am I using defined-risk structures (spreads) rather than unlimited-risk positions?
