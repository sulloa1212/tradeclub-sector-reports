7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the generic logic used to match direction and implied volatility to a defined-risk strategy. The specific setups for each name are in the tables above — this legend is the "how we reason" behind those recommendations.

RSI (0–100 Momentum Gauge)

Measures the speed of recent gains vs losses. Below 30 = selling pressure may be exhausting (oversold). Above 70 = buying pressure may be exhausting (overbought). Below 20 = extreme oversold. Above 80 = extreme overbought. RSI alone is never enough — use with other signals.

Bollinger %B (Band Position)

Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands (a moving price envelope). Below 0 = price is outside the lower band (stretched low). Above 1 = price is outside the upper band (stretched high). Combined with RSI, it strengthens the signal.

Z-Score (Statistical Stretch)

How many standard deviations (how many "average daily moves") price is from its recent mean. Above +2.0 or below −2.0 = statistically unusual — reversion more likely than continuation.

IV (Implied Volatility)

What options buyers are paying for uncertainty — expressed as annualized % move. High IV = options are expensive → favor selling premium (credit spreads). Low IV = options are cheap → favor buying premium (debit spreads). IV rank compares today's IV to the past 52 weeks.

Oversold + High IV → Premium-Selling

Cash-secured put: sell a put below current price, collect premium, obligated to buy if assigned. Bull put spread: sell a put, buy a lower put — cap your max loss (defined risk). Bull call spread: buy a call, sell a higher call — lower cost than buying outright.

Oversold + Low IV → Premium-Buying

Bull call spread or long call: pay a debit to get bullish exposure. Costs less when IV is low. Risk = premium paid only (fully defined).

Overbought + High IV → Premium-Selling

Bear call spread: sell a call, buy a higher call — collect premium with capped risk. Iron condor: sell both a put spread and a call spread — collect on both sides (expects range-bound after a vol spike).

Overbought + Low IV → Premium-Buying

Bear put spread: buy a put, sell a lower put — debit to get bearish exposure with defined risk. Long put: pays off if price falls. Risk = premium paid only.

8Guardrails — How to Use This Scan Safely

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter
A low RSI alone tells you selling was fast, not that it will reverse. Reject these patterns immediately:
• Structural breaks: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, debt blowup.
• Permanent re-ratings: a business model made obsolete (e.g., a mainframe business losing clients to cloud — watch IBM carefully).
• Buyout pins: stock pinned to a deal price — no mean to revert to.
• −50% in days with no stabilization — likely more to come.
Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business at a prior support level.

⚠ Proxy / K-1 / Leverage Caveats

Proxy ETFs (GDX, XLE, XOP, OIH, URA, COPX, etc.) track companies, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying significantly.

K-1 tax filing required for: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, most CurrencyShares. Factor in tax cost; consider PDBC (no K-1) as an alternative for broad commodity exposure.

Leveraged / futures ETFs (UCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, TBT, 2x/3x anything): volatility decay + contango/backwardation mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Short-term tactical use only — prefer the unlevered fund for multi-week holds.

⚠ Long-Vol ETPs — Never "Buy the Oversold"

VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX: structural roll decay (VIX futures contango) grinds these lower over time. A low RSI on a long-vol ETP is not a buy signal — it's just drift. The only mean-reversion trade on these instruments is fading a spike upward. Today there is no spike. Do not buy.
