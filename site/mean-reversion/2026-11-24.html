7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the method — what each structure is and when it fits. The specific trade ideas for each name are in Sections 2–5. Always use defined-risk structures and paper-trade first.

Bull Call Spread (Debit Spread)
Buy a call at strike A, sell a call at higher strike B. Max loss = premium paid. Max gain = difference between strikes minus premium. Use when: oversold + moderate IV — you pay for the right to participate in an upward reversion without unlimited risk. Best when IV is NOT elevated.
Put Credit Spread (Credit Spread)
Sell a put at strike A, buy a put at lower strike B. Net premium collected upfront. Max loss = spread width minus premium. Use when: oversold + elevated IV — you collect premium for agreeing to buy the stock at a lower level, with a floor on your risk. Best in high-IV environments.
Call Credit Spread (Credit Spread)
Sell a call at strike A, buy a call at higher strike B. Net premium collected. Max loss = spread width minus premium. Use when: overbought + elevated IV — you sell premium against an extended upside move, with a cap on your risk.
Bear Put Spread (Debit Spread)
Buy a put at strike A, sell a put at lower strike B. Max loss = premium paid. Max gain = difference between strikes minus premium. Use when: overbought + moderate IV — you pay for the right to profit from a downward reversion. Best when IV is NOT elevated.
Cash-Secured Put
Sell one put, hold enough cash to buy 100 shares at the strike price if assigned. Use when: oversold + elevated IV, and you are willing to own the stock at the strike. The collected premium is your cushion. Defined-risk in dollar terms (the stock going to zero is worst case).
RSI / %B / Z-Score — What They Mean
RSI (0–100): Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought. Extreme: <20 / >80.
Bollinger %B (20,2): Below 0 = below the lower band (oversold); above 1 = above the upper band (overbought).
Z-score: How many standard deviations (units of normal variation) price sits from its 50-day mean. ±2 = historically stretched. A name extreme on 3+ measures is a stronger setup than RSI alone.

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. We reject any name where the decline is driven by: fraud or going-concern risk, bankruptcy or debt blowup, failed drug trial, buyout pinning (the stock now tracks a deal price, not its mean), or a structural >50% collapse with no stabilization.
