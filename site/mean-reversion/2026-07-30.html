7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains how to match direction and IV level to an educational structure — not a recommendation to trade any specific instrument.

Oversold + Elevated IV
Best educational structures: Cash-secured put (collect premium below support); put credit spread (sell a put, buy a lower put — caps both gain and loss); bull call spread (defined cost, defined max gain). You're trying to own the reversion risk while limiting what you can lose.
Oversold + Low IV
Best educational structure: Bull call spread or outright long call — when IV is low, options are cheaper, so buying premium is more attractive than selling it. Defined risk either way.
Overbought + Elevated IV
Best educational structures: Call credit spread (sell a call, buy a higher call); bear put spread (buy a put, sell a lower put). Elevated IV means premium is rich — selling premium with a spread cap is the educational edge.
Overbought + Low IV
Best educational structure: Bear put spread or outright long put — when IV is low, buying directional premium is cheaper and more attractive than trying to sell thin premium.
Post-earnings IV Crush
After a big earnings move, IV typically collapses ("IV crush"). Strategies that were built around selling IV before earnings (credit spreads, iron condors) collect that crush. Strategies that bought premium before earnings often lose even if the direction was right. Always check if IV has already collapsed before entering.
Vol Spike Fade (UVXY/VXX only)
When long-vol ETPs spike to extreme levels, fading via a call credit spread on UVXY or a put debit spread on SVXY captures both the mean-reversion and the structural roll decay. Use only on confirmed spikes (not gradual drift). Defined risk only. Paper-trade first.
Indicator definitions (for new traders):
RSI — 0–100 momentum gauge; <30 = oversold; >70 = overbought Bollinger %B — price position within bands; <0 = below lower band (oversold); >1 = above upper band (overbought) Z-score — how many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average; ±2 = very stretched 50-day SMA — average price over the last 50 trading days; used as "the mean" a price reverts toward IV (implied volatility) — the options market's forecast of future price swings; high IV = expensive options ATR (average true range) — average daily price swing; useful for sizing positions

8How to Read This & Guardrails

The most important filter: Reversion vs. Ruin. A low RSI alone does NOT make something a buy. These are the automatic disqualifiers:
  • Structural business break: fraud, going-concern, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, dividend cut, accounting restatement
  • A drop of 50%+ in days with no stabilization — likely more to come
  • A buyout/merger repricing — stock is pinned to deal, not its mean
What we keep: Names where the move looks like sentiment or positioning excess over an otherwise intact business — ideally near a prior support level. MRNA (#5 in the oversold table) was flagged as a potential ruin candidate, not a clean reversion setup.
Special handling — vol, leveraged, and futures-based ETFs:
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Only trade when stretched UP on a spike. Never buy as "oversold" — structural roll decay grinds them lower permanently. Low RSI = drift, not a setup.
  • Inverse-vol ETPs (SVXY, SVIX): Mirror image. Their reversion setup is the recovery after a vol crash, not fading their grind higher.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, TBT, etc.): Volatility decay + contango means they deviate from any long-run mean. Short-term tactical reversion only; for multi-week holds prefer the unlevered or equity-proxy equivalent.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1. Note this before trading. No-K-1 alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX/GDXJ (gold miners), URA (uranium companies), XOP (exploration companies) track equity companies, not the physical commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying commodity price.
Composite stretch rule: A name extreme on 3+ measures (RSI below 30 AND Bollinger %B below 0 AND Z-score ±2+ AND below 50-day/200-day AND 5+ consecutive down days) has a stronger reversion thesis than a name only extreme on RSI. The tables above prioritize multi-measure extremes. META (RSI 16, below all MAs, consecutive losing days, massive ATR expansion) is a higher-quality reversion candidate on the composite than a name with only RSI 28.
