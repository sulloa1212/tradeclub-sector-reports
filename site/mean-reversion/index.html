7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what each structure is and when to consider it. Specific results for each ticker are in the tables above.

Oversold + High IV

Cash-secured put: Sell a put below the stock price; you collect premium and agree to buy the stock if it falls further. Profit if the stock stays above your strike. Best when you want to own the stock at a lower price.
Put credit spread: Sell a put, buy a lower put. Caps both your risk and your reward. Best for defined-risk traders who don't want to own the stock.

Oversold + Low IV

Bull call spread: Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Costs a premium upfront; profits if the stock rises. Lower IV means these debit spreads cost less. Best when you expect a move but IV is cheap.

Overbought + High IV

Bear call spread (call credit spread): Sell a call at or near the current price, buy a higher call to cap risk. Profits if the stock falls or stays flat. Elevated IV makes the premium you collect larger.
Bear put spread: Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a lower put. Costs a premium; profits if the stock falls. Prefer when IV is moderate and you want directional exposure.

Vol Spike Fade (VXX / UVXY)

Call credit spread on VXX/UVXY: Sell a call near the current elevated price, buy a higher call to cap risk. Profits from either a vol crush (price drops) or time decay (structural decay grinds it lower). Only use when RSI/price is spiked UP. Never use this in the other direction.

RSI Explained

RSI (0–100 momentum gauge): Below 30 = oversold (fell fast — may bounce); above 70 = overbought (rose fast — may pull back); below 20 or above 80 = extreme. RSI alone is not a trade signal — use with at least 2 other confirmations.

Bollinger %B Explained

Bollinger %B (0–1 band gauge): Shows where price is relative to 2-standard-deviation bands. Below 0 = price is outside the lower band (extreme oversold); above 1 = price is outside the upper band (extreme overbought). Confirms RSI readings.

Z-Score Explained

Z-score (standard-deviation stretch): How many standard deviations the price is from its 50-day mean. A Z-score of ±2 or beyond is statistically unusual — prices revert to Z=0 (the mean) over time. Confirms the "stretch" in the composite score.

Defined-Risk First

Always use defined-risk structures — spreads cap your maximum loss. Naked options (selling calls or puts without a hedge) can lose many times your premium. As a rule: always know your max loss before you enter, and paper-trade first until you are consistently profitable.

8How to Read / Guardrails

The most important filters in this framework — the ones that separate a reversion opportunity from a trap.

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter: A low RSI is necessary but NOT sufficient. These names were screened out: ARI (Apollo Commercial RE, –62% one month, structural), MERC (Mercer International, –91.8% one month, near-zero price, possible going-concern), any name with fraud/bankruptcy/failed drug/accounting restatement, buyout targets (price pinned to deal, not mean).

✅ Keep (reversion candidate):

  • Move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business
  • Price is near a prior support or resistance level — the "mean" is well-defined
  • No structural break (no fraud, no bankruptcy, no dividend cut, no going-concern language)
  • Sufficient option liquidity — verify open interest before entering
  • At least 3 stretch measures confirm the extreme (not RSI alone)

🚫 Reject (falling knife / trap):

  • –50%+ in days with no stabilization — likely more to come
  • Structural driver: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed trial, accounting restatement
  • Buyout/merger repricing — stock is pinned to deal price, not mean
  • Volume collapse + price collapse together (distribution, not overselling)
