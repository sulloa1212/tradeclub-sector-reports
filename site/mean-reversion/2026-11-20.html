8Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the method — what each term means generically. The specific trade contexts (which setup uses which structure) are in the body above.

Direction + IV → Defined-Risk Structure (all options trades carry risk; always use defined-risk structures first; paper-trade before using real money)
Oversold + High IV: Sell premium — put credit spread (sell a put, buy a lower put) or cash-secured put. You collect time premium while betting the stock doesn't fall below your short strike. High IV means fatter premiums to collect.
Oversold + Low IV: Buy premium — bull call spread (buy a call, sell a higher call). You pay a small defined debit to bet on a bounce. Low IV means cheaper options to buy.
Overbought + High IV: Sell premium — call credit spread (sell a call, buy a higher call) or bear put spread. You collect premium betting the asset doesn't rally past your short strike.
Overbought + Low IV: Buy premium — bear put spread (buy a put, sell a lower put). Small defined debit to bet on a fade.
Vol Spike Fade: Bear call spread on UVXY/VXX (defined risk if vol keeps spiking) or long put on UVXY. As VIX reverts, these gain. Never naked short vol — unlimited risk on another spike.

Indicator definitions (what the numbers mean):

RSI (14-day): A 0–100 gauge of recent buying vs. selling speed. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone); above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme.
Bollinger %B: Shows where price sits within its 20-day volatility band. Below 0 = price is below the lower band (stretched down); above 1 = above the upper band (stretched up). Confirms RSI extremes.
Z-score: How many standard deviations (units of "normal variation") price is from its 50-day average. ±2 or beyond is statistically unusual. ±3 is rare — high reversion probability.
ATR (14-day): Average True Range — the typical daily price swing in dollars. Used to gauge whether a move is large or small relative to normal for that asset.
Implied Volatility (IV): How expensive options are right now. High IV = fat premiums → favor selling structures. Low IV = cheap premiums → favor buying structures. IV often spikes during selloffs and reverts after.
VIX backwardation: When near-term VIX futures are more expensive than longer-dated ones — a sign of acute near-term fear. Normally futures slope upward (contango). Backwardation = panic premium embedded in short-term contracts.
Composite stretch: Our ranking method: a name showing extremes on RSI AND %B AND Z-score AND multiple consecutive down/up days beats a name oversold on RSI alone. More signals = higher confidence in the reversion thesis.

9How to Read This Scan / Guardrails

The most important rule: low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. An oversold reading flags a candidate — it does not confirm a trade. Apply the ruin filter below before acting on anything.

Reversion vs. Ruin Filter — why we reject some "cheap" names

  • REJECT: Any move driven by fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, or accounting restatement. A low RSI on a broken company is a falling knife.
  • REJECT: Drops of 50%+ in days with no stabilization — these are likely regime changes, not positioning extremes.

Special Handling — Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade them only when spiked UP — as a fade. Never treat a low RSI in these funds as a "buy the dip" opportunity. Structural roll decay and leverage drag grind them lower over time — a low price is drift, not a setup.
