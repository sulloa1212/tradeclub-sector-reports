7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the methodology — why a given direction + IV level suggests a specific strategy. It does NOT mean you should trade any of these; always paper-trade first and confirm data in your brokerage.

RSI (14-day) A 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (recent selling may be overdone). Above 70 = overbought (recent buying may be extended). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. RSI alone is not sufficient — require 3+ confirming measures.
Bollinger %B (20,2) Measures where price sits relative to its Bollinger Bands (a volatility envelope around a 20-day average). Below 0 = price outside lower band (oversold). Above 1 = price outside upper band (overbought). %B <0 or >1 is a strong stretch signal.
Z-score / % from moving average How far price is from its average, measured in standard deviations or percent. A stock 15%+ below its 50-day SMA is statistically unusual and often reverts. Higher % = more stretched.
IV (Implied Volatility) The market's expectation of future price swings, priced into options. High IV = options are "expensive." Low IV = options are "cheap." For reversion setups, high IV favors option-selling strategies (collect premium); low IV favors option-buying.
Oversold + High IV → Selling strategies Cash-secured put: sell a put below current price; collect premium; take assignment if wrong. Bull put spread: sell lower put, buy even-lower put — defines max loss. Bull call spread: buy lower call, sell higher call — limited cost.
Overbought + High IV → Selling strategies Call credit spread: sell OTM call, buy higher-strike call — profit if stock falls/stays flat. Bear put spread: buy put, sell lower put — profit if stock falls. Both define max loss.
Oversold + Low IV → Buying strategies Long call: buy a call option, profit if stock rises. Long call spread: buy lower-strike call, sell higher-strike call — lower cost, capped upside. Defined risk = maximum loss is the premium paid.
Defined-risk first — always Every structure listed in this report limits your maximum loss to a known dollar amount before entry. Never use undefined-risk structures (naked puts/calls) without expert guidance and appropriate account approval. Paper-trade all strategies before using real money.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter
A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. We rejected the following types of names from this scan:
  • Structural breaks: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trials, bankruptcy, debt blowup, accounting restatements. These names do not revert — they re-rate permanently lower.
  • Buyout/merger pins: a stock pinned to a deal price is not "cheap" — it's capped. No reversion trade.
  • Falling knives: −40%+ moves in days with no stabilization and no clear support level. These often have more to come.
  • What we kept: names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business — near a prior support/resistance level, with a well-defined "mean" to revert toward.
Example applied today: Several biotech names (ALMS, RARE, ALHC) appeared on oversold screeners with RSI <30 and −35 to −67% one-month moves. These were EXCLUDED because massive single-session drops on clinical/regulatory catalysts are structural re-ratings, not sentiment excess.
Special handling — Vol, leveraged, and futures-based ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — upside fade only. Never buy as "oversold." Structural contango roll decay grinds them lower. Only trade when they spike UP on a fear event.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TBT, UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.) — short-term tactical only. Volatility decay + daily rebalancing means they diverge from any long-run mean. Use for quick tactical trades; prefer the unlevered version for multi-week holds.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UCO, BOIL, DBC, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form at year-end. This adds tax complexity. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or equity-sector ETFs (XLE, XOP) when the exposure allows.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, URA, XOP, COPX, MOO, SIL track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying spot price — especially during broad market selloffs.
  • Options liquidity: Tier A = deep, tight spreads. Tier B = tradable but check open interest. Tier C = verify chains are active before entering — some funds have gone thin or been liquidated.
