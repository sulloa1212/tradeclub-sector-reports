8Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the educational structures mentioned in the tables. All structures are defined-risk — never risk more than you can afford to lose, and always paper-trade first. These are educational examples, not advice.

📊 What the Indicators Mean

  • RSI (0–100 momentum gauge) — Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought; below 20 or above 80 = extreme.
  • Bollinger %B — Below 0 = price below the lower band (oversold); above 1 = above upper band (overbought).
  • Z-score — How many standard deviations the price is from its 50-day mean. ±2 is "stretched"; ±3 is extreme.
  • Distance from moving averages — % above/below 20d, 50d, 200d SMA. The further, the more stretched.
  • ATR — Average True Range = typical daily price swing. A multi-ATR move in a few days is "extended."

📈 Oversold + IV High → Selling Structures (Upside Bias)

  • Cash-secured put — Sell a put below current price; collect premium; keep cash to buy shares if assigned.
  • Bull put spread (put credit spread) — Sell a put, buy a lower-strike put for protection. Net credit. Profits if stock stays above the short strike.
  • Bull call spread — Buy a call, sell a higher-strike call. Net debit. Profits if stock rises toward upper strike. Use when IV is lower.

📉 Overbought + IV High → Selling Structures (Downside Bias)

  • Call credit spread (bear call spread) — Sell a call, buy a higher-strike call. Net credit. Profits if stock stays below the short strike.
  • Bear put spread (put debit spread) — Buy a put, sell a lower-strike put. Net debit. Profits if stock falls. Use when IV is lower.

⚠️ IV Guidance

  • High IV — Options are expensive. Favor selling premium (credit spreads, cash-secured puts) to collect the inflated premium.
  • Low IV — Options are cheap. Favor buying premium (debit spreads) to pay less for directional exposure.
  • IV crush — After a binary event (earnings, Fed), IV drops sharply. Option buyers lose value even if the stock moves; sellers benefit.
  • Always check IV rank/percentile at your brokerage before choosing a structure.

9How to Read This Report / Guardrails

The most important section: how to avoid the traps.

🚨 The Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter (The Most Important Test)

  • Low RSI ≠ buy signal. A stock can be oversold because it's broken, not because selling is excessive. Always ask: is the business structurally impaired?
  • Reject "falling knives": fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cuts, failed drug trials, bankruptcy, accounting restatements, and debt blowups are NOT reversion setups — they are structural impairments.
  • Reject spin-off repricings: CTVA today is a perfect example. A spin-off changes the reference price permanently. There is no mean to revert to at the old price.
  • Reject buyout pins: A stock locked to a deal price is not reverting — it is pinned.
  • Keep: Names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over a sound business, ideally near a prior support/resistance level with intact fundamentals.

⚠️ Special Handling — Leveraged, Futures & Vol ETFs

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX): Trade only when stretched UP — fade the spike. Never buy them as "oversold" — roll decay grinds them lower over time.
  • Inverse-vol ETPs (SVXY, SVIX): They grind up and crash on volatility spikes. Handle with care — the reversion setup is their recovery after a vol crash, not the fade of an uptrend.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, 2x/3x anything): Volatility decay and roll drag mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Short-term tactical only; prefer unlevered funds or equity proxies for multi-week holds.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER issue a Schedule K-1. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where the exposure allows. Consult your tax advisor.
  • Proxy vs spot: GDX/GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX, MOO track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the commodity's price.
