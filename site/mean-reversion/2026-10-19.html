7Options Structures Legend

This is how the report maps direction and IV level to educational defined-risk structures. These are generic method descriptions — every specific result and reason lives in the tables above.

RSI (14) — What It Means

A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = selling may be overdone (potential bounce). Above 70 = buying may be extended (potential pause/pullback). Extremes below 20 or above 80 are more reliable — but still not guarantees.

Bollinger %B (20,2)

Shows where price sits within its volatility bands. Below 0 = below the lower band (stretched low). Above 1 = above the upper band (stretched high). Confirms RSI extremes — a name outside the bands AND with extreme RSI is a stronger setup.

% from 50-day / 200-day SMA

How far price is from its moving average (a simple "mean"). The larger the gap, the more stretched. 50-day = medium-term mean; 200-day = long-term mean. A stretched name tends to eventually return toward these levels.

Z-Score / ATR Distance

Z-score measures how many standard deviations price is from its mean (2+ is notable; 3+ is extreme). ATR (Average True Range) context: a drop of 5× the daily ATR in one session is extreme. Both confirm RSI/Bollinger readings.

Oversold + HIGH IV → Sell Premium

Put credit spread: sell a put, buy a lower-strike put — collect premium if stock stays above the short strike. Cash-secured put: sell a put, keep cash to buy shares if assigned. Both profit if the stock bounces OR stays flat. Defined max loss.

Oversold + LOW IV → Buy Premium

Bull call spread: buy a call, sell a higher-strike call — cap premium cost; profit if stock rises above the long strike. Lower IV means cheaper options. Defined max loss = premium paid.

Overbought + HIGH IV → Sell Premium

Call credit spread: sell a call, buy a higher-strike call — collect premium if stock stays below the short strike. Profit from a pause, pullback, or continued sideways chop. Defined max loss.

Overbought + LOW IV → Buy Premium

Bear put spread: buy a put, sell a lower-strike put — pay a defined premium; profit if stock falls below the long put strike. Defined max loss = premium paid.

IV (Implied Volatility) — What It Means

IV is the market's estimate of how much a stock might move. High IV = expensive options → favors selling premium. Low IV = cheap options → favors buying premium. Always check IV rank (where current IV sits vs. its history) in your broker before trading.

Defined-Risk First — Always

Spreads cap your maximum loss to the difference in strikes minus premium received (or equal to premium paid). Never sell naked options or go unhedged — the risk is theoretically unlimited. Every structure above is designed so you know your max loss before entering.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter. Low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. A falling knife looks like a bargain until it isn't.
  • Reject structural breaks: If the move is driven by fraud, going-concern doubts, a failed trial, bankruptcy, or an accounting restatement — it is not a reversion candidate. CTVA (spin-off repricing) and SMCI (accounting history) are flagged for this reason in this report.
  • Reject buyout/merger pins: A stock near a merger price is pinned to the deal, not its mean — the "mean" has been redefined.
  • Reject enormous drops with no stabilization: A –50%+ drop in days with no floor is more likely "more to go" than "ripe to bounce."
  • Require 3+ confirmations: A name qualifies only when RSI, distance from moving average, and at least one other measure (Bollinger, ATR context, consecutive-day count) all confirm the stretch. Single-indicator setups are lower quality.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX, MOO, SIL, DRAM track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and most commodity partnerships issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — additional filing complexity. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB where available as no-K-1 alternatives.
  • Leveraged / futures ETF caution: SOXL, SOXS, TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, BOIL, TBT, and any 2x/3x fund suffer from volatility decay and contango/backwardation roll costs. Use for short-term tactical plays only — not multi-week holds. Prefer the unlevered fund or sector proxy for longer windows.
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — upside spikes only: Structural roll decay (VIX-futures contango) grinds them lower over time. A low RSI is drift, not a setup. Only trade them when RSI is HIGH (fading a vol spike).
  • Earnings are a binary override: Any setup within ~21 days of earnings carries an event-risk that can completely negate the technical thesis. LRCX earns Oct 21 — the setup becomes a binary, not a clean reversion. Size accordingly or wait.
