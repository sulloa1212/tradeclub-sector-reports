7Options Structures Legend

This section explains how to think about options structures for mean-reversion setups. It covers the general method — the actual results (what structures are suggested for each name) are in the tables above. Always paper-trade before risking real money.

RSI (0–100 momentum gauge) Below 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Extreme: <20 or >80. RSI alone is not enough — require 3+ measures confirming.
Bollinger %B (band position) Measures where price sits relative to its 2-standard-deviation envelope around a 20-day moving average. Below 0 = outside lower band (extreme oversold). Above 1 = outside upper band (extreme overbought).
Z-score (standard deviations) How many standard deviations above/below the 50-day mean the current price sits. A z-score above +2 or below –2 is statistically unusual and often precedes a snap-back.
Distance from SMA (50d/200d) Price % above/below its 50-day or 200-day simple moving average. Extreme readings (>10% below 50d) reinforce oversold signals.
Oversold + High IV → Sell premium When RSI is low AND implied volatility is elevated, option prices are expensive. Ideal for: cash-secured puts, bull put credit spreads. You collect premium that decays as the expected reversion occurs.
Oversold + Low IV → Buy premium When RSI is low AND IV is depressed, options are cheap. Ideal for: bull call debit spreads, long calls. Risk is limited to what you paid.
Overbought + High IV → Sell premium (bearish) When RSI is high AND IV is elevated: call credit spreads, bear put debit spreads. Collect premium while positioning for a pullback.
Overbought + Low IV → Buy premium (bearish) When RSI is high AND IV is depressed: bear put debit spreads or long puts are cheap. Less common — usually overbought = high IV.
Defined risk — always first Every structure listed uses a maximum-loss limit: credit spreads cap risk to the width of the spread minus premium collected; debit spreads cap risk to premium paid. Never use naked short options on reversion plays — the underlying can keep moving.
Implied Volatility (IV) The market's expectation of future price movement, baked into option prices. High IV = expensive options (favor selling). Low IV = cheap options (favor buying). Check IV Rank (0–100; higher = more expensive than usual) in your brokerage.
Oversold + high IV → Cash-secured put · Bull put credit spread Oversold + low IV → Bull call debit spread · Long call Overbought + high IV → Call credit spread · Bear put debit spread Overbought + low IV → Bear put debit spread

8How to Read & Guardrails

Before acting on anything in this report, understand these filters. They are the difference between a clean reversion setup and a "falling knife" that keeps falling.

Reversion vs. Ruin Filter — The most important rule. A low RSI is necessary but NOT sufficient. Ask: is the move driven by a structural break (fraud, bankruptcy, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, buyout repricing) — or by sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise intact business? If structural: skip it. If sentiment excess near a support level: worth watching. CHTR is borderline — flagged above.
Earnings / Binary Events — Any name with earnings inside ~30 days carries a hard binary risk that overrides the technical setup. The stock can gap 15–30% in either direction. Either size down dramatically or wait for the print. Names flagged ⚠ in this report: VZ Oct 26, CHTR ~Oct 30, MSFT Oct 28, MRNA Oct 29.
Leveraged & Futures ETFs — Short-term only — UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, and any 2x/3x fund experience volatility decay and contango/backwardation roll. They deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical reversion (days to a few weeks); for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
Long-Vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — Spike-fade ONLY — These ETPs carry structural roll decay in contango conditions. A low price in VXX/UVXY is drift, NOT a buy signal. Only trade them when they are stretched UP (a volatility spike) — and then only to fade the spike. Today's VIX at 14.84 does not qualify as a spike. Never buy VXX/UVXY on a low RSI.
K-1 Tax Flag — USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time (more complex than a standard 1099). If you prefer to avoid K-1 complexity, use: PDBC or COMB for broad commodities, BNO for Brent crude, SCO for inverse crude.
Proxy vs. Spot — GDX, GDXJ, XOP, COPX, MOO, URA, SIL track companies (miners, drillers, farmers), not the raw commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying. A gold rally doesn't guarantee GDX follows dollar-for-dollar.
Composite Confirmation Required — This report ranks names by how many independent measures confirm the stretch (RSI + %B + SMA distance + z-score + ATR). Names extreme on 3+ measures rank higher than names extreme on RSI alone. If only one indicator is stretched, it's a "watch" not a setup.
