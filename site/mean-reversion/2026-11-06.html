7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what each structure is and when it fits. The specific "why this read" for each name lives in the tables above.

Oversold + High IV → Put Credit Spread
Sell a put below current price, buy a further-down put for protection. Net credit received. Profits if price stays above the short put at expiration. Best when IV is elevated (you collect more premium) and you believe the price won't fall much further. Max loss: width of spread minus credit received.
Oversold + High IV → Cash-Secured Put
Sell a put and hold enough cash to buy the stock if assigned. Profits if price stays above the strike. You may end up owning the stock — only do this if you're comfortable owning it at that price. Max loss: strike price minus premium received (similar to owning stock at a discount).
Oversold + Low IV → Bull Call Spread
Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call. Net debit paid. Profits if price rises above the long call strike. Better when IV is low (buying options is cheaper). Max loss: the debit paid. Max gain: width of spread minus debit.
Overbought + High IV → Call Credit Spread
Sell a call above current price, buy a higher call for protection. Net credit received. Profits if price stays below the short call. Best when IV is elevated (collect more premium) and you believe the price is stretched. Max loss: width of spread minus credit received.
Overbought + Low/Falling IV → Bear Put Spread
Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. Net debit paid. Profits if price falls below the long put strike. Better when IV is falling (buying options is cheaper). Max loss: debit paid. Max gain: width of spread minus debit.
Vol Spike → Call Credit Spread on VXX/UVXY
On a vol spike, sell a call above the spiked price on VXX or UVXY, buy a higher call for protection. Profits as vol reverts lower (the fund price falls). Keep expiration short — 1–3 weeks. NEVER buy these ETPs as "oversold" plays — decay will erode any gain over time.

Indicator Quick Reference

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = stretched down (oversold); above 70 = stretched up (overbought). Extreme readings: below 20 / above 80. RSI alone is not a signal — use with other measures.
Bollinger %B
Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands (20-day, 2 standard deviations). Below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold extreme); above 1 = outside the upper band (overbought extreme).
Z-Score
How many standard deviations a price is from its 50-day average. ±2 is notable; ±3 is extreme. Bigger number = more stretched; direction (positive vs. negative) tells you which way.
Stochastic %K
0–100 gauge comparing the close to recent high-low range. Below 20 = oversold; above 80 = overbought. When both RSI and Stochastic are extreme together, the signal is stronger.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The market's expectation of how much a stock will move, priced into options. High IV = options are expensive (better for selling premium). Low IV = options are cheap (better for buying options).
ATR (Average True Range)
Average daily price range over 14 days — a measure of how much a stock moves per day. When a move is several times the ATR, it is statistically unusual and may indicate a stretched move.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Read this section before entering any trade. These rules exist to protect you from common mistakes.

  • Reversion vs. Ruin filter: An oversold RSI is necessary but not sufficient. This scan rejects names driven by structural breaks — fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, buyout pinning. It keeps names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business. If you identify a fallen-knife risk in any name below, skip it.
  • Defined risk first, always: Every educational structure in this report uses defined-risk options (spreads). Never trade naked short puts or naked short calls on volatile names unless you are an advanced trader who fully understands the risk. The maximum loss on a spread is known before you enter.
  • Earnings dates are the #1 risk: An earnings report inside a spread's expiration can wipe out the entire thesis in one overnight move. For every name with a confirmed earnings date within ~30 days, keep position sizes very small or choose expirations that clear the event. NVDA Nov 17 is the most urgent: it is confirmed and 11 days away.
  • Long-vol ETP special rule: VXX, UVXY, VIXY, VIXM, UVIX are designed to track volatility futures, not stock prices. They experience structural roll decay (contango) that grinds them lower over time. A low RSI on these ETPs is NOT an oversold buy signal — it is decay. The only clean reversion trade is fading the spike after a volatility event. This is confirmed today.
  • Leveraged ETF special rule: SQQQ, TQQQ, UCO, BOIL, TBT, and all 2×/3× products experience volatility decay and should be held only for short-term tactical positions (days, not weeks). Do not use them to replicate a multi-week directional thesis — use the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy instead.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDX­J, XLE, XOP, OIH, GDX, URA, COPX track companies that produce commodities, not the commodity price itself. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying spot price. This is noted in the tables where applicable.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, and certain CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time (more complex tax reporting). Prefer K-1-free alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) for similar commodity exposure where available.
  • All figures are snapshots — verify before acting: Every price, RSI, moving average, and earnings date in this report is a model-generated estimate from screener sources. Models can be wrong, sources can be outdated, and data goes stale within hours. Always confirm every figure in your live brokerage or primary source before placing a trade.
  • Paper-trade first: If any strategy in this report is new to you, paper-trade it (simulate without real money) before risking capital. Options can expire worthless and you can lose the entire premium paid, or more on naked strategies.
