7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the method — what each term means and how direction + IV shape the structure choice. Specific results for each ticker are in Sections 2–5 above.

RSI (14) A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = oversold (potential bounce). Above 70 = overbought (potential fade). Extreme = below 20 or above 80. Never use alone.
Bollinger %B (20,2) Measures where price sits relative to its 20-day band. Below 0 = below the lower band (oversold). Above 1 = above the upper band (overbought).
Z-score vs 50-day How many standard deviations price is above or below its 50-day average. ±2 or beyond = statistically rare stretch.
% from 50-day SMA The simple moving average is the "reference mean" — where price may revert. Bigger negative % = more stretched to the downside.
IV (Implied Volatility) How expensive options are. High IV = options are pricey — favor selling premium (spreads). Low IV = options are cheap — favor buying options or debit spreads.
Defined-risk structures All strategies below limit max loss to the premium paid or the spread width. Never naked short options without understanding full risk.
Oversold + High IV → Premium-selling with upside bias
Cash-secured put (sell a put at or below support — collect premium, risk owning at that price) · Bull put spread / put credit spread (sell a put, buy a lower put — net credit, capped loss) · Bull call debit spread (buy a call, sell a higher call — net debit, capped gain). High IV makes selling structures attractive; still prefer spreads over naked puts for defined risk.
Overbought + High IV → Premium-selling with downside bias
Bear call spread / call credit spread (sell a call above resistance, buy a higher call — net credit, capped loss) · Bear put debit spread (buy a put, sell a lower put — net debit, capped gain). High IV makes calls expensive to sell — a tactical edge when fading a spike.
Vol-spike fade (VXX/UVXY up hard)
Bear call spread on VXX/UVXY (sell a call near the spike high, buy a higher call) · Put debit spread on VXX (buy a put, sell a lower put). Only when RSI >70 and price has broken above the upper Bollinger Band — do NOT fade low RSI on these ETPs.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. ruin — the most important filter
A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. This scan rejects names where the move is driven by: fraud or accounting restatement; going-concern or bankruptcy risk; dividend cut; failed drug trial; buyout repricing (stock pinned to deal price). It keeps names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business. When in doubt, the report flags the risk and downsizes the idea.
Proxy vs. spot — know what you own
GDX, GDXJ, XOP, GDX, URA, COPX, MOO, SIL track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying. GLD/IAU/SLV track spot metals more closely but still carry tracking error.
K-1 tax flag ⚠
USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL and most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1, which complicates tax filing. Where available, prefer K-1-free alternatives: PDBC (commodities), BCI/COMB (broad commodities). Always confirm with a tax professional.
Leveraged / futures-based ETFs — short-term only
TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TBT, any 2×/3× fund. Volatility decay and roll costs mean these deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical trades (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
Long-vol ETPs — special rules (applies to VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX)
Structural roll decay + leverage drag grind these lower over time. A low RSI is drift, not a setup. The only mean-reversion trade is fading the upside spike. Never buy these "because they're low" — they will likely go lower.
Thin options — verify before trading
UNG, UGA, CPER, COPX, URNM, REMX, FXB, some CurrencyShares ETFs — always check open interest and bid/ask spreads before entering. Thin chains mean wide spreads that can erase any edge.
Dates — the #1 hallucination risk
Every earnings date in this report is flagged "date unconfirmed — verify" because no live earnings calendar was confirmed in this run. Never trade an options strategy near an earnings date without confirming the exact date at your brokerage. An unexpected earnings release can destroy a reversion trade overnight.

Financial markets are expected to experience another relatively quiet trading day today as many US investors and traders look to extend their Thanksgiving Day holiday into a long weekend. Thin liquidity = wider spreads on options. Consider waiting for Monday's full session before entering new positions.
