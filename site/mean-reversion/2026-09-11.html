7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic educational structures used in this report. Use it to decode the "Edu. Structure" column — always verify IV and liquidity before placing any trade. These are starting points, not recommendations.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
  • Cash-Secured Put: Sell a put below current price; collect premium; max risk = stock falls to zero. Only if you're comfortable owning the stock.
  • Bull Put Spread (put credit spread): Sell a put, buy a lower put. Defined max loss. Net credit in. Profits if stock stays above short strike at expiration.
  • Bull Call Spread (call debit spread): Buy a call, sell a higher call. Defined max loss = premium paid. Profits if stock rises.
Overbought + High IV → Defined-Risk Bearish
  • Call Credit Spread (bear call spread): Sell a call, buy a higher call. Net credit in. Profits if stock stays below short strike. Max loss is the spread width minus credit.
  • Bear Put Spread (put debit spread): Buy a put, sell a lower put. Defined max loss = premium paid. Profits if stock falls.
What the Indicators Mean
  • RSI (0–100 momentum gauge): Below 30 = selling pressure extreme (oversold). Above 70 = buying pressure extreme (overbought). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme territory.
  • Bollinger %B (0–1 band position): Below 0 = price outside lower band (oversold). Above 1 = outside upper band (overbought).
  • Z-score (standard deviations from mean): How many "typical daily moves" the price is from its 50-day average. ±2 is stretched; ±3 is extreme.
  • IV (implied volatility): The market's expected future move. High IV = fat option premiums (favor selling strategies). Low IV = cheap options (favor buying strategies).
Composite Stretch Score

Names rank higher when they are extreme on 3 or more of: RSI, Bollinger %B, % from 50d SMA, % from 200d SMA, Z-score, and consecutive up/down days vs ATR. RSI alone is not enough.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Ask: "Is this oversold because of sentiment excess — or because something is structurally broken?" Signs of a falling knife / ruin scenario: fraud, going-concern doubts, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, –50%+ in days with no stabilization, or a buyout pinning the price to a deal. None of the names in this report have those flags, but always verify yourself before acting. The report does not cover the full universe — new information can change a setup instantly.

Proxy / Leverage / Futures / K-1 Caveats
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, XLE, SIL, COPX, URA): Track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices.
  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, UNG, TBT, TQQQ, SQQQ): Volatility decay + contango roll drag = they deviate from any long-run mean. Tactical use only — for multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Structural decay makes them fall over time. Trade only when stretched UP (spike); never as "oversold" buys.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI) where available.
