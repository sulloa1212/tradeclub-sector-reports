7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the generic methods — how the scan reasons about structure selection. The specific setups for each name are in the tables above.

RSI (14)
Relative Strength Index on a 0–100 scale. Below 30 = recent selling may be exhausted (oversold). Above 70 = recent buying may be stretched (overbought). Extreme below 20 or above 80 adds confidence.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Where price sits relative to the Bollinger Bands. %B below 0 = price has broken below the lower band (deeply oversold). %B above 1 = price above the upper band (overbought). Confirms RSI extremes.
Z-score / Distance from SMA
How many percent away the price is from its 50- or 200-day moving average. A name 10%+ below its 50-day with a low RSI scores high on the composite. A name 10%+ above with high RSI may be overbought.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The market's expected price swing baked into option prices. High IV = options are expensive — favor selling premium (credit spreads, cash-secured puts). Low IV = options are cheap — favor buying (debit spreads, long calls/puts).
Oversold + High IV → Premium-Selling Bullish
Examples: cash-secured put (you collect premium, buy shares if price drops to your strike); bull put credit spread (you sell a put at a higher strike, buy at a lower one — limited risk, limited reward). Both profit if the stock stays above your short strike.
Oversold + Low IV → Premium-Buying Bullish
Example: bull call debit spread (buy a call at a lower strike, sell one at higher — pay a small debit, profit if stock rallies). Cheap IV means you're not overpaying for options.
Overbought + High IV → Premium-Selling Bearish
Examples: call credit spread (sell a call at a lower strike, buy one at a higher strike — collect premium, profit if stock stays below your short strike); bear put spread (debit spread to profit from a drop, best when IV is lower).
Defined Risk — Always First
Every structure listed above is defined-risk: your maximum possible loss is known before you enter. Never trade naked short options on individual names without understanding your full risk. Paper-trade all structures first.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Understanding what this scan does — and doesn't — catch is as important as the setups themselves.

Reversion vs. Ruin: A low RSI is necessary but never sufficient. Names with structural breaks — fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trials, going-concern debt blowups, buyout repricing — are NOT reversion candidates. The scan is looking for sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business or well-defined mean (like a pre-shock commodity price). When in doubt, skip it.
Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY) — spike-fade only: These ETFs structurally lose value over time due to VIX-futures contango roll costs. A low RSI on VXX is drift, not opportunity. The only mean-reversion trade here is fading a spike (RSI above 70+, price well above recent range). Never buy them as "oversold."
Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TBT, TQQQ, SQQQ, 2x/3x anything): Daily-reset leverage causes volatility decay that compounds against long-hold investors. Use for short-term (days) tactical reversion only. For multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
K-1 Tax Flag: USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, DBC, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time, creating complexity for retail accounts. Where possible, prefer "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC or COMB (broad commodities), BNO (Brent crude, check), GLDM/IAU (gold).
Proxy vs. Spot: GDX, GDXJ, XLE, XOP, OIH, URA, COPX, MOO, SIL track companies — not the raw commodity. They carry equity beta, sector rotation risk, and individual company exposure. A miner can be oversold for idiosyncratic reasons even if the commodity is flat, and vice versa. Always be clear about what you own.
Earnings Dates = Binary Risk: Any name with earnings inside ~30 days can gap violently in either direction. The reversion thesis may be completely irrelevant after an earnings print. Always check the exact date against your brokerage before entering. Dates flagged ⚠ in this report are from live search but may be approximate — re-verify.
Composite Scoring — More Is Better: A name at RSI 28, %B below 0, 15% below its 50-day SMA, and 5 consecutive down days on above-average volume is a stronger candidate than a name with only RSI 29. Quality beats quantity — 3+ confirming measures required for a high-confidence setup.
Today's specific landmine: The Iran/oil shock is a live geopolitical event with binary outcomes. Military strikes could send oil +15–20% overnight, making all "overbought energy" setups catastrophic without defined risk. Use spreads, not naked positions, on every energy-related name right now.
