7Options Structures Legend

This section maps the direction of a setup and the level of implied volatility to an educational defined-risk structure. These are examples of structure types, not trade recommendations. Always verify data and paper-trade first.

RSI (14)
A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = oversold (fell fast), above 70 = overbought (rose fast). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. It measures pace of the move, not whether the move was right.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Measures where price sits within its 20-day volatility band. >1 = above the upper band (stretched high); <0 = below the lower band (stretched low). Pairs well with RSI.
Z-Score
How many standard deviations the price is from its 50-day average. ±2 is unusual; ±3 is rare. Higher absolute Z-score = more stretched.
Implied Volatility (IV)
The market's options-priced expectation of future price swings. Elevated IV = options are expensive → selling premium (credit spreads) has an edge. Low IV = options are cheap → buying premium (debit spreads) has an edge.
Oversold + High IV → Sell Put Premium
Cash-secured put: sell a put below the stock, collect premium, risk owning at a lower price. Put credit spread: sell one put, buy a lower one — limits max loss. Both profit if the stock stays above the short strike.
Oversold + Low IV → Buy Calls
Bull call spread: buy a call at/near the money, sell a higher call. Lower cost than outright call; profits if the stock rises toward target. Defined max loss = premium paid.
Overbought + High IV → Sell Call Premium
Call credit spread: sell a call above current price, buy a higher call — collect premium, profit if stock stays below short strike. Bear put spread: buy a put, sell a lower one — defined cost, profits on downside.
Defined Risk First — Always
Every structure above caps your maximum loss. Never sell naked options on a mean-reversion thesis — if the thesis is wrong and the move extends, losses are unlimited. Use spreads. Paper-trade first.

8How to Read / Guardrails

These rules govern when a low RSI is a setup worth watching versus a trap that costs you money.

Reversion vs Ruin
Reject any name whose move is driven by: fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, or a buyout repricing. A low RSI on a structurally broken business is not a setup — it's a falling knife. Keep only names where the drop looks like sentiment excess over a still-sound business.
The Spin-Off Exception (CTVA)
A mechanical spin-off repricing (like CTVA's 84% drop on Oct 1 after the Vylor separation) can look like a "falling knife" on an RSI chart but is actually just a new price baseline. The key: is the remaining business intact? If yes, the extreme RSI is a calibration artifact. Still: tiny positions, verified chains, and wait for the stock to stop searching for its range.
Leveraged ETF Rule
UCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, TMF — these do not revert to a stable price mean. Volatility decay + roll costs erode them over time. Use for short-term tactical ideas only. Never hold a leveraged ETF for a multi-week reversion thesis. Prefer the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy for longer holds.
Long-Vol ETP Rule (VXX, UVXY)
Trade these only when they are stretched UP (RSI/price spike on a vol event) — they revert down reliably as spikes fade. Never treat a low RSI on VXX as a buy: contango and leverage drag grind these lower indefinitely. Today: VIX partially faded — monitor for re-spike at 22+ before fading VXX.
Proxy vs Spot
GDX/GDXJ (gold miners), XOP (E&P companies), URA (uranium companies), COPX (copper miners), EWY (Korean tech equities) — these track companies, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta and management risk that diverges from the underlying. Know what you own.
K-1 Tax Warning
USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, DBC, CPER, and most CurrencyShares ETPs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — more complex filing. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC or BCI for broad commodities; BNO for Brent oil. Check your own tax situation with a professional.
Earnings Landmines
Any earnings report within ~30 days can override a technical setup completely. Highlighted in amber on the tables. The near-term calendar: INTC Oct 29, AMD Nov 3, AMAT ~Nov 12, CTVA Nov 11. Manage position size accordingly — or wait until after earnings to enter.
