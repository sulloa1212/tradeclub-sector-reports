7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains how to match a technical setup to an educational structure type. The actual results (which tickers, which direction) are in the tables above; this is the method behind the recommendation.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling

Put credit spread: Sell a put at a lower strike, buy an even lower put for protection. You collect a credit; you profit if the stock stays above your short strike. Defined risk = the spread width minus credit received.

Cash-secured put: Sell a put and hold enough cash to buy the shares if assigned. Works well when you're willing to own the stock at the strike price.

Oversold + Low IV → Debit Buying

Bull call spread: Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. You pay a debit; max profit if the stock reaches or exceeds your short strike. Defined risk = the debit paid.

Long call: Simple bullish bet. Works best when IV is low (options are cheap). Max loss = premium paid.

Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling

Call credit spread: Sell a call at a higher strike, buy an even higher call for protection. You collect a credit; profit if the stock stays below your short strike. Defined risk = spread width minus credit received.

Bear put spread: Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. You pay a debit; profit if the stock falls toward your short strike. Works when IV is low.

Indicators Quick Guide

RSI (0–100): Measures recent momentum. Below 30 = oversold pressure; above 70 = overbought pressure. Extremes below 20 or above 80 are more significant.

Bollinger %B: Shows where price sits within its normal range. Above 1.0 = above the upper band (stretched high); below 0 = below the lower band (stretched low).

Z-score vs 50-day mean: How many standard deviations away from the 50-day average. A z-score beyond ±2 is statistically unusual.

IV (Implied Volatility): The market's estimate of how much a stock will move. High IV = expensive options (credit strategies work better). Low IV = cheap options (buying strategies work better).

The universal rule: Always use defined-risk structures first — spreads and cash-secured puts cap your maximum loss at the outset. Never short naked calls or puts without knowing exactly what your maximum loss is. When in doubt, paper-trade (simulate without real money) until the mechanics are second nature.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

These guardrails explain the filters applied to every name above — understanding them makes you a better trader, not just a better reader of this report.

  • Reversion vs. ruin filter: An oversold RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Before acting on any oversold name, ask: Is the company financially intact? Is the drop caused by sentiment/positioning, or by a real structural change (fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, dividend cut, accounting restatement)? If the latter, skip it — a low RSI on a broken business is not a reversion setup; it's a trap. NKE is flagged above as a borderline case for this reason.
  • Falling-knife rule: If a name is down 50%+ in days with no stabilization, it is probably not done falling. Wait for at least one or two sessions of price stabilization or a failed-breakdown candle before considering any long structure.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, XLE, XOP, OIH, URA, COPX, MOO track companies that produce a commodity — not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta (sensitivity to the overall stock market) and can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity price. Know what you're trading.
  • K-1 tax warning: USO, UNG, UCO, BOIL, DBC, UGA, CPER, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form, which complicates tax filing. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB for commodity exposure; XLE/XOP for energy equity.
  • Leveraged and futures-based ETF warning: SOXL (3×), TQQQ (3×), UCO (2×), TBT (−2×), UVXY (1.5×), BOIL (2×), and similar funds suffer from volatility decay — daily rebalancing erodes value over time in choppy markets. Use them only for short-term tactical trades (days, not weeks) and prefer the unlevered equivalent for longer-hold reversion ideas.
  • Long-vol ETP special rule: VXX, UVXY, VIXY are never "oversold buys." Their structural downward drift is not a setup — only fade them when they spike UP on a vol event.
  • Dates are the #1 hallucination risk: Every earnings date in this report is flagged as "date unconfirmed — verify" unless sourced from a live web search in this run. Cross-reference every date with your brokerage's earnings calendar before acting. An options trade placed before an unexpected earnings announcement can result in a much larger move than anticipated.
  • Verify everything before acting: All RSI, price, and technical figures in this report are screener snapshots from Sep 2–4, 2026. Pre-market on Sep 8, prices may have already moved materially. Confirm every number at your live brokerage before placing any trade.
  • Paper-trade first: If you are new to options, simulate every structure in a paper-trading account until you can answer: "What is my maximum loss? At what price does this trade work? At what price does it fail?" before risking real money.
