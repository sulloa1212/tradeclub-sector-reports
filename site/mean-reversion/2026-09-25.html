7Options Structures Legend

How we map direction + implied volatility level to a defined-risk structure. This is the general method; always apply it to the specific risk/reward of your own position.

▲ Oversold + High IV (most common setup today)
Bull Put Spread: sell a put at or near support, buy a lower put to cap max loss. Collects premium while IV is elevated; profits if the stock stays above the short put at expiration.

Cash-Secured Put: sell a single put, hold cash to cover assignment. Only for accounts large enough to handle assignment — more risk than a spread.
▲ Oversold + Low/Normal IV
Bull Call Spread: buy a call at or near the money, sell a higher call to offset cost. Lower net debit than an outright call; profits on a measured bounce.

Best when IV is compressed and you expect a bounce — buying premium is cheaper.
▼ Overbought + High IV (second most common today)
Call Credit Spread: sell a call above resistance, buy a higher call to cap max loss. Collects premium; profits if the stock stays below the short call strike.

Bear Put Spread: buy a put near the money, sell a lower put to reduce cost. Profits on a measured pullback; lower max loss than an outright put.
▼ Overbought + Low IV
Bear Put Spread: buying premium is relatively affordable; pay for a put spread rather than selling calls (which collect little when IV is low).

This scenario is less common in today's environment — IV is elevated across the board due to the macro shock.

How to read the technical measures

RSI — 14d (0–100 momentum gauge)
Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buying may be exhausted). Extremes below 20 or above 80 are stronger signals. RSI alone is not a trade — it is one input.
Bollinger %B (0-to-1 band position)
Below 0 = price outside the lower band (statistically stretched down); above 1 = price outside the upper band (stretched up). Computed on a 20-day window, ±2 standard deviations.
Z-Score vs 50d Mean (standard deviation count)
How many standard deviations the price sits from its 50-day average. A Z-score below −2 or above +2 is extreme (roughly the bottom/top 5% of historical readings).
% from 50d / 200d SMA
Simple distance metric. A name −15% from its 50d SMA has given back a lot in a short time — useful context but not a signal by itself.
Composite stretch rating
We rank candidates higher when they are extreme on 3+ measures simultaneously (RSI + %B + Z-score + SMA distance + consecutive days). One-measure extremes are watch-list items, not setups.
IV Note (implied volatility level)
IV elevated = premium-selling structures (put spreads, call spreads) are more efficient. IV low = premium-buying structures (call spreads, put spreads from the buy side) are cheaper. Current environment: IV is broadly elevated from the macro shock — lean toward selling premium with defined risk.

8How to Read This / Guardrails

Before you act on anything in this report, understand these filters — they are the difference between a reversion setup and a trap.

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter
  • Ruin signal: fraud, going-concern doubt, bankruptcy filing, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, -50%+ drop in days with no stabilization, or a buyout repricing that pins the stock to a deal price. These are falling knives — do not trade them as mean-reversion candidates.
  • Reversion signal: the drop looks like sentiment/positioning excess (macro shock, rotation, event fear) over an otherwise fundamentally sound business, ideally near a prior support level. All names in Sections 2 and 3 passed this filter based on available information — but always re-verify there is no new structural news before entering.
  • Today's main ruin risk: biotech names (MRNA) carry binary FDA/trial risk; always confirm no imminent binary event. Energy names (XLE, XOP) could re-spike if Hormuz diplomacy collapses.

⚠ Leveraged & Futures ETFs

USO, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT — volatility decay + futures roll (contango or backwardation) means they deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical reversion only; for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy (e.g. XLE over USO).

⚠ K-1 Tax Flag

USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and many CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — more complex than a 1099. When the same exposure is available via a No-K-1 alternative (PDBC, BCI, COMB for commodities; XLE/XOP for energy equity), prefer those for most accounts. Always confirm with your broker or tax advisor.

⚠ Proxy vs. Spot

GDX/GDXJ track gold-mining companies, not spot gold — they carry equity beta (labor costs, FX, financing). XOP/XLE track energy companies, not WTI or Brent. URA/COPX track mining equities, not the underlying metal. Divergence from spot is common and can be extreme in volatile markets.

⚠ Dates Are a Hallucination Risk

Every earnings date listed as "unconfirmed — verify" is exactly that. AI models frequently hallucinate specific dates. Always confirm earnings and event dates against your own brokerage, the SEC Edgar filing calendar, or the company IR page before entering any position that straddles a potential binary event.

⚠ Vol ETPs — Upside Only

VXX and UVXY should only be traded when stretched UP (RSI spike on a vol event) as a short-term fade. Never treat a low RSI on VXX/UVXY as an "oversold buy" — structural contango decay grinds them lower permanently over time. A low RSI on VXX is drift, not a setup. Today, VIX is ~15.67 — NO fade setup is active.
