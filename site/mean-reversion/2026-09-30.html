7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the method — the general educational approach — not specific trade recommendations. Every structure listed here is educational only; verify with your broker and paper-trade first.

What RSI Means
RSI (14-day Relative Strength Index) is a 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = historically oversold (selling pressure may be exhausting). Above 70 = historically overbought (buying pressure may be thinning). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. RSI alone is not a buy/sell signal — it needs confirmation from other measures.
What Bollinger %B Means
%B places price relative to its Bollinger Bands (typically 20-day, ±2 standard deviations). %B below 0 = price is below the lower band (extreme oversold); %B above 1 = price is above the upper band (extreme overbought). Best used together with RSI for confirmation.
What Z-Score Means
Z-score measures how many standard deviations a price is from its recent mean (typically 50-day). A Z-score of −2 means the price is 2 standard deviations below average — statistically uncommon, suggesting a stretched sell-off. Above +2 = stretched to the upside.
Oversold + High IV → Selling Premium
When a name is oversold AND implied volatility (IV) is elevated (options are expensive), selling premium can be more attractive than buying it. Educational structures: cash-secured puts (sell a put below support), put credit spread (sell lower put, buy even-lower put for protection). Both are defined-risk if a spread is used — you know your max loss upfront. Always paper-trade first.
Oversold + Low IV → Buying Options
When a name is oversold AND IV is low (options are cheap), buying calls or call debit spreads costs less and can capture a rebound. Educational structure: bull call spread (buy lower call, sell higher call) — defined maximum risk upfront. Always paper-trade first.
Overbought + High IV → Selling Premium
When a name is overbought AND IV is elevated, selling premium on the upside can be attractive. Educational structure: bear call spread (sell lower call, buy higher call) — limits max loss. Always paper-trade first.
Defined-Risk First Rule
Every structure in this report uses spreads (buying a further OTM option to cap the max loss) rather than naked short options. Beginners should only use defined-risk structures. "Defined-risk" means you know the worst-case loss before you enter. Never sell naked puts or naked calls unless you fully understand the risk — naked short options can lose multiples of what you paid.
IV Crush Warning
Implied volatility (IV) often spikes ahead of events (earnings, PCE, Fed decisions) and then collapses after the event regardless of direction. If you BUY options ahead of a known event, you can be right on direction and still lose money because the IV premium you paid evaporates. For event plays, iron condors (sell both a call spread and a put spread) can capture this "IV crush" — but the risk is a large directional move blowing through your short strikes.

8How to Read This Report & Guardrails

Before doing anything with a setup from this report, run it through these filters. Skipping one can turn a reversion thesis into a "catching a falling knife" disaster.

The Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter (most important check)
  • Ask why it moved. Is the sell-off due to sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business? (Good reversion candidate.) Or is it driven by fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy risk, or a deal repricing? (Reject — not a reversion setup, it's a ruin scenario.)
  • Look for stabilization. A name that is down 50%+ in days with no sign of stabilization is likely a falling knife. Wait for price to "base" for at least a few sessions before entering.
  • Check the balance sheet quickly. High-debt names (CCL, WBD) require smaller positions and defined-risk only — because a balance sheet stress can turn an oversold bounce into a default spiral.
  • Buyouts/mergers. A stock pinned to a deal price is NOT reverting to its technical mean — it's pinned to the acquisition price. Always screen for M&A activity.
Proxy, K-1, and Leverage Caveats
  • Equity proxies (GDX, XOP, URA, etc.) track companies that produce or mine a commodity — they carry full equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying commodity spot price. They are NOT direct commodity exposure.
  • K-1 tax forms are issued by: USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, DBC, CPER, and many CurrencyShares ETFs. These can complicate tax filing. Prefer "no K-1" alternatives (PDBC, COMB, BCI, XLE/XOP for energy equity) where the exposure allows.
  • Leveraged / futures-decay ETFs (TBT, UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, SQQQ, etc.) deviate from any long-run mean due to daily reset compounding and futures roll. Use them only for short-term tactical plays (days, not weeks) and size them accordingly.
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) decay structurally via contango roll — their "oversold" is drift, not opportunity. Fade spikes only.

  • Earnings dates marked ⚠ were not confirmed by a live search result this run — they are estimates and MUST be independently verified. An unknown earnings date inside the next 30 days can completely override a technical setup.
  • AI can misinterpret data, confuse tickers, or generate confident-sounding numbers that are wrong. Treat this report as a starting-point checklist, not a confirmed trade sheet.
