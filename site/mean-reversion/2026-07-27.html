7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic framework — how direction + implied volatility (IV) maps to educational defined-risk structures. Results for each name are in the tables above; this is the method behind them.

Oversold + High IV → Sell Premium

When a stock is stretched down AND options are expensive, you can sell the fear. Educational structures: cash-secured put (agree to buy at a lower price, collect premium), put credit spread (sell one put, buy a cheaper one — defines your max loss). You profit if the stock stays above your short strike.

Oversold + Low IV → Buy Premium

When a stock is stretched down AND options are cheap, buying direction can work. Educational structures: bull call spread (buy one call, sell a higher one — defines cost and max gain). You profit if the stock rebounds above your long strike by expiration.

Overbought + High IV → Sell Premium

When a stock is stretched up AND options are expensive, selling upside premium works. Educational structures: call credit spread (sell one call, buy a higher one), bear put spread (buy one put, sell a cheaper one). You profit if the stock stays below your short strike or falls.

Overbought + Low IV → Buy Premium

When a stock is stretched up AND options are cheap, buying puts can work. Educational structure: bear put spread. You profit if the stock declines. Low IV = cheaper entry for directional buyers.

RSI — What It Means

RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a 0–100 momentum gauge measuring how fast and far prices have moved. Below 30 = oversold (selling extreme); above 70 = overbought (buying extreme); below 20 or above 80 = severe extreme. RSI alone is NOT sufficient — confirm with other measures.

Bollinger %B — What It Means

Bollinger %B measures where price sits relative to its Bollinger Bands (a volatility envelope around a 20-day moving average). %B > 1.0 = above the upper band (overbought/extended); %B < 0.0 = below the lower band (oversold/extended). Combined with RSI = stronger signal.

Z-Score — What It Means

Z-score measures how many standard deviations (a unit of spread) a price is from its recent average. Z > +2 or < −2 = statistically unusual (stretched). Z > +3 = rare extreme. Used to confirm RSI reads on a more statistical basis.

Composite Ranking

Names stretched on 3+ measures (RSI + Bollinger + distance from MA + Z-score) rank higher than names showing only one signal. More confirmations = higher conviction on the statistical stretch. Higher conviction still doesn't guarantee a reversal — always define your risk.
