8How to Read This · Guardrails

The single most important rule: oversold ≠ buy. Overbought ≠ short. Mean reversion only works when the selloff/rally is sentiment excess over a sound underlying — not when the fundamentals have permanently changed. Always ask: "Is this stretched because people are over-reacting, or because the business is broken?"

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Key Filter

Reject any name where:

  • Structural break — fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, dividend elimination, going-concern doubt. A low RSI from a structural break is NOT a reversion setup.
  • Catastrophic drop — −50%+ in days with no stabilization. Likely more to come. Stay away.
  • Buyout/merger repricing — the stock is now pinned to a deal price, not its mean. Skip it.
  • Regime change — a permanent repricing (e.g., oil staying above $100 permanently for structural supply reasons). The "mean" may have moved.

Keep names where the move looks like crowd positioning over a sound business, ideally near a prior support/resistance level.

Special Rules — Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY) — upside only. Only fade them when they spike UP. Never treat a low VXX as "oversold" — contango decay grinds them down structurally.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX) — opposite drift. They recover after vol crashes; don't fade their up-trends as overbought.
  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (TBT, UCO, SCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.). Volatility decay means they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical trades (days to ~2 weeks max). For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered ETF or the equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time. If you want to avoid it, use the "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC, BCI, SCO (for inverse oil), XOP (equity proxy for oil).
  • Proxy flag: GDX/GDXJ, URA, XOP, COPX, MOO track companies, not the commodity — they carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying in a risk-off sell. Always know what you own.
