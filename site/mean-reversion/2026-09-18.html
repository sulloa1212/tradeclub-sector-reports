RSI(14) — Relative Strength Index
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Under 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone). Over 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Extreme: under 20 or over 80. RSI alone is NOT sufficient — combine with other measures.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Measures price relative to upper and lower Bollinger Bands. Under 0 = below the lower band (oversold extreme). Over 1 = above the upper band (overbought extreme).
% Distance from Moving Average
How far the current price is above or below the 50-day or 200-day simple moving average (SMA). Larger negative % = more oversold. Larger positive % = more overbought.
Z-score (price vs. 50-day mean)
Number of standard deviations the price is from its 50-day average. Beyond ±2 is statistically stretched. Beyond ±3 is extreme. A standard deviation is a statistical measure of how spread out prices are from their average.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The market's estimate of how much an asset will move, extracted from option prices. High IV = expensive options (favors selling premium). Low IV = cheap options (favors buying).
IV Rank
Current IV compared to its own 52-week range. IV rank of 80% means IV is higher than 80% of days in the past year — very elevated. Helps compare across different assets.
Defined-Risk Structures
Options strategies where your maximum loss is known upfront (e.g., a spread). Always preferred over naked (undefined-risk) structures, especially for newer traders. Paper-trade before using real money.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter: A low RSI is not enough. A name is rejected when the move is driven by a structural break — fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, or accounting restatement. A name that falls 50%+ in days with no stabilization is likely a falling knife, not a reversion. Names in this scan passed that filter: the moves appear to be sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business or broad market index. But this scan does NOT have access to real-time news feeds — always check for breaking company news before trading any name here.

Counter-evidence to keep in mind for every setup on this page:

  • Oil shorts: Hormuz is still contested. Any new attack or Trump military action spikes oil immediately. Size small.
  • Homebuilder / rate-sensitive longs: Warsh signaled more hikes ahead. If November brings another hike, TLT and XHB have further to fall.
  • Gold fade: Gold has a structural dual bid (geopolitical + inflation). A "reversion" here may be shallow and short-lived.
  • Small-cap semis (CRDO): High beta + thin options chains. Verify open interest before trading.
  • All bond ETFs: A sustained rate-rise cycle makes "oversold" bond ETFs structurally cheap, not technically stretched — the thesis requires rate stability or peaking.
⚠ Leveraged ETF Caveat
Leveraged ETFs (TBT 2×, UCO 2×, BOIL 2×, TQQQ 3×, etc.) suffer volatility decay — they lose value over time even if the underlying stays flat. Use for short-term tactical trades only. For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy.
⚠ Futures-Based ETF Caveat (K-1 flag)
USO, UNG, BNO, DBC, UCO, BOIL, and similar funds hold futures contracts, not the physical commodity. Contango (when futures prices are higher than spot) creates a "roll cost" that can erode returns over time. These also issue a Schedule K-1 tax form — check with your tax adviser. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where available.
⚠ Proxy ETF Caveat
ETFs marked "Equity Proxy" (GDX, XOP, OIH, URA, XLE, MOO, etc.) track companies in a sector, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta: management risk, balance-sheet risk, and general market beta on top of commodity exposure. They can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity price.
⚠ Long-Vol ETP Rule
VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX are UPSIDE-ONLY reversion trades. Only trade them when they are stretched UP on a volatility event — the fade as the spike decays. Never treat a low RSI on VXX as an oversold buy: structural roll decay grinds these products lower over time.

The Composite Rule
