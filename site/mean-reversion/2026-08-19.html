7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic method — how to match a reversion direction and IV environment to a defined-risk structure. Specific setups for each name are in the tables above.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling (bullish)

  • Cash-secured put: Sell a put below current price. You keep the premium if the stock stays above strike. Max loss: large (stock falls to zero) — only on stocks you'd own. Defined max gain, large max loss.
  • Bull put credit spread: Sell a put + buy a lower put. The bought put caps your loss. Premium comes in on day 1. Best for oversold + high IV. Fully defined risk.
  • Bull call debit spread: Buy a lower call + sell a higher call. Pays out if stock rises. Net debit (you pay up front). Good if IV is moderate. Fully defined risk.

Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling (bearish)

  • Call credit spread: Sell a call + buy a higher call. Premium comes in on day 1. Profits if stock stays below the short strike. Fully defined risk.
  • Bear put debit spread: Buy a put + sell a lower put. Net debit. Profits if stock drops. Good for lower IV environments. Fully defined risk.
  • Avoid: naked short calls. Unlimited loss potential — not appropriate for this educational framework.
Key vocabulary:
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index): a 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought. Extreme: below 20 or above 80.
  • Bollinger %B: measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands. Below 0 = outside lower band (oversold); above 1 = outside upper band (overbought).
  • Z-score: how many standard deviations price sits from its 50-day average. ±2 or beyond is statistically unusual.
  • IV (Implied Volatility): the market's forward-looking expectation of how much a stock will move, extracted from option prices. High IV = more expensive options (favor selling premium); low IV = cheaper options (favor buying).
  • Defined risk: a structure where the maximum possible loss is known before you enter. Always use defined-risk first.
  • ATR (Average True Range): the typical daily price swing for a given asset. A move of 3×–5× ATR in a few days is statistically "stretched."

8Guardrails — How to Read & Reversion vs. Ruin

Before using any setup from this report, run every name through this checklist. The most dangerous mistake in mean-reversion trading is confusing a falling knife with an oversold bounce.

The Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter — reject a name if ANY of these apply:
  • ❌ Structural break: fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy filing, debt blowup, accounting restatement. A low RSI here is a warning, not a buy signal.
  • ❌ Catastrophic drop: −50%+ in days with no stabilization — more likely to be a permanent re-rating, not a bounce.
  • ❌ Buyout/merger pin: stock is trading at the deal price; mean reversion is irrelevant — it's pinned to the deal.
  • ❌ Binary event imminent: an unconfirmed earnings date or drug readout within a week can override the entire technical setup. Always check before entering.
  • ✅ Keep: sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, ideally near a prior support/resistance level, with options still trading normally.
Proxy flag (proxy)
ETFs like GDX, OIH, XOP, URA track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta — they can diverge from the underlying commodity on earnings, management news, or equity-market selloffs.
K-1 tax flag (K-1)
USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — more complex than a standard 1099. Prefer No-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, XLE, XOP) where the exposure allows.
Leveraged ETF warning
2x and 3x ETFs (TBT, SOXL, UCO, BOIL) decay due to daily rebalancing math. They are for short-term tactical trades only — multi-week holds can produce large losses even if the underlying moves your way.
Volatility ETPs (VXX/UVXY)
Structural contango roll decay means VXX/UVXY grind lower over time. A low VXX is not a "buy" — it's drift. Only trade them SHORT-TERM when they spike up on a vol event. Fading spikes is the only clean reversion trade here.
Dates are the #1 hallucination risk
Every earnings date in this report is flagged where unconfirmed. Never trade into an earnings date without verifying it in your own brokerage or on the company's IR page. AI models can get dates wrong.
