Bull Put Spread: Sell a put at a higher strike, buy a put at a lower strike. Profit if the stock stays above the sold put. Best when the asset is oversold + IV is elevated (you collect more premium). Max loss = width of the spread minus the credit received.
Cash-Secured Put: Sell a put with enough cash in the account to buy 100 shares if assigned. Collect premium; if assigned, you own the stock at the strike (your effective cost = strike minus premium). Best for oversold + elevated IV when you'd be willing to own the stock.
Bull Call Spread: Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Profit if the stock rises above the long call. Best when oversold + IV is low (debit spread is cheaper). Max loss = premium paid.
Call Credit Spread: Sell a call at a lower strike, buy a call at a higher strike. Profit if the stock stays below the sold call. Best when overbought + IV is elevated. Max loss = width minus the credit received.
Bear Put Spread: Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. Profit if the stock falls below the long put. Best when overbought + IV is low (debit spread is cheaper). Max loss = premium paid.
Post-Earnings Only: Do not enter directional options before a binary earnings event. Wait for the print, then use the resulting IV crush or new range to structure a trade.

How the indicators work (plain-English)

RSI (0–100): A 14-day momentum gauge. Above 70 = buying pressure extreme; below 30 = selling pressure extreme. Not a signal alone — combine with other measures.
Bollinger %B: Where price sits relative to its 20-day upper and lower bands. Below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold); above 1 = outside the upper band (overbought).
% from 50/200-day SMA: How far price is from its recent (50d) and long-run (200d) average. The further from the average, the more "stretched" and the more likely a reversion.
Z-Score: Number of standard deviations price is above/below its 50-day mean. A z-score of ±2 is extreme; ±3 is rare.
IV (Implied Volatility): How expensive options are. High IV = options are pricey; sell premium (spreads, CSPs). Low IV = options are cheap; buy premium (debit spreads).
Composite Rank: Names are ranked by how many measures confirm the extreme — RSI alone gets lower weight than RSI + %B + distance from mean + consecutive down days all firing together.
