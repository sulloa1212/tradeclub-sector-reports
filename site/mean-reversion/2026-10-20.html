7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — how direction and implied volatility map to defined-risk structures. The specific "why this read" for each name is in the tables above.

RSI (14-day)

A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Extreme readings: below 20 or above 80. RSI alone is not sufficient — confirm with at least 2 other measures.

Bollinger %B (20,2)

Shows where price sits relative to its Bollinger Bands (a volatility envelope around a 20-day average). Below 0 = price below the lower band (oversold extreme). Above 1 = price above the upper band (overbought extreme).

Z-score / % from 50d

How far (in standard deviations or percent) price is from its 50-day average. Beyond ±2 standard deviations is statistically unusual. A stock 15%+ below its 50-day average is significantly stretched.

IV (Implied Volatility)

The market's options-priced expectation for future price swings. High IV = expensive options; favors selling premium (credit spreads). Low IV = cheap options; favors buying (debit spreads / long calls or puts). A reversion setup with high IV is ideal for selling premium.

Oversold + High IV → Selling Puts

Cash-secured puts, put credit spreads, or bull call spreads. You sell premium while expecting a bounce. Defined-risk: always cap your max loss with a spread — never sell naked options. Paper-trade first.

Overbought + High IV → Selling Calls

Call credit spreads or bear put spreads. You sell premium while expecting a pullback. Same rule: always use a spread to define your max loss. Never sell a naked call — unlimited loss potential.

Oversold + Low IV → Buying Calls

Long calls or bull call debit spreads (you pay a debit). Cheap options mean the cost to bet on a bounce is low. Risk = premium paid only.

Overbought + Low IV → Buying Puts

Long puts or bear put debit spreads. Cheap options to bet on a pullback. Risk = premium paid only.

8How to Read / Guardrails

The guardrails below explain what makes a setup reliable versus dangerous — read these before acting on anything above.

Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter (the most important check)

A low RSI alone does not make a stock a buy. Reject any setup where the move is driven by: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, debt blowup, accounting restatement, or a buyout repricing (stock pinned to deal price). Keep only names where the drop looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, near a defined prior support level.

Long-Vol ETFs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX) — Spikes Only

These ETFs are ONLY traded when stretched UP (spike on a volatility event) — they revert down reliably as the spike fades. NEVER treat them as "oversold buys": structural contango roll decay grinds them lower over time. A low RSI on VXX = drift, not a setup.

Leveraged / Futures ETFs (SOXL, UCO, BOIL, UNG, TBT, 3× anything)

Volatility decay + futures roll (contango/backwardation) mean these drift away from any stable long-run mean. Use ONLY for short-term tactical reversion (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy.

K-1 Tax Flag

USO, UNG, UCO, DBC, BOIL, CPER, and most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form — not a 1099. This creates extra tax complexity. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives where available (e.g., PDBC instead of DBC, BNO instead of USO for Brent).

Proxy vs Spot

GDX/GDXJ (gold miners), XLE/XOP (energy companies), URA/URNM (uranium producers), COPX (copper miners) track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry additional equity beta and can diverge sharply from spot prices. Size and hedge accordingly.

Dates Are the #1 Hallucination Risk

Every earnings and event date in this report came from a live web search in this run. Even so, AI can misread sources. ALWAYS confirm earnings dates against your brokerage or the company's investor-relations page before trading — a surprise earnings gap destroys a mean-reversion thesis instantly.

Composite Confirmation Rule

A name extreme on 3+ measures (RSI, Bollinger %B, % from 50-day, consecutive losing sessions, Z-score) is a higher-quality setup than a name extreme on RSI only. The tables above aim for 3+ confirmations; treat single-measure entries with extra skepticism.
