7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains how we map a direction and an IV reading to an educational structure — the actual setup logic belongs in Sections 2–5. Always confirm data, understand the risk fully, and paper-trade first.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
When a stock is stretched down AND options are expensive (high implied volatility = IV), the best educational structures are ones that collect premium:
• Cash-secured put — sell a put at a strike below current price; collect premium; your obligation is to buy shares at that strike.
• Put credit spread — sell a lower put, buy an even lower put for protection; keeps risk defined. Best for most beginners.
Oversold + Low IV → Option Buying
When IV is low, options are "cheaper" — buying is relatively efficient:
• Long call — simple upside bet, premium paid is max loss.
• Bull call spread — buy a call, sell a higher call to reduce cost; capped gain, defined risk. Generally preferred over a naked long call.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling (bearish)
Stretched up + expensive options = sell elevated premium against the downside:
• Call credit spread — sell a call above price, buy a higher call; collect net premium; risk is the spread width minus premium received.
• Bear put spread — buy a put, sell a lower put; pay a debit; profits if price falls. Lower risk than naked puts.
Technical Term Glossary
• RSI — 0–100 momentum gauge; <30 = oversold, >70 = overbought, <20/>80 = extreme.
• Bollinger %B — position within the price channel; <0 = below lower band (stretched down), >1 = above upper band (stretched up).
• Z-score — how many standard deviations from the mean; >2 or <−2 is statistically unusual.
• ATR — Average True Range; average daily price swing; used to judge move size vs. normal volatility.
• IV / Implied Volatility — option market's forecast for future price movement; higher = pricier options.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter
A low RSI is necessary but never sufficient. This report rejects names that failed the following checks:
  • Structural break: fraud, going-concern, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, or significant permanent contract loss. (DOX rejected — T-Mobile contract dispute signals potential structural revenue impairment.)
  • Enormous gap with no stabilization: drops of 40–50%+ in a single session rarely mean-revert quickly. The gap IS the new mean repricing.
  • Buyout/merger pin: a stock locked to a deal price has no "mean" to revert toward.
  • Meme/political stocks: no stable fundamental anchor. (DJT rejected.)
Special Handling — Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY) — upside fades only. Structural contango decay grinds them lower always. Never treat as an "oversold buy." Only fade when RSI >70 on a vol spike.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX) — opposite drift. Their reversion setup is recovery after a crash, not fading up-moves.
  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.) — short-term tactical only. Volatility decay and roll costs mean no stable long-run mean. Max hold: days to a week. Prefer unlevered alternatives for multi-week ideas.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1 (a tax form with extra complexity). Where possible, prefer No-K-1 alternatives like PDBC, BCI, COMB.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX/GDXJ, XOP, GLD, URA, COPX track companies or futures, not the commodity directly — they carry additional equity or roll risk. This report labels all proxies.
