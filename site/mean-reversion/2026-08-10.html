7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the generic method — what each structure is and when it fits. The actual setups (which ticker, which strike) are in the tables above. Always paper-trade first. Always confirm live prices.

Oversold + High IV
Favor premium-selling upside structures:
• Cash-secured put: sell a put below the stock, collect premium. Profit if stock stays above the strike.
• Bull put spread (put credit spread): sell a higher put, buy a lower put. Defined risk. Profit if stock closes above the short strike at expiry.
Oversold + Low IV
Favor premium-buying upside structures:
• Bull call spread (call debit spread): buy a lower call, sell a higher call. Pay a debit. Profit if stock rises to the short call by expiry. Lower cost than buying naked calls.
Overbought + High IV
Favor premium-selling downside structures:
• Call credit spread (bear call spread): sell a lower call, buy a higher call. Collect a credit. Profit if stock stays below the short call strike at expiry.
• Bear put spread: buy a higher put, sell a lower put. Pay a debit. Profit if stock falls below the long put.
Overbought + Low IV
Favor premium-buying downside structures:
• Bear put spread: buy a put near the money, sell a put further out of the money. Pay a debit. Lower cost; limited profit. Profit if stock falls below the long put.
What each indicator means (first-time glossary):
RSI (14-day)A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = oversold (stretched low); above 70 = overbought (stretched high). Extreme readings: below 20 or above 80.
Bollinger %B (20,2)Where price sits inside/outside its Bollinger Bands (price envelope 2 standard deviations wide). %B below 0 = price below the lower band (oversold extreme); %B above 1 = price above the upper band (overbought extreme).
% from 50d SMAHow far price is above or below its 50-day simple moving average — the medium-term "mean." A −10% reading means price is 10% below the average, suggesting a potential upside reversion.
% from 200d SMASame concept but the long-term (200-day) mean. A large negative here signals a prolonged downtrend; a large positive signals a prolonged rally.
Z-scoreHow many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. Z-score ≥ 2 = unusually far. Bigger number = more stretched.
IV (Implied Volatility)The option market's guess at how much the stock could move. High IV = options are expensive (favors selling premium). Low IV = options are cheap (favors buying options). "IV elevated" means premium-selling structures cost less to defend and collect more credit.
Composite scoreA name flagged on 3+ measures (RSI, %B, 50d distance, Z-score) is a stronger setup than one flagged on RSI alone. Quality beats quantity.

8How to Read This Report & Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter

A low RSI does NOT guarantee a bounce. Ask these questions before treating any oversold name as a setup:

  • Structural break? Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy filing, accounting restatement → reject. The stock may keep falling.
  • Enormous gap down (−50%+ in days) with no stabilization? Likely a knife — reject.
  • Buyout/merger repricing? Stock is now pinned to a deal price, not its mean — reject as a reversion setup.
  • Sentiment excess over a sound business? This is the keeper. Look for a name where the move looks like positioning or macro spillover, the business hasn't broken, and there is a clear "mean" (a 50d SMA, a prior support level) to revert toward.

SMCI (Super Micro Computer) is flagged above with a caution on prior accounting history — treat as a potential trap until earnings resolve cleanly.

Special caveats — Leveraged, Futures-Based, and Proxy ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Only trade them when stretched UP (spike). A low RSI on VXX is structural decay — NOT a buy. Never hold long-vol ETPs for multi-week periods in calm markets.
  • Inverse-vol ETPs (SVXY, SVIX): They grind up in calm markets and crater on spikes. The reversion trade is recovery after a spike — not a pre-emptive long.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TBT, TQQQ): Volatility decay + roll costs mean these drift from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical only; for multi-week holds prefer the unlevered ETF or equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, UUP issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time, which can complicate your taxes. Prefer No-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB for commodities) where available.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX/GDXJ track gold miners (companies), not gold. XOP/XLE track oil-company stocks, not WTI crude. They carry equity beta and can diverge meaningfully from the underlying commodity.
