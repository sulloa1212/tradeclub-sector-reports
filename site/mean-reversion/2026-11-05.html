Technical Indicator Quick-Reference

RSI — Relative Strength Index (0–100 momentum gauge)
Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buying may be exhausted). Extreme readings: below 20 / above 80. RSI alone is not a signal — it needs confirmation.
Bollinger %B (position within the price bands)
Below 0 = price below the lower band (statistically stretched down); above 1 = above the upper band (stretched up). Bands are set at 2 standard deviations from a 20-day moving average.
Z-score vs 50-day mean (how many standard deviations stretched)
Z-score of ±2 is unusual; ±3 is rare. A Z-score of −3 means the price is 3 standard deviations below its recent average — a statistical extreme that often precedes a snap-back.
% from 50-day / 200-day SMA (simple moving average — a smoothed recent price line)
Shows how far the current price has drifted from its recent "normal." The 50-day is the short-term mean; the 200-day is the long-term trend baseline. A big negative % = oversold; big positive % = overbought.
IV — Implied Volatility (the options market's forecast of future price movement)
High IV = options are expensive (premium selling has an edge); low IV = options are cheap (premium buying has an edge). IV often spikes on the same event that creates the RSI extreme — that double-signal is the best reversion setup.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Read this section before acting on anything above. These are the rules that separate a reversion trade from a loss.

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter:

  • Reject falling knives: A low RSI alone is not a reason to buy. If a stock dropped 50%+ in days with no stabilization, a structural break is likely (fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, going-concern). No reversion setup here.
  • Reject structural re-ratings: Intel's −26% gap is partly structural (PC cycle + interest coverage −8.99). That doesn't make it untradeable — but it means the reversion target is lower, and position sizing must reflect the ongoing uncertainty.
  • Reject buyout pins: If a stock is repriced to a merger/acquisition deal price, it will not "revert" — it's anchored to the deal.
  • Require stabilization before entry: Look for the RSI to stop falling, two consecutive green candles, or a volume pattern showing sellers exhausted. Don't buy while the chart is still falling.

Proxy and K-1 flags — know what you own:

  • Equity proxies (XLE, XOP, GDX, SMH, etc.): These hold stocks, not commodities. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying spot price, especially in a macro rotation. XLE falling 10% while WTI falls 8% is normal — but XLE can also fall more if the equity market sells off for unrelated reasons.
  • K-1 tax forms: USO, UNG, UCO, DBC, and many commodity futures ETFs issue a Schedule K-1, which complicates your taxes. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives where available: PDBC (broad commodities), XLE/XOP (energy equity, no K-1). Note this to your accountant if you trade K-1-issuing funds.
  • Leveraged and futures ETFs (UCO, SCO, TQQQ, BOIL, TBT, etc.): These are for short-term tactical use only. Volatility decay and futures roll (contango/backwardation) cause them to drift from any long-run mean. Never hold these for multi-week reversion plays — use the unlevered fund instead.
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — special rule: Trade these ONLY when stretched UP (RSI high, price spiking on a fear event). They revert down reliably as the spike fades. Never buy them as "oversold" — structural roll decay grinds them lower over time. A low RSI on VXX is drift, not a setup.
  • Crypto ETFs (IBIT, BITO, ETHA, GBTC): Treat like volatility. Strong trends, extreme gaps, 20%+ moves in hours. Defined risk only. Paper-trade first. The Fear & Greed Index at 34 means crypto sentiment remains fragile despite Bitcoin's bounce.

Verify before trading — always:

  • Every price, RSI, moving average, and date in this report is a snapshot from automated sources assembled pre-market. By the time you read this, they have changed.
  • Earnings dates are the #1 hallucination risk. Any earnings date marked "⚠ date unconfirmed — verify" should be looked up in your own brokerage or on the company's investor relations page before entering a position. A surprise earnings announcement inside your expiration window can destroy an options trade overnight.
