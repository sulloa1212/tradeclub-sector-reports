7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the general method — how direction and IV combine to suggest educational structures. The specific results (which ticker uses which) appear in the tables above.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone); above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme. Does NOT predict direction alone — always confirm with other measures.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Tells you where price sits within its 20-day trading band. Below 0 = outside lower band (oversold); above 1 = outside upper band (overbought). Confirms RSI signals.
Z-Score vs 50d Mean
How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score of –2 or lower (or +2 or higher) is statistically extreme and supports a reversion thesis.
Implied Volatility (IV)
How expensive options are right now, expressed as an annual % move the market implies. High IV = options are pricey = selling premium (credit spreads, CSPs) is more attractive. Low IV = options are cheap = buying premium (debit spreads) is more attractive.
Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
Cash-secured put (CSP): Sell a put below support. Collect premium; buy the stock if assigned at a discount. Max loss = strike price minus premium received.

Bull put spread: Sell a higher put, buy a lower put. Both strikes below the current price. Defined max loss = spread width minus premium collected. Lower risk than a naked put.
Oversold + Low IV → Premium Buying
Bull call spread (debit): Buy a call at or near the money, sell a higher-strike call. Pay a net debit. Max loss = debit paid. Profits if the stock rises back toward the mean. Cheap IV makes the debit smaller.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling
Call credit spread (bear call spread): Sell a lower call, buy a higher call — both above current price. Collect a credit; profit if the stock stays below the short strike. Max loss = spread width minus credit.
Overbought + Low IV → Premium Buying
Bear put spread (debit): Buy a higher put, sell a lower put. Pay a net debit. Max loss = debit paid. Profits if the stock falls toward the mean. Post-earnings IV crush makes this the right structure after an earnings-gap overbought stock.
Always Defined-Risk First
Every structure above defines your maximum loss before you enter. No naked calls or puts — those have unlimited or very large risk. Always know your max loss BEFORE you place a trade. Paper-trade these structures first.

8How to Read / Guardrails

The Most Important Filter: Reversion vs. Ruin

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Always ask: "Is this a stretched but intact business, or is the market correctly re-pricing a broken one?" Here is the filter in plain English:

  • Keep if: the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over a sound business, near prior support, with no fundamental impairment.
  • Reject / flag if: driven by fraud, going-concern doubt, failed trial, bankruptcy, dividend cut, debt blowup, or a structural earnings reset like INTC's Q4 guide miss. A –25% gap is NOT a reversion candidate on day one.
  • Reject buyouts — the price is pinned to a deal, not its historical mean.
  • Flag spinoff resets (like CTVA) — the new entity has a different fundamental profile; the old "mean" no longer applies until a new base forms.
Special Handling Reminders
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Only trade the downside fade after a spike. A low RSI is structural decay — not a buy signal. Today's RSI is ~31–37 = no spike-fade setup active.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TQQQ, SOXL, TBT, UCO): Volatility decay + contango/backwardation mean they drift away from any stable mean. Short-term tactical only; prefer unlevered funds for multi-week holds.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX): These track companies, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying. State so explicitly when using them.
  • K-1 tax forms: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, many CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at year-end. This complicates taxes. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB for no-K-1 commodity exposure.
  • ETF liquidity check: Always verify open interest and average option volume before trading ETF options — even Tier B/C funds can have thin chains that widen spreads unacceptably.
The #1 Beginner Mistake: Buying Every Dip

Mean reversion works best when there is a clear, stable mean and a credible reason the current price will return to it. Today's INTC is a perfect counter-example: the Q4 guidance miss has set a new, lower fundamental mean. The old $92 price is not the "mean" to revert to — it was already the prior bull run. A new base must form over weeks before a reversion thesis is valid.

Similarly, a downtrending sector (semis) can stay oversold for weeks. Oversold is a necessary condition for a reversion setup, not a sufficient one. Wait for signs of stabilization — a volume dry-up, a morning-star candle, an RSI divergence — before sizing in.
