7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what each structure is and when it fits. The specific "why this read" reasons for each name live in the tables above.

Direction + IV → Structure
Oversold + High IV: Cash-secured put or put credit spread (sell the fear premium; defined risk below a strike you're comfortable owning at; collect inflated premium).
Oversold + Low IV: Bull call debit spread (buy the cheap premium rather than selling it; pay less for a defined-risk upside position).
Overbought + High IV: Call credit spread or bear put spread (sell rich call premium in a stretched-up name; define your max loss with the short strike + long strike structure).
Overbought + Low IV: Bear put debit spread (buy cheap downside; if IV is low, premium is affordable).
Always: Choose defined-risk structures first. Never sell naked options on highly volatile names. Paper-trade any structure before risking real capital.
What the numbers mean
RSI(14): A 0–100 momentum gauge comparing recent gains vs. losses over 14 sessions. Below 30 = oversold (sellers may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buyers may be exhausted). Not a prediction — a flag for further investigation.
Bollinger %B (20,2): Where price sits within its Bollinger bands. Below 0 = price is outside the lower band (very stretched down); above 1 = outside the upper band (very stretched up).
Z-score vs 50d mean: How many standard deviations the price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score of −2 or below (or +2 or above) means an unusually large departure from the recent norm — the bigger the absolute number, the more stretched the asset.
% from 50d / 200d SMA: How far the price has moved from its 50-day and 200-day simple moving averages — the "reference means" a reversion candidate might return toward.
IV (Implied Volatility): The options market's estimate of expected future price swings. High IV = expensive options (favors selling premium); Low IV = cheap options (favors buying premium). IV is not a direction signal — it's a pricing signal.
Composite stretch rating: A name scores higher the more of these measures align simultaneously. 3+ measures at an extreme = higher-quality reversion thesis vs. RSI alone.

8How to Read This / Guardrails

Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter — the most important rule

An oversold RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Always ask: is this selling driven by positioning excess over a fundamentally sound business, or by a permanent structural break? Reject any name with:

  • Fraud, going-concern doubt, bankruptcy risk, accounting restatement.
  • A single-session drop of −30%+ with no stabilization (e.g., CRDO was excluded today).
  • Buyout/merger pinning the price to a deal (not a "mean" — a new price anchor).
  • A drug trial failure, earnings guidance disaster, or dividend cut that changes the business permanently.

Keep names where the selloff looks like macro fear, crowded positioning, or sector rotation over an otherwise intact franchise — and where a visible prior support level or moving average defines a credible "mean" to revert toward.

Special ETF Caveats
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade them ONLY when stretched UP (vol spike). Never treat a low RSI on VXX/UVXY as a buy setup — structural contango roll decay + leverage drag grind them lower permanently. The clean trade is fading the spike, not buying the dip.
  • Inverse-vol ETPs (SVXY, SVIX): They grind UP and crater on vol spikes. Their reversion setup is recovery after a crash — not the same as the long-vol fade.
  • Leveraged / Futures ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, UNG, TBT, TQQQ, SQQQ, 2x/3x anything): Volatility decay + contango/backwardation roll mean they drift from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical reversion (days, not weeks); for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 Tax Flag: USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, CPER, DBC, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form, which complicates tax filing. Prefer PDBC, BCI, COMB, or equity-sector ETFs (XLE, XOP) where the exposure allows. Always consult your tax adviser.
  • Proxy vs. Spot: GDX/GDXJ track gold-mining companies, not gold bullion. XOP tracks E&P companies, not crude directly. OIH tracks oil-service companies. URA/URNM track uranium miners. These carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying commodity — especially in risk-off episodes where stocks fall even if the commodity rises. Always know what you're actually buying.
