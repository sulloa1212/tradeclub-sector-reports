🚨 Vol Spike Fade — Highest-Confidence Setup
VXX / UVXY / VIXY: Fade the Spike

What happened: The VIX surged from 19.30 to 35.89 in a single week — an increase of ~86% and the 5th-largest weekly gain on record. VIX hit an intraday high of 40.03 on Friday, a level only reached during genuine panics. VXX and UVXY (which track VIX futures) spiked in lockstep. This morning, VIX is already pulling back ~13% to ~31.

Why this is the highest-confidence reversion:

  • VIX mean-reverts structurally: it spikes in panics and reliably falls as conditions normalize. The VIX cannot stay at 35–40 forever.
  • VXX and UVXY are structurally decaying instruments (VIX-futures contango + leverage drag). They grind lower over time. A spike gives you a rare, well-defined entry point to fade from the top.
  • Historical data: after the previous 4 times the VIX rose 16.5+ points in a week (since 1990), the S&P 500 averaged +5.1% the next session and +3.3% the following week — with 100% positive returns. (Source: Kobeissi Letter / KuCoin — verify independently.)
  • The GS Panic Index hit 9.8/10 — a "capitulation crescendo" signal, not a fresh trend start.

Educational structure: When VXX/UVXY are stretched UP, the defined-risk trade is to sell premium on the instruments (e.g., bear call spread on VXX, or bear put spread on SVXY as SVXY recovers). Never buy long-vol ETPs as a "dip buy" — the structural contango decay makes that a losers' bet over time. Always paper-trade first. Indicator definitions — what each measure means
  • RSI(14) — Relative Strength Index: a 0–100 gauge of recent price momentum. Below 30 = oversold (sellers may have overdone it). Above 70 = overbought (buyers may have overdone it). Extreme zones: below 20 / above 80.
  • Bollinger %B (20,2) — where price sits within its normal range (the "bands"). Below 0 = price below the lower band (extreme oversold); above 1 = above the upper band (extreme overbought).
  • % from 50-day / 200-day SMA — how far price is from its moving average (the "mean"). A large negative % (–10%, –20%) means stretched below the mean — potential for a snap-back. These are reference points for WHERE the mean is, not predictions.
  • Z-score — how many statistical standard deviations (units of "normal variation") price is from its 50-day average. Z < –2 means very stretched below average.
  • IV (Implied Volatility) — the market's expectation of future price moves, embedded in option prices. High IV = options are expensive (better to sell). Low IV = options are cheap (better to buy).
  • Composite stretch score (1–5) — how many of the above measures confirm an extreme simultaneously. 3+ measures = strong setup. 1 measure only = weak / pass.

⚠ Special Rules for Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade them ONLY when stretched UP — fade the spike. NEVER treat a low RSI on VXX as a "buy the dip" opportunity. Structural contango decay and leverage drag grind them lower over time regardless of VIX levels.

  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, TBT etc.): Volatility decay + roll costs mean they deviate from any long-run price mean. Use for short-term tactical reversion only; for multi-week holds prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 (a complex tax form). Prefer no-K-1 alternatives where available: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB. Always consult a tax professional.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, XOP, COPX, URA, MOO, SIL track companies, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying commodity. This is stated explicitly in every entry where it applies.
