7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — the educational structures mentioned in the tables above, and what each indicator measures. Specific results and trade theses are in Sections 2–5 above, not here.

RSI (14-day)
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (stock fell fast); above 70 = overbought (stock rose fast). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. RSI alone is not enough — combine with other measures.
Bollinger %B
Measures where price sits within its recent 20-day band. Below 0 = price below the lower band (stretched down); above 1 = price above the upper band (stretched up). A reading outside the bands on RSI + %B simultaneously = stronger setup.
Distance from 50/200-day SMA
How far the current price is from its average over 50 or 200 days. A stock trading −20% below its 50-day SMA is statistically stretched — the average is the "mean" it could revert toward.
IV (Implied Volatility)
How expensive options are. IV elevated = options cost more = premium-selling structures (credit spreads, cash-secured puts) receive more premium. IV low = options cost less = buying spreads (debit spreads) is more efficient.
Bull Put Spread (oversold + high IV)
Sell a put at a higher strike, buy a put at a lower strike. You collect premium up front; your max loss is the difference between strikes minus premium received. Profits if the stock stays above your short put. Always defined risk.
Bear Call Spread (overbought + high IV)
Sell a call at a lower strike, buy a call at a higher strike. You collect premium; max loss is the difference between strikes minus premium received. Profits if the stock stays below your short call. Always defined risk.
Bull Call Spread (oversold + low IV)
Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. You pay a debit (net cost); max loss is the premium paid. Profits if the stock rises toward or past your long call. Better when options are cheap.
Bear Put Spread (overbought + low IV)
Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. You pay a debit; max loss is the premium paid. Profits if the stock falls toward or past your long put. Better when options are cheap.
Cash-Secured Put
Sell a put and hold the cash required to buy the stock if assigned. You keep the premium if the stock stays above your put strike. Max loss = strike price minus premium, if stock goes to zero. Best for stocks you'd be willing to own at a discount.
Defined Risk First — Always
Every structure above caps your maximum loss at the premium paid (debit spreads) or the difference between strikes minus premium (credit spreads). Never sell naked options without understanding the unlimited-risk profile. Paper-trade every structure before using real money.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Know the rules before using any setup — these filters protect you from the most common mean-reversion mistakes.

The reversion-vs-ruin filter (most important check)

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Always ask: why did it fall? If the answer is fraud, bankruptcy, debt blow-up, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, going-concern doubt, or a permanent political regime change — it is not a reversion candidate, it is a falling knife. Reject it. Today's example: EWZ is rejected because a 20%+ political shock is a structural re-rating, not a sentiment extreme over a sound underlying.

Proxy vs. Spot
ETFs like GDX (gold miners), XOP (E&P companies), URA (uranium miners), and ITA (aerospace stocks) track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity price. Always know what you actually own.
K-1 Tax Warning
USO, UNG, UCO, BOIL, DBC, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form instead of a 1099. This complicates taxes. Where available, prefer no-K-1 alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB. Always check with your tax adviser.
Leveraged ETF Decay
2× and 3× ETFs (UCO, SOXL, BOIL, TQQQ, TBT, etc.) suffer from volatility decay — they lose value over time relative to 2× or 3× the underlying index return. Use them for short-term tactical trades only; for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered ETF or equity proxy instead.
Long-Vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY)
These only make sense as reversion trades when they have just spiked UP on a fear event. The contango roll and leverage decay grind them lower over time — a low RSI in VXX is just the structural drift, not a buy signal. Fade spikes; never buy the dip in long-vol ETPs.
FOMC Override Risk (Wed Oct 28)
The Fed's decision Wednesday can move rates, energy, gold, equities, and the dollar simultaneously. Every rate-sensitive setup (TLT, GLD, XLU, XLRE, BAC, IBIT) is vulnerable to a FOMC surprise. Do not hold undefined-risk positions through the announcement.
Earnings Dates Are Hallucination-Prone
All earnings dates in this report are sourced from live searches where possible. Where a date could not be confirmed, "date unconfirmed — verify immediately" is written. Never trade an options structure through an earnings event without first confirming the date from your own brokerage calendar.
Multiple Confirmations Rule
A name qualifies for this list only when 3+ measures are stretched (RSI + Bollinger %B + distance from moving average, or RSI + multi-day streak + ATR distance). RSI alone is a yellow flag, not a trade signal. The more measures that agree, the stronger the setup quality.
Paper Trade First
Every structure in this report should be paper-traded (simulated, no real money) before you risk actual capital. Mean-reversion trades look compelling on paper and can still go very wrong in practice. Confirm the setup is still valid at time of entry, not just at report generation time.
