−58
Direction Score
The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.73% — near its highest in months and rising — is the direct, mechanical enemy of REITs (real estate investment trusts). REITs borrow heavily and pay dividends; when risk-free rates rise, their valuations compress and their yields look less attractive. Cross-currents (what makes this wrong): A shockingly cool CPI tomorrow triggers a rate-cut rally — yields drop sharply, REITs spike hard. Any ceasefire/deal on Hormuz also crushes oil and inflation expectations, potentially sparking a REIT relief rally. These are real risks if you're short.

7How to Read This Report

This legend explains the generic methodology — how the scores are built. The actual results and plain-English reasons are in the body above.

Direction Score (−100 to +100)
Weighted composite: ETF trend & price structure (30%), relative strength vs SPY 1–3 months (25%), macro tailwind/headwind (20%), news & catalyst flow (15%), momentum/breadth (10%). Positive = net bullish; negative = net bearish. Not a prediction — a current-conditions read.
Conviction (High / Medium / Low)
How clean and aligned the evidence is across all five inputs. High = multiple factors point the same way. Low = mixed signals or thin data. Low-conviction scores should be weighted less in your planning.
Swing-Conviction Score (0–100)
Technical setup quality (35%), relative strength vs sector & market (25%), catalyst & earnings proximity (20%), move-strength potential — beta/range/liquidity (20%). Measures how likely a stock is to move hard in the sector's direction over 3 days–6 weeks. Higher = stronger expected move.
Fundamental Health Score (0–100)
