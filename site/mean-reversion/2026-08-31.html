7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the method — what the indicators measure and which options structures fit which combination of direction and IV. Every specific result and "why this read" stays in the tables above; this is the generic reference.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Under 30 = potentially oversold (selling may be exhausted); over 70 = potentially overbought (buying may be exhausted); extreme reads are <20 / >80. RSI alone is not a signal — require 3+ confirming measures.
Bollinger %B
Measures where price sits within its 20-day, 2-standard-deviation band. Above 1.0 = price outside the upper band (overbought); below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold). Useful for confirming RSI extremes.
Z-Score vs 50-day Mean
How many standard deviations (a measure of typical price spread) the price is from its 50-day average. Z > +2 = stretched up; Z < −2 = stretched down. A higher absolute Z-score = more stretched.
% from 50 / 200-day SMA
The simple distance between current price and its moving averages (price trend lines). The larger the gap, the more stretched the move. A "mean" is the level price tends to revert toward.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The market's option-price-implied expectation of future price swings. High IV = options are expensive (favors selling premium via spreads). Low IV = options are cheap (favors buying calls/puts outright). IV is not a directional indicator — it only tells you the "price" of options.
Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling Bullish
Cash-secured puts, bull put spreads (sell a put, buy a lower put), or bull call spreads. Defined risk = you know the maximum loss before entering. Always paper-trade first.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling Bearish
Call credit spreads (sell a call, buy a higher call), bear call spreads, or iron condors (sell both a call spread and a put spread). Defined risk means capped max loss regardless of how far the stock moves.
Oversold + Low IV → Buying Calls
When options are cheap (low IV) and the asset is oversold, buying calls or bull call spreads can be cost-effective. Low IV buying is rarer in today's environment — check IV percentile before selecting structure.
Composite Rank Logic
A name that shows 3+ stretch measures (RSI, %B, Z-score, % from 50-day, consecutive down days vs ATR) is ranked higher than one showing RSI alone. Quality of the reversion thesis (clear pre-shock mean, positioning excess, sound business) is weighted over raw stretch.
Defined Risk First — Always
Every structure in this report is defined-risk: you know the maximum loss before entering. This means spreads over naked options. Especially important on leveraged ETFs (UCO, TQQQ), vol ETFs (VXX, UVXY), and single stocks with binary events.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Read this before using any setup in this report.

  • Reversion vs. Ruin: A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. We reject names that are "falling knives" — driven by structural breaks (fraud, bankruptcy, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, dividend cut, accounting restatement) or down 50%+ in days with no stabilization. Every name in this report cleared that filter. If a name you're watching fails any of those criteria, treat it as a falling knife, not a reversion setup.
  • Proxy vs. Spot — always know what you own: GDX/GDXJ track gold mining companies, not gold. XLE/XOP/OIH track energy companies, not crude oil. URA/COPX/LIT track industrial-sector equities, not the commodity. Equity proxies carry company-specific risk, earnings risk, and beta to the broader equity market — they can diverge dramatically from the underlying commodity.
  • K-1 Tax Warning: USO, UCO, UNG, UGA, BOIL, CPER, VIXY, UVXY, and several others issue a Schedule K-1 (a complex partnership tax document) instead of a standard 1099. If you hold these in a taxable account, consult a tax professional. Prefer No-K-1 alternatives where possible: PDBC (broad commodity, no K-1), BCI, COMB, or equity-sector ETFs (XLE, XOP).
  • Leveraged and Futures ETFs — short-term only: UCO, BOIL, KOLD, SCO, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, and all 2×/3× funds suffer volatility decay and futures roll drag. They do NOT track their stated multiple over multi-week periods. Use for very short-term tactical reversion only (days, not weeks). Always prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy for any hold beyond a week.
  • Long-vol ETFs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY) — fade spikes only: These funds are structurally designed to decay over time (contango roll cost ~5–10%/month in calm markets). UVXY has lost over 99.99% of its value since inception. The only clean trade is fading a spike when RSI is stretched UP. A low RSI on a long-vol ETP is drift, not a buy signal. Never treat them as "oversold" buys.
  • Earnings and event dates are #1 hallucination risk: Every earnings date in this report that is not confirmed by a live search result is labeled "date unconfirmed — verify." Do not enter any options trade without confirming the next earnings date in your brokerage. An unexpected earnings announcement after you open a position can override any technical thesis.
  • All figures are snapshots — verify before acting: RSI, price levels, % from moving averages, and IV readings in this report are model-generated estimates based on pre-market data as of 8:58 AM ET on August 31, 2026. Markets move. Always confirm every number against your live brokerage or charting platform before placing any trade.
