7How to Read This Report

A quick legend so the numbers always make sense — what each score means and how they work together.

Direction Score (−100 to +100) Rates the sector's net bullish or bearish lean. Inputs: price trend vs moving averages (30%), relative strength vs SPY (25%), macro tailwinds/headwinds (20%), news/catalyst flow (15%), momentum/breadth (10%). A score of +60 or above = strong bullish setup; −60 or below = strong bearish setup; ±20 or less = neutral.
Conviction (High / Medium / Low) How many of the five scoring inputs agree. High = four or five inputs aligned in one direction. Low = only one or two inputs aligned, with significant counter-arguments. Conviction is independent of the score magnitude — a −45 score with Low conviction is less actionable than a −45 with High conviction.
Swing-Conviction Score (0–100) Rates how strongly a stock is expected to move in its sector's direction over a 3-day to 6-week swing. This is a technically-led score — chart setup, relative strength, catalyst proximity, and "move strength" potential (beta/range/liquidity). Higher = more likely to move harder in the expected direction. Used for stock selection.
Fundamental Health Score (0–100) Measures absolute business quality, separate from the chart. Inputs: revenue growth, EPS growth/beat history, margins, FCF & balance sheet, analyst revisions, guidance/backlog, dividends/buybacks. This score is stable (changes quarterly with earnings), while the swing score changes daily with the chart.
⛽ ADDS FUEL / NEUTRAL / ⚠ FIGHTS TREND The "Fuel Tag" shows whether the fundamental health helps or hurts the directional swing thesis. For longs: Health ≥65 = ⛽ ADDS FUEL (strong company, trend has fundamental backing). For shorts: Health ≤40 = ⛽ ADDS FUEL (weak company — easier to fall further). "FIGHTS TREND" means the fundamentals push back against the chart direction — higher risk, require more caution.
⚠ Earnings in Window Any earnings date inside the 6-week swing window is flagged amber. An earnings event can move a stock 10–40% in either direction overnight, overwhelming any technical setup. Options prices (implied volatility) will also expand going into the print and collapse after — both affect risk. If a stock's earnings are inside the window, size the position smaller or use defined-risk options. Always verify dates with your broker.
