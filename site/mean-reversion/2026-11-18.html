7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — which structure fits which direction and IV environment. The specific setups for each name are in the tables above.

First rule, always: Paper-trade any strategy before risking real money. Options involve substantial risk — you can lose the entire premium paid, and in some structures, more than you invested.
RSI (0–100)A momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (potential bounce territory). Above 70 = overbought (potential pullback territory). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. It measures speed and size of recent moves, not fundamental value.
Bollinger %BShows where price sits inside its 20-day "normal range" (the bands). %B below 0 = price outside the lower band (stretched down). %B above 1 = stretched above the upper band. Used as a confirmation signal alongside RSI.
Z-Score vs 50dHow many "standard deviations" (average-sized moves) price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score of −2 or lower = unusually far below the mean. A strong reversion signal when combined with RSI and %B.
IV (Implied Volatility)The options market's estimate of how much a stock might move. High IV = options are expensive (premiums are fat). Low IV = options are cheap. Reversion + high IV = ideal for premium-selling (collecting fat premiums on a move you expect to reverse).

Direction + IV → Structure (educational — always defined-risk first):

Oversold + High IV

Cash-secured put (sell a put at or below support, collect premium, buy shares if assigned). Bull put spread (sell a higher put / buy a lower put — limits loss). Both collect premium if the stock stays above your short strike.

Oversold + Low IV

Bull call spread (buy a lower call / sell a higher call — pay a small debit). Long call (simple, cheap when IV is low, but loses 100% if wrong). Less ideal because there is no premium cushion.

Overbought + High IV

Bear call spread (sell a lower call / buy a higher call — collect premium if stock stays below your short strike). Bear put spread (buy a higher put / sell a lower put — pay a small debit). Both are defined-risk.

Overbought + Low IV

Bear put spread (buy puts at a low cost when IV is cheap). Long put (buy a put outright — costs little when IV is low, but requires the stock to actually move lower). Less premium cushion than when IV is high.

Vol Spike Fade (VXX/UVXY up)

When long-vol ETPs are stretched UP (RSI >75+): bear call spread or put debit spread on VXX/UVXY as the spike fades. Only use when RSI is genuinely stretched high — not at the current neutral reading.

8How to Read This Report & Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter: A low RSI is necessary but NOT sufficient for a reversion setup. We actively reject "falling knives." Names are excluded from the oversold table when: (a) the move is driven by a structural break — fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, dividend cut, accounting restatement; (b) the drop is enormous (-50%+ in days) with no stabilization and no clear catalyst washout; (c) it is a buyout/merger repricing (the stock is now pinned to a deal price, not its mean). We keep names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business — ideally near a prior support level.
Proxy ETFsETFs marked "proxy" (GDX, XOP, URA, COPX, etc.) track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from spot prices. GDX miners may move differently from gold itself.
K-1 Tax FlagUSO, UNG, DBC, UCO, BOIL and some commodity ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — adds complexity. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB where the exposure allows. Always confirm with your brokerage or tax adviser.
Leveraged / Futures ETFsUCO, SOXL, BOIL, TQQQ, TBT, etc. suffer volatility decay and contango/backwardation roll costs. Use for short-term tactical reversion only — for multi-week holds prefer the unleveraged fund or equity-sector proxy.
Long-Vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY)Trade only when stretched UP (RSI/price spike on a vol event). They revert down reliably as the spike fades. Never buy them because the RSI is low — structural contango roll grinds them lower over time. A low RSI in VXX is drift, not a setup.
Earnings / EventsAn upcoming earnings report or major macro event inside ~30 days can override any technical setup — it's a known binary that can gap a stock in either direction. Always check your brokerage calendar before entering a reversion trade.
