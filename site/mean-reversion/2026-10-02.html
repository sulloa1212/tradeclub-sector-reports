How to map direction + IV to a structure

7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — how each structure works and when it fits. The specific setups and the reasons they apply are in the body tables above. Always paper-trade first, always use defined-risk structures, and always verify data before acting.

📐 What the indicators mean
  • RSI (14) — Relative Strength Index (0–100 momentum gauge): <30 = oversold; >70 = overbought; <20 / >80 = extreme.
  • Bollinger %B (20,2) — where price sits within its band: <0 = below lower band (oversold); >1 = above upper band (overbought).
  • Z-score — how many standard deviations price is from its 50-day mean: >2 = significantly stretched; >3 = extreme.
  • Stochastic / StochRSI — momentum oscillators similar to RSI but more sensitive to recent price swings.
  • ATR (Average True Range) — average daily price swing; used to assess whether a move is unusually large.
🎯 Direction + IV → Structure map
  • Oversold + High IV: Cash-secured put or bull put spread — collect premium while positioning for a bounce; ideal combo.
  • Oversold + Low IV: Bull call spread — buy the upside cheaply; less premium income.
  • Overbought + High IV: Call credit spread or bear put spread — sell the elevated premium on the downside thesis; ideal combo.
  • Overbought + Low IV: Bear put spread — buy puts cheaply; less premium income but still defined risk.
  • All structures: defined-risk first. Never sell naked options without a hedge leg. Paper-trade before risking real money.
📏 Composite scoring explained

Names ranked by how many of these fire simultaneously. A name at RSI extreme AND outside Bollinger bands AND >2 SD from its 50d mean beats a name at RSI extreme alone. Three or more confirmations = higher quality setup. Quality always beats quantity.

⚡ IV note key
  • IV elevated: Options premiums are expensive — selling strategies (credit spreads, cash-secured puts) collect more income. Ideal for "sell the extreme" setups.
  • IV low: Options are cheap — buying strategies (debit spreads, long calls/puts) cost less. Less income from selling.
  • IV very elevated: Consider wide strikes or smaller size — the market is pricing in big moves. Don't let the elevated premium tempt you into ignoring the risk.
Critical filters and warnings

8How to Read · Guardrails

Before using any name in this report, run it through these filters. A low RSI is a starting point for research, not a buy signal by itself.

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter
  • Reject falling knives: If a stock is down −50%+ in days with no stabilization, it may have more to fall. Look for some price stabilization before considering entry.
  • Reject structural breaks: Fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cuts, failed drug trials, bankruptcy, accounting restatements, failed mergers. An RSI of 20 means nothing if the business is broken.
  • Reject buyout pins: A stock repriced to a deal price is not reverting to its prior mean — it's pinned to deal value.
  • Keep: Names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, ideally near a prior support level with a well-defined pre-shock "mean."
Proxy, K-1, and Leverage Warnings
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, XOP, ITA, XLE, MOO): These track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from spot prices.
  • K-1 issuers (USO, UNG, DBC, UCO, most CurrencyShares): Issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time, adding complexity. Prefer PDBC, COMB, or BCI for broad commodity exposure without K-1.
  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (TBT, UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, UVXY): Volatility decay + contango/backwardation mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical plays only (days to a week). Do not hold multi-week as mean-reversion positions.
  • Long-vol ETPs specifically (VXX, UVXY): Only trade when stretched UP. A low RSI on these is not a buy setup — it is structural decay. Never buy VXX/UVXY because the RSI is low.
