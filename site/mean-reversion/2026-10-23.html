7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic method — how direction and implied volatility (IV) map to defined-risk structures. The specific setups for each name appear in the tables above.

Oversold + High IV
Put credit spread or cash-secured put. You collect premium as IV is elevated; your max loss is defined by the spread width. Profits if price stays above the short strike.
Oversold + Low IV
Bull call spread. Buying calls is cheaper when IV is low; the spread caps your max loss at the net debit paid. Profits if price rises to/beyond the long strike.
Overbought + High IV
Call credit spread or bear put spread. Selling a call spread collects inflated premium; max loss is defined. Profits if price stays below the short call strike.
Overbought + Low IV
Bear put spread. Buying puts is cheaper when IV is low; spread caps max loss at net debit. Profits if price falls to/beyond the long put strike.
Vol spike (VXX/UVXY spiked UP)
Put credit spread on VXX or short call spread on UVXY. Fades the spike as contango drag and mean-reversion work together. Short-dated expiry (1–2 weeks). Defined risk mandatory.
Range-bound / Uncertain
Iron condor (sell both a call spread and a put spread around the current price). Profits if price stays inside both spreads through expiry. Best in high-IV, low-directional-conviction setups.

Key indicator definitions:

RSI (14)
Relative Strength Index — a 0–100 gauge comparing recent gains to recent losses over 14 sessions. Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought; extreme readings: <20 or >80.
Bollinger %B
Shows where price sits within its Bollinger Band (20-day, 2 standard deviations). Below 0 = outside lower band (oversold extreme); above 1 = outside upper band (overbought extreme).
Z-score
How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. A z-score of ±2 is statistically extreme; ±3 is very rare.
ATR
Average True Range — a measure of typical daily price movement. A move of 3× ATR in one day signals an unusually large, potentially exhausted move.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The options market's forecast of future price swings — expressed as a percentage. High IV = expensive options (favor selling structures); low IV = cheaper options (favor buying structures).

8How to Read / Guardrails

Before trading any idea in this report, run each name through these filters — in order.

Filter 1 — Reversion vs. Ruin (most important)

A low RSI alone is not enough. Reject a name if any of these apply:

  • The move is driven by fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, or a dividend cut that breaks the business model.
  • The drop is –50%+ in days with no stabilization candle — that is a falling knife, not a reversion setup.
  • It is pinned to a buyout/merger price — the stock will track the deal price, not its old mean.

Keep a name only when the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise intact business, ideally near a prior support level.

Filter 2 — Special Handling Rules
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade only when stretched UP (spike to fade). Never treat as "oversold" buys — contango roll decay grinds them lower in calm markets.
  • Leveraged ETFs (SOXL, UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.): Volatility decay + roll cost means they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term (1–5 day) tactical trades. For multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund.
  • K-1 tax flag (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL): These commodity partnerships issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time, adding complexity. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI) where the same exposure is available.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX/GDXJ, XLE/XOP/OIH, SMH/SOXX track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices. A chip-sector ETF is not the same as being long semiconductor prices.
  • Crypto ETFs (IBIT, BITO, ETHA, GBTC): Treat like volatility products — strong trends, extreme beta, can stay overbought/oversold for extended periods. Defined-risk structures mandatory; never naked short.
