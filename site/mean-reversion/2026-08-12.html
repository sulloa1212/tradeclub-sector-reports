7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic method — how direction and IV level map to defined-risk structures. The specific results and recommendations for each name are in Sections 2–5 above. Always paper-trade first and verify IV rank against your live brokerage before entering.

Direction + IV → Structure Map

▲ Oversold + High IV

The asset is stretched down AND options premiums are rich. Selling premium captures both the edge. Use: Bull put spread (sell a put, buy a lower put = net credit, defined max loss) or cash-secured put (sell a put, hold cash to cover assignment).

▲ Oversold + Low IV

The asset is stretched down but premium is thin. Buying options is cheaper here. Use: Bull call spread (buy a call, sell a higher call = net debit, defined max loss) or long call for maximum upside with defined downside.

▼ Overbought + High IV

The asset is stretched up AND options premiums are rich. Use: Bear call spread (sell a call, buy a higher call = net credit, defined max loss) or bear put spread for directional downside with defined risk.

▼ Overbought + Low IV

The asset is stretched up but premium is thin. Buying puts is cheaper here. Use: Bear put spread (buy a put, sell a lower put = net debit, defined max loss) or long put for maximum downside exposure.

⚠ Volatility Spike Fade

When VXX/UVXY are stretched UP: Use bear call spread on VXX or UVXY. Sell a call close to the current elevated price, buy a higher-strike call for protection. Net credit trade — you collect premium as vol reverts. Exit quickly (1–5 days).

→ Always Defined Risk

Every structure above has a defined maximum loss — the key principle for beginners. Never sell naked calls or puts without a hedge. The spread (buying one option while selling another) caps your worst-case loss to the difference in strikes minus the net credit received.

Indicator Glossary

RSI (14d)A 0–100 momentum gauge: below 30 = oversold (too far down), above 70 = overbought (too far up). Extreme = below 20 or above 80.
Bollinger %B (20,2)Shows price position relative to its 20-day price bands. Below 0 = below the lower band (oversold); above 1 = above the upper band (overbought).
Z-Score vs 50d MeanHow many standard deviations (units of normal variation) price is from its 50-day average. Above ±2 is statistically unusual and flags a stretch.
% vs 50d / 200d SMASimple moving average distance: how far price is above (+) or below (−) its 50-day or 200-day average. Larger deviation = more stretched.
IV (Implied Volatility)The options market's estimate of future price swings, expressed as a % annualized. High IV = expensive options; low IV = cheap options. High IV favors selling; low IV favors buying.
ATR (Average True Range)The average size of a stock's daily price range. Used to judge whether a move is "big" relative to the stock's normal swings. A move of 5× the ATR in a few days signals an extreme.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter in this framework
A low RSI is a starting point, not a conclusion. The critical question is: why did the stock fall? A setup is only valid if the move looks like sentiment or positioning excess over an otherwise intact business — not a structural break. Always apply this test before acting.

Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter — Reject If:

  • Structural break: Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, dividend cut, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, or debt blowup. Low RSI here = falling knife, not a reversion.
  • Catastrophic drop with no floor: A −50%+ move in days with no stabilization signal usually means more selling ahead. Let it find support first.
  • M&A repricing: A stock pinned to a deal/buyout price is not reverting to a technical mean — it is converging to a deal price. RSI is meaningless in this context.
  • Legal/regulatory overhang: An ongoing securities fraud investigation (e.g., IBM) is a structural-risk flag. The reversion thesis is still possible, but the tail risk is enlarged. Size conservatively and monitor news flow actively.

Special Handling — Leveraged, Futures, and Proxy ETFs

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY, UVIX): Trade ONLY when stretched UP (fading a spike). Structural contango decay + leverage drag grind these lower over time — a low RSI is drift, not a setup. Never "buy the dip" on these.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX): They grind UP and crater on vol spikes. Their reversion setup is recovery after a vol crash. Mirror of VXX — handle with care.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, SCO, TBT, 2x/3x anything): Daily-reset leverage + contango decay means no stable long-run mean exists. Use for short-term tactical reversion only (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, and most CurrencyShares issue a Schedule K-1 tax form — not a 1099. Consult your tax advisor. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB for commodity exposure without K-1.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, XLE, XOP, OIH, URA, COPX, SIL, MOO track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry additional equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices.
