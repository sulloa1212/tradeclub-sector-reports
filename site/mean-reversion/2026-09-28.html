7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the educational logic behind the structure recommendations above. No specific trade is being recommended. Always confirm quotes, open interest, and expiration availability in your own brokerage before acting.

📉 Oversold + IV Elevated → Best for Premium-Selling Structures

Put credit spread: Sell a put near/below current price, buy a cheaper put further below. You collect premium upfront; max profit if the stock stays above the short strike. Max loss = width of strikes minus credit. Cash-secured put: Sell a put and hold the cash to buy shares if assigned. Good when you'd actually want to own the stock at a lower price. Bull call spread: Buy a call at a lower strike, sell one higher. Cheaper than buying a call outright; limited upside but lower cost basis. All three express "I think the selling is overdone and the stock stabilizes or bounces."

📈 Overbought + IV Elevated → Best for Defined-Risk Downside Structures

Bear call spread: Sell a call above current price, buy a cheaper call even higher. You collect premium; profit if the stock stays below your short strike. Max loss = spread width minus credit. Bear put spread: Buy a put at a higher strike, sell one lower. Costs a debit upfront; profit if the stock falls toward the long strike. Better when IV is lower (put debit is cheaper). Today's environment (elevated IV) favors the bear call spread (sell premium) over the bear put spread (buy premium).

📏 How the Signals Are Scored

RSI (14)0–100 scale. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausted). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be exhausted). Extreme zones: below 20 / above 80. Does NOT predict direction — it measures how stretched momentum is.
Bollinger %B (20,2)Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands (2 standard deviations around the 20-day average). Below 0 = price is below the lower band (oversold). Above 1 = above the upper band (overbought).
% from 50/200 SMAHow far (in %) the current price is from the 50-day or 200-day moving average. A stock trading 20%+ above its 50-day is historically stretched; the further it is, the higher the rubber-band tension.
Z-scoreHow many standard deviations the current price is from its 50-day average price. Z-score above 2 or below −2 = statistically extreme. Higher absolute Z-score = higher-quality setup.
Composite rankA name that is extreme on RSI AND above/below Bollinger Bands AND far from its moving average beats a name extreme on just one measure. The tables above are sorted by composite stretch quality.
IV (Implied Vol)The option market's expectation of future price movement. High IV = options are expensive (favor selling premium via credit spreads). Low IV = options are cheap (favor buying options via debit spreads).

8How to Read / Guardrails

Read this section before acting on anything above. These are the most important filters that keep mean-reversion from becoming "catching falling knives."

Reversion vs. Ruin Filter (the most important filter): A low RSI is necessary but NOT sufficient. A stock down 80% with RSI of 5 is more likely a structural collapse than a bounce. Check: Is this a going-concern situation? Fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, dividend cut, or accounting restatement? Is the drop -50%+ in days with no stabilization? Is it a buyout at a pinned price? If yes to any → REJECT. Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business near prior support.
Volatility ETP Special Rules (VXX, UVXY, VIXY, SVXY, SVIX): Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) should ONLY be traded when stretched UP on a volatility spike — they revert DOWN reliably as the spike fades. NEVER treat them as "oversold buys" — structural roll decay (VIX futures contango) grinds them lower over time. A low VXX RSI is NOT a setup. Inverse-vol ETPs (SVXY, SVIX) are the opposite — they grind up and crater on vol spikes.
Leveraged & Futures-Based ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, UNG, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, all 2x/3x): Volatility decay + contango/backwardation roll mean these funds deviate from any long-run mean. Use ONLY for short-term tactical reversion. For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy (e.g., XLE instead of UCO; XOP instead of SCO).
⚠ K-1 Tax Warning: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — more complex than a 1099. Consider "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI, COMB. Verify with your tax adviser.
Proxy vs. Spot Reminder: GDX, GDXJ, XOP, COPX, URA, MOO, SIL track companies, not the commodity. They carry stock-market (equity) beta and can diverge sharply from spot commodity prices — both up and down. "GDX oversold" ≠ "gold oversold" and vice versa.
Earnings / Event Dates are the #1 Override: A mean-reversion setup that has an earnings date within ~30 days becomes a binary bet, not a reversion bet. The earnings report can permanently reset the "mean." Always check your broker's earnings calendar before entering any position.
