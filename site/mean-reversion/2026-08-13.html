Educational Reference

7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the terms used in the tables above. These are educational definitions of the method — not personalized advice for any specific trade.

Oversold + High IV → Defined-Risk Premium Selling

Cash-secured put: Sell a put below the current price; collect premium; obligation to buy shares at the strike. Profits if the stock stays above the strike through expiration.

Put credit spread: Sell a put, buy a lower put. Limits max loss to the spread width minus premium received. The "defined-risk" version — preferred for beginners.

Bull call spread: Buy a call near the money, sell a higher call. Caps profit but also caps cost. Best when IV is low (buying options is cheaper).

Overbought + High IV → Defined-Risk Premium Selling

Call credit spread: Sell a call above the current price, buy a higher call. Profits if the stock stays below the short strike. Max loss = spread width minus premium. Always use defined-risk (buy the upper call — never sell a naked call).

Bear put spread: Buy an ATM put, sell a lower put. Caps max gain but reduces cost. Best when IV is low (buying options is cheaper).

What RSI Means

RSI (Relative Strength Index): A 0–100 gauge of how fast a price has moved recently. Above 70 = overbought (moved up fast). Below 30 = oversold (moved down fast). Extremes above 80 or below 20 are more significant. RSI alone is not a trade signal — it's one input in a composite read.

What %B and Z-Score Mean

Bollinger %B (20,2): Where price sits within its Bollinger Bands. Above 1 = outside the upper band (extended up). Below 0 = outside the lower band (extended down).

Z-score: How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score above +2 or below −2 is statistically unusual and flags a stretched name.

What IV (Implied Volatility) Means

IV elevated: Options are expensive — the market expects big moves. Favors selling premium (credit spreads) because you collect more, and it tends to mean-revert lower after the event.

IV low: Options are cheap — favors buying options (debit spreads) because your cost of entry is lower.

IV crush: After a binary event (earnings), IV drops sharply — bought options lose value even if the stock moves correctly.

Composite Scoring (How Names Are Ranked)

Names ranked highest when extreme on 3+ measures simultaneously: RSI at extreme + %B outside bands + price extended vs. 50d SMA + Z-score ≥ |2| + multiple consecutive directional days vs. ATR. A name oversold on RSI only = lower confidence. A name oversold on all five = highest confidence reversion candidate.

Flag What it means Action
K-1 ETF issues a Schedule K-1 tax form instead of a 1099. More complex at tax time. Examples: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives where available (PDBC instead of DBC; BNO for Brent). Ask your tax adviser.
PROXY The ETF tracks companies, not the commodity spot price. GDX tracks gold miners; OIH tracks oil service companies. Carries equity beta — can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity. Understand you are buying a basket of equities, not the metal or crude. The ETF can fall even if the commodity rises, if the companies have other problems.
DECAY Leveraged or futures-based ETFs (TQQQ, UCO, BOIL, UNG, VXX, UVXY) suffer from volatility decay and/or contango roll. They are NOT suitable for multi-week holds as mean-reversion vehicles. Use only for short-term tactical reversion. For multi-week exposure, prefer the unlevered equivalent or an equity-sector proxy.
⚠ Earnings A confirmed binary event within ~30 days. Earnings can gap a stock 10–30% overnight, overriding any technical setup instantly. Wait for the event, or use defined-risk structures sized for a potential gap. Never hold an undefined-risk position through a binary event.
