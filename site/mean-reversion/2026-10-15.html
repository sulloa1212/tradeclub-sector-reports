8Options Structures Legend

This legend explains what the strategies mean — the actual setup for any name must be verified against your own brokerage data.

Oversold + High IV
Bull Put Spread / Cash-Secured Put
Sell a lower-strike put, buy an even-lower-strike put as protection. Collect premium now; profit if stock stays above your short strike. "Defined risk" = loss capped at spread width minus premium collected. Best when IV is high (premium is fat) and you expect a floor. Always paper-trade first.
Oversold + Low IV
Bull Call Spread
Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Pay a debit; profit if the stock rises to or past your short strike. Better when IV is low (options are cheaper). Max loss = premium paid.
Overbought + High IV
Call Credit Spread / Bear Put Spread
Call credit spread: sell a call, buy a higher-strike call. Collect premium; profit if stock stays below short call. Bear put spread: buy a put, sell a lower-strike put; pay a debit; profit if stock falls to your long put. Both define your max loss.
Event / Earnings Nearby
Wait or Size Down
An earnings or Fed event within ~30 days inflates IV (options are expensive) and creates a binary outcome that can override any technical setup. Wait for the print, then trade the vol crush. Or size down significantly and accept the extra uncertainty.
What the indicators mean:
  • RSI (0–100 momentum gauge): below 30 = oversold (sellers may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buyers may be stretched). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme.
  • Bollinger %B: below 0 = price below the lower band (unusually weak); above 1 = above the upper band (unusually strong). A stretch in both RSI and %B is a stronger signal than either alone.
  • Z-score vs 50d mean: how many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score of −2 or lower = statistically stretched; historically tends to mean-revert in stable markets.
  • Consecutive down/up days vs ATR: ATR (average true range) measures typical daily moves. A drop of 3–4× ATR in 5 days is abnormal and often precedes a pause or bounce.
  • Composite ranking: a name extreme on 3+ measures (RSI + %B + distance from SMA + ATR move) beats a name extreme on RSI alone — composite beats single-signal.

9How to Read / Guardrails

The most important section — the rules that keep a reversion scan from becoming a list of falling knives.

🔪 Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter

An oversold RSI is not sufficient to trade. Reject setups where:

  • Structural break: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, debt blowup, permanent business disruption (e.g., a disruptive competitor who has permanently taken market share).
  • −50%+ in days with no floor: likely more to come.
  • Buyout/merger repricing: the stock is now pinned to a deal price, not its mean.

Keep setups where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an intact business, near a prior support level. VZ and T are borderline — include them with small size and defined risk only until there is evidence of stabilization.

⚠ Proxy / K-1 / Leverage Caveats
  • Equity proxies (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, XLE): track companies, not the commodity. They carry stock beta and can diverge from the underlying. A low GDX RSI does not mean gold is oversold.
  • K-1 tax forms (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL): these issue a Schedule K-1, which complicates tax filing. Prefer No-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where available.
  • Leveraged/futures-based ETFs (TBT, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ): volatility decay + contango/backwardation roll means they deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical only; prefer the unlevered fund for multi-week holds.
