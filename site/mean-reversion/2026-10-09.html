7Options Structures — How to Read

This legend explains the general method — how we map direction and implied volatility (IV) to defined-risk educational structures. The specific setups and reasons for each name are in Sections 2–3 above.

RSI (Relative Strength Index, 0–100) — momentum gauge. Above 70 = overbought (too hot, potential pullback). Below 30 = oversold (too washed out, potential bounce). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme.

Bollinger %B (20-period, 2 std dev) — price position within its statistical range. Above 1.0 = outside the upper band (overbought). Below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold).

Z-score vs 50-day mean — how many "standard deviations" (typical daily swings) price is above or below its 50-day average. A Z-score of ±2 or more is statistically stretched.

Implied Volatility (IV) — the options market's expectation of future price swings. High IV = options are expensive = selling premium (spreads) is more attractive. Low IV = options are cheap = buying options is more attractive.

OVERSOLD + HIGH IV → Bull call spread · Put credit spread · Cash-secured put
OVERSOLD + LOW IV → Long call · Call debit spread
OVERBOUGHT + HIGH IV → Call credit spread · Bear put spread
OVERBOUGHT + LOW IV → Long put · Put debit spread

All structures above are defined-risk — your maximum loss is capped at what you paid (debit spreads) or the width of the spread minus the credit received (credit spreads). Never sell naked options until you have extensive experience. Paper-trade first, always.

Composite stretch scoring used in this report: a name scoring extreme on 3+ measures (RSI + distance from moving average + StochRSI or Williams %R) is ranked higher than one extreme only on RSI. Quality of the reversion thesis (intact business, event-driven excess, near known support) is weighted equally with the technical score.

8How to Read This · Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter: A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient for a reversion trade. We apply a hard ruin filter before any name makes this list.
  • Structural breaks are rejected: Fraud allegations, going-concern doubt, dividend cuts, failed trials, bankruptcy filings, debt blowups — these are falling knives, not reversion setups. None of the names in this report have known structural breaks as of this run.
  • Catastrophic drops are rejected: A name down −50%+ in days with no stabilization is likely repricing permanently, not bouncing. We only include names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business or asset class.
  • Buyout/merger pins are rejected: A stock pinned to a deal price isn't reverting to a technical mean — it's converging on a legal outcome. Not in this report.
  • INTC (Intel) warning: Intel is included but flagged as higher risk due to ongoing structural headwinds (server CPU market-share loss, foundry losses). It passes the ruin filter but carries more fundamental risk than SOXX/MU/AMD.
Special handling reminders (ETFs):
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Trade them ONLY when RSI is stretched UP (spike fade). NEVER buy them on low RSI — structural roll decay grinds them lower forever. Today's VXX is not stretched; no setup exists.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TBT, UCO, BOIL, SOXL, TQQQ): Volatility decay + futures roll mean they deviate from any long-run price mean. Use only for short-term tactical trades (days, not weeks).
  • K-1 Tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time, which can create administrative complexity. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where possible for similar commodity exposure.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XLE, XOP, SOXX, SMH, COPX, URA): These track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta (broader market risk) and can diverge significantly from spot prices during equity risk-off events.
