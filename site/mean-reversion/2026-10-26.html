8Options Structures Legend

This section explains what each structure is — so you understand the tools before verifying and paper-trading them. Never trade a structure you haven't practiced first.

RSI (14-day)
A 0–100 momentum gauge comparing recent gains to recent losses. Below 30 = selling may be overdone (oversold). Above 70 = buying may be extended (overbought). Not a buy/sell signal on its own.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Tells you where price sits relative to its 20-day band. Below 0 = price is outside the lower band (oversold zone). Above 1 = above the upper band (overbought zone).
Z-Score (vs 50d mean)
How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. Beyond ±2 is statistically unusual and often reverts. It doesn't predict when — just that the stretch is extreme.
IV (Implied Volatility)
How expensive options are. High IV = options cost more. Low IV = options cost less. IV is neither good nor bad — it changes which structure makes sense.
Bull Call Spread
Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Defined max gain and max loss. Good when you expect a rebound but want to cap the cost of entering during high IV.
Put Credit Spread
Sell a put at a higher strike, buy a put at a lower strike for protection. Collect premium upfront. Profits if the stock stays above your short put. Works well with oversold + high IV.
Bear Call Spread
Sell a call at a lower strike, buy a call at a higher strike. Collect premium upfront. Profits if the stock stays below your short call. Good for overbought + high IV situations.
Bear Put Spread
Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. Pay a net debit. Best when IV is low (cheaper to buy puts). Good for overbought + low IV situations (like XLP).
Direction + IV Matrix
Oversold + High IV → Put credit spread or bull call spread.
Oversold + Low IV → Long call or bull call spread (calls are cheaper).
Overbought + High IV → Bear call spread.
Overbought + Low IV → Bear put spread.
Defined Risk First
Every structure above is defined-risk: you know your maximum loss before entering. Never sell naked options (unlimited risk). Always paper-trade any structure before using real money.

9How to Read This Report & Guardrails

The most important filter: Reversion vs. Ruin. Not every oversold stock bounces. Some are oversold because they are broken. This scan explicitly removes:
  • Structural breaks — spin-offs (CTVA/Vylor), fraud, bankruptcy, going-concern doubt, accounting restatements. These don't revert to the old mean; they trade at a new, lower one.
  • Deal-pinned stocks — acquired companies (BMBL at $6.05 deal price) are pinned to a deal price, not a technical mean.
  • Falling knives — stocks down 50%+ in days with no stabilization and continued deteriorating fundamentals. An oversold RSI alone is not a buy signal.
A reversion candidate has: (1) an intact business; (2) the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess; (3) a prior support/reference level to revert toward; (4) 3+ stretched measures, not RSI alone.
Special rules for dangerous instruments:
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Fade spikes only — never buy as "oversold." Structural decay grinds them to zero over time.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX): They grind up and crater on spikes. Don't fade their uptrend as mean reversion.
  • Leveraged ETFs (SOXL, TQQQ, UCO, BOIL, 2–3× anything): Volatility decay and roll costs destroy value over time. Use only for very short-term tactical trades. For multi-week reversion, use the unlevered ETF or a direct stock.
  • K-1 Tax Flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, and certain CurrencyShares ETFs issue Schedule K-1 tax forms — more complex than 1099. Confirm before trading in a taxable account. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB for commodity exposure without K-1.
  • Proxy vs. Spot: GDX/GDXJ track mining companies, not gold spot. URA/URNM track uranium companies. XOP/OIH track oil producers. These carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity price.
