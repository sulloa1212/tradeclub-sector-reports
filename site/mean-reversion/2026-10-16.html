HOW TO USE THE STRUCTURES

7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — how different technical conditions map to educational option structures. The specific setups appear in the tables above. Always paper-trade any structure before risking real money, and always confirm data in your brokerage first.

RSI (0–100 overbought/oversold gauge) A 14-day momentum oscillator. Below 30 = historically oversold (selling may be overdone); above 70 = historically overbought (buying may be extended). An extreme reading is one input, not a trade signal by itself.
Bollinger %B (band position gauge) 0 = price at lower band, 1 = at upper band. >1 = price above the upper band (stretched up); <0 = below the lower band (stretched down). Works best combined with RSI.
Z-score (standard-deviation gauge) How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day mean. A Z-score of ±2 is statistically unusual; ±3 is rare. Higher Z-score = more stretched.
Oversold + High IV → Put Credit Spread Sell a put below current price, buy a lower put for protection. You collect premium upfront; maximum loss is capped. Best when IV is elevated (premium is fatter).
Oversold + High IV → Cash-Secured Put Sell a single put, hold enough cash to buy shares if assigned. Collects premium; effective if you'd be comfortable owning the stock at the strike price. Higher risk than a spread.
Oversold + Low IV → Bull Call Spread Buy a call closer to the money, sell a higher call. Pay a net debit; risk is limited to what you pay. Targets a price reversion toward the mean.
Overbought + High IV → Call Credit Spread Sell a call above current price, buy a higher call for protection. Collects premium; profits if price stays below the sold strike. Ideal when IV is elevated.
Overbought + Any IV → Bear Put Spread Buy a put closer to the money, sell a lower put. Pay a net debit; capped risk, targets a pullback. Use when IV is moderate and you want a directional bet.
Earnings Inside 30 Days ⚠ Any setup with earnings inside ~30 days carries binary event risk. The stock can gap 10–20%+ in either direction on earnings, easily overriding a technical reversion thesis. Reduce size, close before earnings, or avoid the setup entirely.
FILTERS & SAFETY RULES

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

These rules govern how this scan separates a real reversion candidate from a "falling knife" that will keep going down — or a structural change that invalidates the entire thesis.

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter
  • Reject structural breaks: If the drop is caused by fraud, going-concern risk, bankruptcy, dividend cut, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, or a spin-off repricing — it is NOT a mean-reversion setup. CTVA is today's example: the 84% drop is a value transfer to Vylor, not a sentiment overshoot.
  • Reject buyout/merger pins: A stock pinned to a deal price is not reverting to a technical mean — it is reverting to a contract price. Not applicable today.
  • Reject enormous drops with no stabilization: A name down 50%+ in days with no floor is a falling knife. Even if RSI is at 5, the next support may be 30% lower. CTVA showed this dynamic.
  • Keep: sentiment/positioning excess over sound businesses. SOXX falling on AI debt fears while TSMC reports +69% net profit growth = sentiment likely overshooting. That's the sweet spot of this scan.
Special Handling — Volatility, Leveraged & Futures ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — upside ONLY: Trade them only when stretched UP (spike). Never buy them on a low RSI — structural contango decay grinds them lower regardless of RSI. A low RSI in VXX/UVXY is drift, not a reversion opportunity.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TBT, 2x/3x anything): Volatility decay + roll costs mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical reversion only (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
  • Futures-based ETFs with K-1 tax forms (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, CurrencyShares): May issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time. Consider K-1-free alternatives: PDBC or BCI for broad commodities, BNO for Brent crude.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, COPX, URA, MOO, SIL): These track companies, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta — a gold miner ETF can diverge significantly from spot gold if miners have hedging, debt, or operational issues. Always noted in the table above.
