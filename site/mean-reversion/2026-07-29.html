8Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic building blocks used above — how each structure works and when it fits the direction + IV combination. Results (which names use which) are in the tables above.

📈 Oversold + High IV → Sell Premium

Cash-secured put: Sell a put below current price; collect premium. Obligated to buy shares if price falls to strike. Works when IV is high (bigger premium) and you're willing to own the stock.
Put credit spread: Sell a lower-strike put, buy an even lower-strike put for protection. Defined risk; max loss is the spread width minus credit received.

📈 Oversold + Low/Normal IV → Buy Premium

Bull call spread: Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. Limits cost compared to buying a call outright. Works when you expect an upside move but want to cap your cost.

📉 Overbought + High IV → Sell Premium

Call credit spread: Sell a call above current price, buy a higher-strike call for protection. You keep the credit if price stays below the short strike. Max loss = spread width minus credit.
Bear put spread: Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put lower. Defined cost; profits if price falls.

⚠ Binary Events → Iron Condor or Avoid

Iron condor: Sell both a call spread above the market and a put spread below. Collect premium from both sides; profitable if price stays in range. Used when IV is very high and direction is unclear (e.g., earnings).

📏 RSI Explained

RSI (14) — Relative Strength Index. A 0–100 scale measuring recent price momentum. Below 30 = "oversold" (selling may be exhausted). Above 70 = "overbought" (buying may be exhausted). Extreme readings: <20 or >80.

📏 Other Measures Explained

Bollinger %B: Where price sits within its normal range (0–1 band). >1 = above upper band (stretched up). <0 = below lower band (stretched down).
% vs SMA: How far price is above/below its moving average — the further, the more stretched.
Z-score: Standard deviations from average price (±2 = significant stretch).

9How to Read This Report / Guardrails

The most important section — read this before acting on anything above.

Reversion vs. Ruin Filter

A low RSI alone does not make a safe trade. Before entering any oversold setup, ask: is this a stretched-but-intact business, or a permanent collapse?

  • Reject: Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial (verify for biotech), bankruptcy, accounting restatement — these are not "oversold," they are re-rated lower forever.
  • Reject: Drops of 50%+ in days with no stabilization — likely more to come. Check news first.
  • Reject: Buyout/merger repricing — stock is now pinned to deal price, not its mean.
  • Keep: Sentiment/positioning excess over a sound business near prior support — that is a genuine reversion candidate.

Special ETF Rules

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Trade only when stretched up (spike fade). Never buy as "oversold" — contango roll decay grinds them lower permanently.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.): Short-term tactical only. Volatility decay means they drift from any long-run mean. Use unlevered or equity proxies for multi-week holds.
  • K-1 Tax flag (USO, UNG, UCO, DBC, UGA): These issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where available.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, OIH, URA): These track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying spot price significantly.
