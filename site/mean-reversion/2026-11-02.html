7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic methodology — what each structure is and why it fits. The specific setups and their "why this read" reasons are in the tables above.

Bull Call Spread (oversold + any IV) Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call in the same expiry. Caps cost; defines max loss. Use when you expect a bounce toward a target (e.g., the 50d SMA) but don't want unlimited upside risk/cost.
Put Credit Spread / Cash-Secured Put (oversold + HIGH IV) Sell a put (or put spread) below the current price, collecting premium. High IV means you collect more. Max loss is defined (spread width minus premium). Best when IV is elevated and you want to sell fear.
Call Credit Spread (overbought + HIGH IV) Sell a call spread above the current price. Collect premium; profit if the stock stays below the short strike. High IV environment makes the credit richer. Used on XLE, XOP, USO overbought setups.
Bear Put Spread (overbought + any IV) Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. Pays if the stock falls toward target. Costs a net debit; max loss = debit paid. Best when IV is lower or you want directional exposure with defined risk.
RSI (14-day, 0–100) Below 30 = oversold (sellers may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buyers may be exhausted). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme. A signal, not a guarantee — confirm with other measures.
Bollinger %B (20,2) Measures where price sits within the Bollinger Bands. Below 0 = price outside the lower band (oversold extreme); above 1 = price above the upper band (overbought extreme). Confirms RSI readings.
Z-Score (vs 50d mean) How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. Z-score of ±2 or more = statistically stretched (roughly the outer 5% of normal history). Ranked alongside RSI and %B for composite scoring.
IV Note (High / Elevated / Low) High IV: Option premiums are expensive — favor selling premium (credit spreads, cash-secured puts). Low IV: Options are cheap — favor buying premium (long calls/puts, debit spreads). IV note is directional for structure choice, not a prediction of future moves.

8How to Read / Guardrails

The reversion-vs-ruin filter — the most important concept in this report: Not every low RSI is a buy. Reject a setup when the move is driven by a structural break (fraud, bankruptcy, failed drug, going-concern, or — as with LITE today — a merger/buyout that pins price to a deal, not a mean). Keep setups where the move looks like positioning/sentiment excess over an otherwise sound business near a prior support level.
🔴 Falling Knife Rule
If a stock is down 50%+ in days with no stabilization and a structural catalyst (fraud, bankruptcy, deal gone wrong), it is not a reversion candidate. It is still falling.
⚠️ Buyout / Merger Pin
When a stock is acquired, its price is pinned to the deal. It no longer reverts to its own technical mean. LITE is today's example — rejected from all tables.
📋 K-1 Tax Warning
USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, DBC issue a Schedule K-1 (complicated tax form). Prefer no-K-1 alternatives: SCO (bearish oil), PDBC (broad commodities), KOLD (bearish nat-gas).
⚡ Leveraged / Futures ETF Warning
UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, VXX, UVXY — all suffer from volatility decay and/or contango roll. For holds beyond 1–5 days, prefer the unlevered fund or the equity-sector proxy.
🔗 Proxy vs Spot Warning
GDX, GDXJ, XOP, XLE, COPX, URA, SIL track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying commodity price.
🚫 Never Naked-Short Vol ETPs
VXX and UVXY can spike 30–50%+ in a single session on bad news. A naked short position can lose multiples of your premium. Defined-risk bear put spreads only — always.
