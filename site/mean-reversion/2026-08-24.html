7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic how-to reference — what each structure means and when it fits. Results (IV levels, actual setups) are in the tables above.

Direction + IV Preferred Structure Why Key Risk
Oversold + High IV Cash-Secured Put · Put Credit Spread · Bull Call Spread High IV inflates option premiums — selling premium (put credit spread) collects that inflated value. A cash-secured put is similar but requires cash to buy shares if exercised. Bull call spread limits cost if you want to buy. Stock keeps falling past your short strike — you own it at a loss on a CSP, or lose max on a debit spread.
Oversold + Low IV Long Call · Bull Call Spread Low IV makes buying options cheaper. A long call captures upside if the reversion is strong; a spread caps cost and risk. IV can expand when the move happens — "buying cheap premium" can still expire worthless if timing is off.
Overbought + High IV Call Credit Spread · Bear Put Spread High IV on an extended move = expensive calls you can sell. A call credit spread profits if the stock pulls back or goes sideways; a bear put spread is a defined-risk debit trade targeting the pullback. The trend continues; short call is tested. A credit spread has a defined max loss (the width minus premium collected).
Overbought + Low IV Long Put · Bear Put Spread Low IV makes buying puts cheaper. A bear put spread caps cost and risk while targeting a pullback. Strong momentum can persist even as IV is low — timing is everything.
Event Binary (earnings/Fed) Wait; or use a very narrow defined-risk structure after the event clears IV is always inflated into binaries. Buying premium is expensive; selling it exposes you to a gap. The cleanest reversion setup begins after the event reprices the move. An earnings gap in the wrong direction can immediately invalidate a technical setup that looked clean the day before.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

The reversion framework has hard filters. Before acting on any setup, run this checklist:

  • Reversion vs. Ruin: A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Reject names driven by fraud, going-concern doubt, bankruptcy, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, or buyout repricing. Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an intact business — ideally near prior support.
  • Binary event check: If an earnings report or major macro event (Fed, PCE) is within ~30 days, the technical setup is subordinate to that event. Wait for the binary to clear, or use a very narrow defined-risk structure. This week: NVDA earnings (Wed Aug 26), PCE (Wed Aug 26), Jackson Hole/Warsh (Fri Aug 28).
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX, GDXJ, URA, XOP, XLE, COPX, SIL, MOO track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying commodity price — especially if the broad market sells off. Don't treat them as pure commodity plays.
  • K-1 tax warning: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and certain CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1, which complicates taxes. Prefer No-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB, XOP/XLE for energy equity) where the exposure allows.
  • Leveraged & futures ETFs — short-term only: TBT, TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, UVXY — volatility decay + contango/backwardation roll make these deviate from any long-run mean. Use for very short-term tactical reversion only; for multi-week holds, prefer unlevered funds or equity-sector proxies.
  • Vol spike rule (long-vol ETPs): VXX, UVXY, VIXY — only trade when spiked UP (RSI >70 on a volatility event). Never buy on a low RSI — structural roll decay causes these to drift lower over time. A low VXX RSI is drift, not a setup.
  • Verify every data point: All RSI readings, prices, % vs. moving averages, and dates in this report are model-generated snapshots from automated sources. They can be wrong. Confirm against your own brokerage charting before acting.
  • Paper trade first: Practice any new structure in a simulated account before risking real money. Options involve the risk of losing your entire premium; defined-risk structures (spreads) cap your max loss but you can still lose 100% of what you put in.
  • Size small — never bet the farm on a reversion: Stretched can get more stretched. Position size relative to account equity; set a max loss before entering; know your exit before you enter.
