7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method behind the structures named in the tables above. The body already shows which structure fits each name — this section explains what each structure is and why it maps to a given scenario.

Bull Put Credit Spread

Sell a put at strike A, buy a put at a lower strike B. You collect a credit upfront; max loss is the difference between strikes minus the credit. Use when: oversold + IV elevated. You profit if the stock stays above strike A at expiration. Best when you believe selling is exhausted but don't want unlimited downside.

Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at strike A, sell a call at a higher strike B. You pay a debit upfront; max loss is that debit. Use when: oversold + IV is low (buying options is cheaper). You profit if the stock rallies above strike A toward B by expiration.

Bear Call Credit Spread

Sell a call at strike A, buy a call at a higher strike B. You collect a credit upfront; max loss is the difference between strikes minus the credit. Use when: overbought + IV elevated. You profit if the stock stays below strike A at expiration.

Bear Put Spread

Buy a put at strike A, sell a put at a lower strike B. You pay a debit; max loss is that debit. Use when: overbought + IV is low. You profit if the stock falls below strike A toward B by expiration.

Cash-Secured Put

Sell a put while holding enough cash to buy 100 shares if assigned. You collect a premium; if the stock falls below the strike, you buy shares at an effective lower price. Use when: oversold + IV elevated + you'd actually want to own the stock. More capital-intensive than a spread.

RSI / %B / Z-Score — What They Measure
  • RSI (0–100): Below 30 = oversold (sellers may be exhausted); above 70 = overbought (buyers may be exhausted). Below 20 or above 80 = extreme.
  • Bollinger %B (0–1): Below 0 = price outside the lower band (stretched down); above 1 = above upper band (stretched up). Used here as a confirming measure.
  • Z-score: How many standard deviations the current price is from its 50-day average. ±2 is stretched; ±3 is extreme.
  • IV (Implied Volatility): High IV → options are expensive → prefer selling premium (credit spreads). Low IV → options are cheap → prefer buying spreads (debit spreads).

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient for a reversion trade. Reject any name that has:

  • A structural break: fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, or accounting restatement.
  • A catastrophic drop (−50%+ in days) with no stabilization — that's a falling knife, not a mean-reversion setup.
  • A buyout/merger pin — the stock is pinned to a deal price (see MKTX above). This is explicitly not a reversion trade.

Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment or positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, ideally near a prior support level.

Special Handling — Leveraged, Futures-Based & Vol ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade only when stretched UP — fade the spike as it fades. NEVER buy them as "oversold" mean-reversion setups. Contango/roll decay grinds them lower permanently.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX): Mirror of above — they grind up and crater on spikes. Their reversion setup is recovery after a volatility crash, not a short of low-vol levels.
  • Leveraged / futures ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, UNG, TQQQ, TBT, 2x/3x anything): Use for short-term tactical plays only. Do not hold multi-week — volatility decay and roll costs devour returns.
  • K-1 tax flag: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER issue a Schedule K-1. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) for commodity exposure in taxable accounts.
  • Proxy vs. spot: GDX/GDXJ track gold miners (equity beta), not gold itself. XLE/XOP track energy companies, not crude oil. They can diverge significantly from the underlying commodity — especially during equity market stress.
