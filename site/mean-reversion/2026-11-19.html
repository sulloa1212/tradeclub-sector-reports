8Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what each structure is and when to use it. The specific setups (tickers, strikes, prices) are in the tables above and must be verified before acting.

Bull Call Spread (Oversold + Any IV)
Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call in the same expiry. Defined max risk (the net debit paid); defined max profit (spread width minus debit). Best for: oversold names where you expect a bounce but want capped loss.
Put Credit Spread (Oversold + High IV)
Sell a put, buy a lower-strike put. Collect a net credit; max risk is the spread width minus credit. Best for: oversold names with elevated IV where you're being paid to hold downside risk you're comfortable with.
Bear Put Spread (Overbought + Any IV)
Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. Defined risk; profits if the stock falls toward or below the lower strike. Best for: overbought names where you expect a pullback.
Call Credit Spread (Overbought + High IV)
Sell a call, buy a higher-strike call. Collect a net credit; max risk is the spread width minus credit. Best for: overbought names with high IV — you get paid to bet the stock stops rising.

Indicator quick reference:

  • RSI (Relative Strength Index, 0–100): Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought. Extreme: below 20 or above 80.
  • Bollinger %B (position within Bollinger Bands): Below 0 = price below lower band (oversold extreme); above 1 = price above upper band (overbought extreme).
  • Z-score: How many standard deviations (SDs) the price is from its 50-day average. ±2 SDs = statistically stretched.
  • Stochastic Oscillator: Below 20 = oversold; above 80 = overbought. Slower to signal than RSI.
  • IV (Implied Volatility): Options market's expectation of future price moves. High IV = expensive options; favor selling structures. Low IV = cheap options; favor buying structures.
  • ADX (trend strength, 0–100): Above 25 = trending. High ADX + oversold RSI = "oversold in a trend" — handle with care.

Always paper-trade first. Never risk money you can't afford to lose. Options can expire worthless — 100% loss of premium paid.

9How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Before acting on anything in this report, read these guardrails. They explain how to tell a good reversion setup from a trap.

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter:

An oversold RSI is necessary but not sufficient. These situations look oversold but are likely traps:

  • Structural breaks: Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement, CEO resignation + regulatory probe (flag on NVDA this week). The company may never recover its old "mean."
  • Enormous drops with no stabilization: −50%+ in days with no support (see CTVA — 22% single-day earnings crash — this is NOT in our oversold tables and should be verified as a ruin scenario before touching it).
  • Buyout/merger repricing: Stock pins to deal price, not its old mean.
  • Structural re-rating: A business model that no longer works (e.g., a disrupted retailer).

Keep names where: the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, ideally near a prior support level, and no structural break has occurred.

Special handling — volatility, leveraged & futures-based ETFs:

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade only when stretched UP (spike). Never treat a low RSI as a buy signal — structural contango decay grinds them lower permanently.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, TQQQ, etc.): Volatility decay and roll costs mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical reversion plays.
  • Futures-based ETFs (USO, BITO, UNG, UGA, DBC): These may issue a Schedule K-1 (tax form). Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, IBIT, BITO for crypto) where possible. Check with your broker.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, MOO, URA): These track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices.
  • Crypto ETFs (IBIT, BITO, ETHA): High-beta. Treat like a leveraged position. Defined-risk structures are mandatory.
