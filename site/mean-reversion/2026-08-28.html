8Options Structures Legend

This section explains the terms and logic used in the tables above — for beginners reading their first options report.

RSI (14-day)

A 0–100 overbought/oversold gauge. Below 30 = selling may be exhausted (oversold). Above 70 = buying may be exhausted (overbought). Extreme: below 20 / above 80.

Bollinger %B (20,2)

Shows where price sits inside its normal price "band." Below 0 = price is below the lower band (oversold extreme). Above 1 = above the upper band (overbought extreme).

Z-Score

How many standard deviations (how many "normal steps") price is from its 50-day average. Above ±2.0 is considered statistically stretched.

% from 50d / 200d SMA

How far the current price is above or below its 50-day or 200-day simple moving average (the "moving mean"). The further below, the more stretched to the downside.

IV (Implied Volatility)

Options market's expectation of future price swings, expressed as a percentage. High IV = options are expensive. Low IV = options are cheap.

Bull Put Spread

Sell a put option at a higher strike, buy one at a lower strike — same expiry. Profits if price stays above the short strike. Defined max risk = the width of the spread minus premium collected.

Bear Call Spread

Sell a call option at a lower strike, buy one at a higher strike — same expiry. Profits if price stays below the short strike. Defined max risk = spread width minus premium collected.

Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike — same expiry. Profits if price rises. Max risk = net premium paid.

Bear Put Spread

Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike — same expiry. Profits if price falls. Max risk = net premium paid.

Cash-Secured Put (CSP)

Sell a put option and hold cash equal to the obligation to buy shares. Profits if price stays above the strike. Risk = assigned ownership at below-market price.

High IV → Sell Structures

When IV is high, options premiums are inflated. Selling credit spreads (bull put or bear call) collects that rich premium. The edge: you profit even if price doesn't move much.

Low IV → Buy Structures

When IV is low, options are cheap to buy. Long call/put spreads cost less and offer better risk/reward for directional plays.

Direction + IV → Structure mapping (summary):
  • Oversold + High IV: Cash-secured put · Bull put spread · Bull call spread (premium selling first)
  • Oversold + Low IV: Long call · Bull call spread (buying structure cheaper)
  • Overbought + High IV: Bear call spread · Call credit spread · Bear put spread
  • Overbought + Low IV: Long put · Bear put spread

Always defined-risk first. Paper trade before risking real money. Confirm IV rank in your brokerage before choosing a structure — IV data here is a snapshot estimate only.

9How to Read / Guardrails

Before using any setup above, run through these five filters — they're what separate a reversion trade from a losing bet on a broken name.

🔍 Reversion vs. Ruin filter
RSI below 30 is necessary but not sufficient. Reject if: structural break (fraud, going-concern, failed drug trial, accounting restatement, bankruptcy), drop is >50% in days with no stabilization, or it's a buyout repricing (price is now pinned to deal price, not its mean). Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business — ideally near prior support.
⚠ K-1 Tax Flag
USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, KOLD, and most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form (complex partnership tax filing). If tax simplicity matters, use "No K-1" alternatives: PDBC or BCI for broad commodities, XLE/XOP equity proxies for oil. Flag applies to every K-1 name in this report.
🔁 Leveraged & Futures ETF Rule
2x/3x products (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, UVXY, UVIX) suffer volatility decay and leverage reset — they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical (1–5 days max) reversion; prefer the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy for multi-week holds.
🏭 Proxy vs. Spot Reminder
GDX, GDXJ, XOP, OIH, COPX, URA, SIL, MOO track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta, management risk, and leverage — they can diverge significantly from spot prices. A gold rally doesn't guarantee GDX rallies equally; an oil drop doesn't mean XOP falls exactly the same.
📅 Earnings Date Landmine
