7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what each structure is and why it fits a given scenario. The specific setups (ticker, direction, IV level) are in the tables above. Always paper-trade before risking real money.

Direction + IV → Preferred Defined-Risk Structure
Oversold + High IV
Put credit spread (sell a lower put, buy an even-lower put as your "safety net") — you collect premium and profit if the stock stays above your short strike. Also: Cash-secured put (sell a put; if assigned, you own the stock at a lower price). Both benefit from elevated IV — you're getting paid more to sell the premium.
Oversold + Low IV
Bull call spread (buy a call, sell a higher-strike call to offset the cost) — you pay a smaller debit; profit if the stock rises toward the short strike. Preferred when IV is low so you're not overpaying for the long leg.
Overbought + High IV
Call credit spread (sell a higher call, buy an even-higher call) — collect premium, profit if the stock stays below your short strike. Also: Bear put spread (buy a put, sell a lower put; pay a debit; profit on downside). Call credit spreads are most premium-efficient when IV is high.
Overbought + Low IV
Bear put spread — debit spread; cheaper when IV is low.
RSI (14-day)
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausted). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be crowded). Extreme: below 20 or above 80. Use as one input, never alone.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Band. Below 0 = price below the lower band (stretched down). Above 1 = price above the upper band (stretched up). Confirm RSI signals.
Z-score (vs 50d mean)
How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score below −2 or above +2 = statistically unusual — 95% of the time it isn't this far out. Doesn't mean it must revert; it means the bet has historical edge.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The market's forward expectation of how much a stock will move. High IV = expensive options (better to sell premium). Low IV = cheap options (better to buy premium). Compare IV to its own historical range (IV Rank or IV Percentile).

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

The most important filter: Reversion vs. Ruin

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Every oversold name on this list was tested against these "ruin" signals — if any fire, the name is dropped or flagged as a trap:

  • Structural break: fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement → TRAP — AVOID
  • Catastrophic drop: −50%+ in days with no stabilization → likely more to come.
  • Buyout/merger pin: stock anchored to deal price, not its mean → no reversion thesis.

Names that passed: sound businesses hit by sentiment, positioning, or macro events — oversold but not broken. Even so, always check your own sources for any news since this report was generated.

Special rules for ETF types — know what you own
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade ONLY when stretched UP on a vol spike. Never buy because RSI is low — contango decay grinds them down indefinitely. Today: no spike → no setup.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX): They grind higher in contango; their reversion setup is the recovery after a vol crash. Handle with care — they can be zeroed on a spike.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TBT, BOIL, UCO, BRZU, TQQQ, etc.): Volatility decay + roll costs mean they deviate from any stable price mean. Use for short-term tactical fades only; multi-week holds erode value. Prefer the unlevered ETF for anything longer than 1–2 weeks.
  • K-1 tax flag ⚠: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — more complexity at tax filing. Where exposure allows, prefer no-K-1 alternatives (BNO for Brent, PDBC for broad commodities).
  • Proxy ETFs (proxy): GDX (gold miners), GDX/GDXJ, XOP, OIH, URA track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying in either direction.
Verify before every trade — these are the biggest hallucination risks in AI reports:
  • Earnings dates — AI models frequently get these wrong. Always check your brokerage or an earnings calendar.
  • Live RSI / prices — this report uses screener estimates; actual values move intraday.
  • Options liquidity — open interest and bid/ask spreads change daily; always check the actual chain.
  • ETF chain status — some tickers in the universe have gone thin or been delisted. Verify before placing orders.
