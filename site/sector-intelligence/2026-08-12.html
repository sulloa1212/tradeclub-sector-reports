7How to Read This Report

A quick legend — the methodology behind the numbers, so you can judge the reasoning yourself.

Direction Score (−100 to +100)
A composite of five inputs: ETF trend/price structure (30%), relative strength vs. SPY 1–3 months (25%), macro tailwind/headwind (20%), news & catalyst flow (15%), and momentum/breadth like RSI/MACD (10%). +100 = max bullish; −100 = max bearish; 0 = neutral. Not a buy/sell signal — it measures the sector's directional lean.
Conviction (High / Medium / Low)
How confident the model is in the direction score based on agreement across all five inputs. High = all inputs pointing same way. Low = mixed or conflicting inputs. Low conviction + extreme score = treat with extra skepticism.
Swing-Conviction Score (0–100)
A stock-level score measuring how likely a stock is to move STRONGEST in its sector's direction over the 3-day–6-week swing horizon. Inputs: technical setup quality (35%), relative strength (25%), catalyst & news (20%), move-strength potential/beta (20%). Higher = stronger expected move — NOT automatically "better" or safer.
Fundamental Health Score (0–100)
Measures absolute business quality independent of the chart: revenue growth (20%), EPS growth & beat history (20%), margins (15%), FCF & balance sheet (15%), analyst revisions (10%), guidance/backlog (10%), dividends/capital returns (10%). For non-payers, the 10% dividend weight is reallocated to growth & FCF. ≥65 = green. 45–64 = amber. <45 = red.
⛽ Fuel Tag
For longs: Health ≥65 → ⛽ ADDS FUEL (strong business supports the trade). 45–64 → NEUTRAL. <45 → ⚠ FIGHTS TREND (weak business is a risk). For short/avoid calls: Health ≤40 → ⛽ ADDS FUEL (weak company is a riskier one to hold). 41–60 → NEUTRAL. >60 → ⚠ FIGHTS TREND (a fundamentally strong company is a riskier short).
⚠ Earnings-in-Window Flag
Any stock with an earnings date inside the 3-day–6-week swing window gets an amber ⚠ warning. Earnings = binary event risk. The stock can gap 10–30% in either direction regardless of your technical read. Size smaller or wait for the print if you want to reduce risk. Always verify earnings dates independently before trading.
