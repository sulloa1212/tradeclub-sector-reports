7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the method behind the structures suggested in the tables above. All structures are educational — confirm your brokerage supports them and always paper-trade first.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
A 0–100 gauge of recent price momentum. Below 30 = "oversold" (selling may be stretched); above 70 = "overbought" (buying may be stretched). Extreme readings: <20 or >80. RSI alone is not sufficient — the report requires 3+ confirmations.
Bollinger %B (a band-distance gauge)
Measures where price sits within its 20-day statistical band. %B < 0 = below the lower band (stretched low). %B > 1 = above the upper band (stretched high). Confirms RSI signals.
Z-Score (standard deviations from mean)
How many standard deviations (units of normal variation) a price is from its 50-day average. ±2 = statistically unusual. ±3 = rare extreme.
IV (Implied Volatility — options pricing)
How expensive options are right now, based on market pricing. High IV = options are costly → favors selling premium (collect the high price). Low IV = options are cheap → favors buying options (pay a low price).
Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
Defined-risk structures: Cash-secured put (CSP), Put credit spread (bull put spread), Bull call spread. All cap your maximum loss. CSP: sell a put below the stock price, collect premium, obligated to buy shares at the strike. Credit spread: sell one option, buy another farther out — limits both profit and loss.
Oversold + Low IV → Option Buying
Defined-risk structures: Long call or bull call spread. You pay a premium; maximum loss is the premium paid. Use when IV is low because options are cheap.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling
Defined-risk structures: Call credit spread (bear call spread), Iron condor. Sell a call above the stock, buy a higher call. Collect premium if the stock stays below the short strike.
Overbought + Low IV → Option Buying
Defined-risk structures: Bear put spread or long put. Pay premium; maximum loss is what you paid. Use when IV is low.
Around Earnings (binary events)
Always size down or avoid directional bets before an earnings report. An iron condor (selling both a call spread and a put spread simultaneously) can capture elevated IV on both sides — but earnings gaps can exceed any condor's wings. Paper trade first.
Tier A / B / C — Options Liquidity
Tier A: deep, tight spreads, high open interest (SPY, QQQ, GLD, TLT, SMH, IBIT, SOXX). Tier B: tradable but verify before entering. Tier C: thin chains — always check bid/ask spread before placing any order; wide spreads eat your edge.

8How to Read This Report & Guardrails

Reversion vs Ruin — the most important filter
A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. This report rejects "falling knives" — names where the move reflects a permanent regime change, not positioning excess. We flag or exclude: fraud/going-concern doubt, bankruptcy risk, failed drug trials, acquisition repricing, and drops of 50%+ with no stabilization. What we keep: names where the sell-off looks like sentiment/crowding over an otherwise intact business, ideally near a prior support level.
⚠ Leveraged ETF caution
Leveraged and 2x/3x funds (SOXL, TQQQ, TBT, UCO, BOIL) suffer volatility decay — daily rebalancing erodes value over time, especially in choppy markets. Use only for short-term tactical trades (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, use the unleveraged fund or equity-sector proxy instead.
⚠ Futures-based ETF decay
USO, UNG, BITO, and similar funds hold futures contracts (agreements to buy at a future date). When the market is in contango (futures priced above spot), rolling those contracts costs money — the fund drifts down even if the commodity stays flat. This is structural decay, not a buy signal.
⚠ Proxy ≠ Spot PROXY
GDX, GDXJ, XOP, COPX, URA, SIL, MOO track companies (miners, drillers, etc.), not the commodity directly. They carry equity beta — they can rise or fall based on earnings, management, and equity-market sentiment independent of the underlying commodity.
⚠ K-1 tax flag K-1
Some commodity ETFs (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, some CurrencyShares) issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time instead of a 1099-DIV. This complicates tax filing and can trigger unexpected income recognition. Where possible, prefer no-K-1 alternatives: PDBC (vs DBC), COMB, BCI (vs commodity basket).
⚠ Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — special rule
These funds grind lower over time due to VIX-futures contango roll decay. A low RSI on VXX is structural drift, not a reversion buy signal. The only clean mean-reversion trade in long-vol ETPs is fading a spike — selling (or buying puts on) VXX/UVXY after a sharp fear-driven surge, as vol reliably mean-reverts downward after spikes.
