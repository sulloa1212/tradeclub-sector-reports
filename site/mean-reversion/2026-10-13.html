7Options Structures Legend

This section explains which options structure fits which situation. The goal is always defined risk first — never more loss than you planned for when you opened the trade.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
Put credit spread (sell lower put, buy even lower put) or cash-secured put. You collect premium; if the stock holds above your short strike, you keep it. Risk is capped by the bought put below.
Oversold + Low IV → Option Buying
Bull call spread (buy a call, sell a higher call). Cheaper when IV is low; profit if the stock bounces toward your target. Max loss = debit paid.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling
Call credit spread (sell a call, buy a higher call). Collect premium if the stock stays below your short strike. Also consider bear put spread for a debit bearish play.
Overbought + Low IV → Option Buying
Bear put spread (buy a put, sell a lower put). Pays off if the stock falls toward mean; max loss is the debit paid.
RSI (14)
A 0–100 momentum gauge comparing recent gains to recent losses. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausted). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended). Extreme readings: <20 / >80.
Bollinger %B (20,2)
Where price sits relative to its Bollinger Bands. Below 0 = outside the lower band (very oversold). Above 1 = outside the upper band (very overbought). Confirms RSI readings.
Z-Score vs 50d Mean
How many standard deviations (a measure of typical daily swing size) the price is from its 50-day average. A Z-score of ±2 or more is statistically stretched.
IV (Implied Volatility)
The options market's expectation of future price swings, expressed as an annual % — like a fear gauge baked into option prices. High IV = options are expensive (favor selling). Low IV = options are cheap (favor buying).

8How to Read / Guardrails

Before using any setup, run it through these filters. Most losing trades skip at least one of them.

The Reversion-vs-Ruin Filter (most important). A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Ask: why did it fall? If the answer is fraud, going-concern doubt, failed trial, bankruptcy, or accounting restatement — it is a falling knife. Walk away. A clean reversion candidate has: (1) an intact business, (2) a well-defined pre-shock "mean," and (3) a catalyst that looks like sentiment excess rather than a permanent re-rating.

Proxy & Equity-Beta Warning
ETFs marked proxy (GDX, GDXJ, GDX, XOP, URA, COPX, MOO) track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry stock-market beta and can diverge sharply from spot prices. Do not assume they will revert with the underlying commodity.
K-1 Tax Warning
ETFs marked K-1 (USO, UNG, UCO, DBC, BOIL) issue a Schedule K-1 instead of a 1099 at tax time. This creates administrative complexity and potential surprises. Where possible, prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB for broad commodity; BNO for Brent).
Leveraged / Futures Decay Warning
ETFs marked lev. (TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, BOIL, UNG) suffer volatility decay and/or futures contango roll costs. They are tactical short-term instruments only — never hold for multi-week mean-reversion plays. Use the unlevered equivalent for longer holds.
Vol ETPs — Special Rules
VXX, UVXY, VIXY: trade them only when stretched UP (spike fade). A low RSI on UVXY is structural decay, not a buy signal. SVXY/SVIX: grind up, crater on spikes — their reversion is recovery after a vol crash. Never fade their up-moves as mean reversion.
Dates Are the #1 Hallucination Risk
Every earnings date in this report marked "date unconfirmed — verify" should be cross-checked against your brokerage or a primary source before you trade. Never assume an earnings-free window without confirming it yourself.
Composite Stretch, Not RSI Alone
Names are strongest when extreme on 3+ measures (RSI + %B + Z-score + distance from 50d/200d). A single RSI reading is a starting point, not a signal. More confirmations = higher-conviction thesis.
