7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the educational structures mentioned in the tables — it's the methodology behind the labels, not a recommendation to trade any specific name. Always confirm IV and chain liquidity in your own brokerage, and paper-trade first.

RSI (14-day)

A 0–100 speed-of-price-change gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling pressure may be exhausted). Above 70 = overbought (buying pressure may be exhausted). Extreme readings: below 20 / above 80. RSI alone is not enough — always confirm with 2+ other signals.

Bollinger %B (20,2)

Shows where price sits within its own 20-day price "envelope." Below 0 = price is below the lower band (oversold). Above 1 = price is above the upper band (overbought). Combines with RSI to confirm extremes.

Z-Score vs 50-day mean

How many "standard deviations" (average daily moves) the price is away from its 50-day average. Z-score beyond ±2.0 is statistically unusual. Higher Z-score = more stretched. Used alongside RSI and %B for composite ranking.

Implied Volatility (IV)

What the options market is "pricing in" for future moves. High IV = expensive options (favor selling premium). Low IV = cheap options (favor buying). Mean-reversion + high IV is the ideal setup for premium-selling structures because you collect more credit.

Bull Put Spread (oversold + high IV)

Sell a higher-strike put, buy a lower-strike put. You collect a credit and profit if the stock stays above your short strike. Risk is capped at the spread width minus credit received. Best when you expect a floor, not necessarily a big rally.

Cash-Secured Put (oversold + high IV)

Sell a put and hold enough cash to buy the stock if assigned. You collect premium; your effective buy price is the strike minus the premium. Risk: being assigned shares if the stock falls further. Only use if you'd be comfortable owning the stock.

Bull Call Spread (oversold + low IV)

Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call. You pay a debit (limited cost) and profit if the stock rises above your long strike. Best when IV is low (options are cheap) and you want defined upside exposure.

Call Credit Spread (overbought + high IV)

Sell a lower-strike call, buy a higher-strike call. You collect a credit and profit if the stock stays below your short strike. Risk capped at spread width minus credit. Best for overbought + high-IV situations where you expect consolidation or pullback.

Bear Put Spread (overbought + low/med IV)

Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. You pay a debit and profit if the stock falls below your long put. Best when IV is moderate and you expect a meaningful pullback, not just a pause.

Vol Spike Fade (VXX/UVXY only)

When VXX or UVXY spike UP sharply on a fear event, they tend to revert quickly as the VIX mean-reverts. Fade = call credit spread or put spread after the first sign of peak. NEVER trade long-vol ETPs as an "oversold buy" — structural decay kills that trade.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Before acting on anything in this report, read these filters. They are the most important part of the whole document.

Reversion vs. Ruin: An oversold RSI does not mean a stock will bounce. Reject "falling knives": fraud, bankruptcy risk, going-concern doubt, failed drug trials, massive structural breaks. Keep names where the move looks like sentiment excess over a sound business. Ask: "Is the mean still there to revert to?"
3+ Signals Rule: A name extreme on RSI alone is borderline. A name extreme on RSI + %B + Z-score + consecutive down-days vs. ATR is a high-quality setup. Quality beats quantity — a single strong setup beats five RSI-only names.
Proxy vs. Spot: GDX, GDXJ, URA, XOP, COPX, MOO, SIL track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the spot price. A gold miner can fall even if gold rises.
K-1 Tax Warning: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER, and some CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form — complex tax treatment. Prefer K-1-free alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where the exposure allows. Confirm with your accountant.
Leveraged / Futures ETFs (short-term only): UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, TBT, TBF, TMF, TMV, and all 2x/3x products suffer from volatility decay and futures roll costs. They deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical reversion only — for multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
Long-Vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — Never buy on low RSI: Structural contango roll decay grinds these products lower over time. A low RSI is drift, not a setup. Only trade them when they spike UP on a volatility event — and then only to fade the spike back down.
Earnings Dates = #1 Hallucination Risk: Every earnings date in this report was sourced from live web search. Dates that could not be confirmed from a live source are marked "date unconfirmed — verify." Never trade through a binary event without checking the actual date in your brokerage.
Options Liquidity First: Before entering any trade, confirm the options chain has (1) weekly or monthly expirations, (2) open interest > 500 contracts at your strike, and (3) bid-ask spreads tight enough to fill at a fair price. Tier C ETFs in this report may have thin chains — verify before entering.
Defined Risk First, Always: Every structure recommended in this report is defined-risk (spreads, not naked positions). This means your maximum loss is known before you enter. Never sell naked calls or puts unless you have extensive experience and your broker permits it with the appropriate account level.
Paper-Trade First: If any of these setups are new to you, simulate them in a paper-trading account for at least 30 days before using real capital. Understanding how a position behaves through good and bad scenarios is essential before risking money.
