8How to Read This / Guardrails

Before acting on any setup above, run it through these four filters — they are the difference between a reversion trade and a trap.

1. Reversion vs. Ruin Filter (the most important check)

A low RSI alone is not a buy signal. The scan rejects "falling knives" and permanent re-ratings. Before considering any oversold name, ask: Is the move driven by sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise intact business — or by a structural break (fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, buyout pin, debt blowup)? Only the former qualifies. Every name on today's list passed this filter, but verify it hasn't changed since this report was generated.

2. Proxy vs. Spot Flag

GDX/GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX, MOO, SIL, EWY track companies in a sector — not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the commodity price. When you trade these, you are trading equities, not the metal or barrel directly.

3. K-1 Tax Flag

USO, UNG, UGA, UCO, BOIL, DBC, and most CurrencyShares ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB for broad commodities; XLE/XOP for energy equity exposure) where the thesis allows. Confirm with your tax adviser.

4. Leveraged / Futures ETF Warning

SOXL (3x chips), UCO/SCO (2x oil), BOIL/KOLD (2x nat-gas), TBT (-2x bonds), and all other leveraged products experience volatility decay — they do NOT track 2x or 3x the long-run return. Use them for short-term tactical reversion only (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, use the unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.

5. Earnings / Binary Events

Any setup with an earnings date inside ~30 days is flagged ⚠ in the tables. Earnings can override a technical setup completely. Check the date before you trade, and consider whether you want to hold through the binary event.
