7Options Structures Legend

This is the generic method — how direction and IV combine to suggest educational structures. The specific results (which ticker, which direction) are in the tables above.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling
Cash-secured put: Sell a put below the market, collect premium, agree to buy at that price. Risk = you own the stock if it falls further.
Put credit spread: Sell a put, buy a lower put. Caps both profit and loss. Defined risk.
Bull call spread: Buy a call, sell a higher call. Pays off if stock rises. Risk = premium paid only.
Oversold + Low IV → Premium Buying
Long call / call debit spread: Buy a call or buy a lower call + sell a higher call. Pays off on upside move. Risk = premium paid. Lower IV makes buying cheaper.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling
Call credit spread: Sell a call, buy a higher call. Collects premium if stock stays below your short strike. Defined risk.
Bear put spread: Buy a put, sell a lower put. Pays off on downside. Risk = net premium paid.
Key Technical Terms
RSI (14d): 0–100 momentum gauge — below 30 = oversold, above 70 = overbought, extreme below 20 / above 80.
Bollinger %B: Where price sits within its 20-day band — below 0 = outside lower band, above 1 = outside upper band.
Z-score: How many standard deviations price is from its 50d mean. Beyond ±2 is historically rare.
IV (implied volatility): The option market's expectation of future moves, priced into premiums — higher IV = more expensive options.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Before you use any name on this list, run it through these filters — they determine whether a low RSI is a setup or a trap.

Reversion vs. Ruin Filter — reject these even if RSI is at 10:
  • 🚫 Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy filing, accounting restatement
  • 🚫 Drop of −50%+ in days with no stabilization — likely structural, not sentiment
  • 🚫 Buyout/merger repricing — stock is pinned to a deal price, not its mean
  • 🚫 Fundamental earnings revision so severe the old "mean" is simply not valid anymore
  • ✅ Keep: moves that look like sentiment/positioning excess over a sound business, near prior support
Vol ETF Rules (VXX, UVXY)
Trade only when stretched UP — fade the spike as it reverts down. Never treat a low RSI as a buy. Roll decay grinds these lower permanently over time.
Leveraged / Futures ETFs (UCO, UNG, BOIL, TQQQ)
Volatility decay + contango means they deviate from any long-run mean. Short-term tactical only; for multi-week holds, prefer the unlevered fund or equity proxy.
K-1 Tax Flag (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL)
These ETFs issue a Schedule K-1 tax form — more complex tax filing. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB, SCO) where the exposure allows.
Proxy vs. Spot (GDX, XOP, URA, COPX)
These track companies, not the commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying. GDX ≠ gold; XOP ≠ oil prices.
