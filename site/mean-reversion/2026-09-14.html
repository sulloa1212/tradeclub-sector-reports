7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains how direction and IV level combine to suggest a structure. Every structure here is defined-risk — meaning your maximum loss is fixed before you enter. Always paper-trade first.

Oversold + High IV → Bull Put Spread Sell a put at/near the current price; buy a lower put as protection. You keep premium if the stock stays above the sold strike. Max loss = difference in strikes minus premium received.
Oversold + Low IV → Bull Call Spread Buy a call above current price; sell a higher call. You profit if the stock moves up. Max loss = premium paid. Low IV means options are cheaper to buy.
Overbought + High IV → Bear Call Spread Sell a call above current price; buy a higher call as protection. You keep premium if the stock stays below the sold strike. Max loss = difference in strikes minus premium received.
Overbought + Low IV → Bear Put Spread Buy a put below current price; sell a lower put. You profit if the stock drops. Max loss = premium paid.
Cash-Secured Put Sell a put and set aside enough cash to buy 100 shares if assigned. You keep the premium if the stock stays above the strike. Only suitable if you're willing to own the stock at the strike price.
RSI (14): 0–100 momentum gauge Below 30 = oversold (selling may be excessive). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be excessive). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme. Not a standalone signal — combine with other measures.
Bollinger %B Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands. %B > 1 = above upper band (overbought); %B < 0 = below lower band (oversold). Works best combined with RSI and volume.
Z-score (price vs 50-day mean) How many standard deviations the price is from its 50-day average. Z > +2 = significantly overbought. Z < −2 = significantly oversold. Bigger absolute Z = stronger stretch signal.
IV (Implied Volatility) The option market's forecast of how much a stock might move. High IV = options are expensive (premiums are fat) → favor selling structures (credit spreads, CSPs). Low IV = options are cheap → favor buying structures (debit spreads).
Composite Rank = 3+ Confirmations A name is ranked higher when RSI, %B, Z-score, and consecutive-day move all agree it's stretched. One signal alone is noise; three or more together is a higher-quality setup.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

Before you act on anything in this report, read these guardrails. They are the most important section on the page.

Reversion vs. Ruin — The Most Important Filter
An oversold RSI is not a buy signal by itself. Stocks can stay oversold for weeks while the business collapses. Before entering any setup, ask:
  • Is there a structural break — fraud, bankruptcy risk, failed drug trial, debt blowup? If yes → skip.
  • Is the drop −50%+ in days with no stabilization? If yes → likely more to come → skip.
  • Is this a buyout repricing? If yes, the stock is pinned to a deal price, not its mean → skip.
  • Does the business have intact fundamentals, and does the move look like sentiment/positioning excess? If yes → potentially a reversion candidate.
  • Is there a nearby support level where buyers have historically stepped in? If yes → higher-quality setup.
Special Handling — Leveraged, Futures, and Vol ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Trade ONLY when stretched UP (vol spike). Never buy them as "oversold" — structural roll decay grinds them lower permanently. The clean trade is fading the spike down.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, TQQQ, TBT, 2x/3x anything): Volatility decay + futures roll = no stable long-run mean. Short-term tactical only (<1–2 weeks). Prefer unlevered alternatives for multi-week holds.
  • Futures ETFs with K-1 (USO, UNG, UCO, DBC): Issue a Schedule K-1 tax form (complex tax filing). Prefer no-K-1 alternatives: PDBC, BCI, COMB for commodity exposure.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX): Track companies, not the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge significantly from spot prices. Account for this in your thesis.
  • Crypto ETFs (IBIT, BITO, ETHA): Treat as high-beta financial instruments. Strong trends, extreme vol. Never size as you would a stock. Binary risk around macro events (FOMC, CPI).
