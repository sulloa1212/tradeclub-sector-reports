7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the methodology — what each indicator means and which structures fit which setup. The body of the report shows the results; this is the "how to read" key.

RSI (14-day): 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be exhausting). Above 70 = overbought (buying may be exhausting). Extreme: <20 or >80.
Bollinger %B: Where price sits within its 20-day band (0=lower, 1=upper). Below 0 or above 1 = outside the band = stretched.
Z-score: How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day mean. Above 2 or below −2 = statistically stretched.
% from 50/200-day: Simple distance from the moving average. The 50-day is the short-term "mean"; the 200-day is the long-term mean.
IV (Implied Volatility): How expensive options are. High IV = option premiums are rich → favor selling structures. Low IV = options are cheap → favor buying structures.
Oversold + High IV → Cash-secured puts / bull put spreads / bull call spreads (defined-risk). Selling puts collects elevated premium while waiting for a bounce.
Oversold + Low IV → Long calls or bull call debit spreads (buying cheap options for a bounce).
Overbought + High IV → Call credit spreads / bear put spreads (sell rich calls above the market, buy cheaper ones higher to cap risk).
Overbought + Low IV → Long puts or bear put debit spreads.
Always defined-risk first: A credit spread, debit spread, or long option defines your maximum loss upfront. Never sell a naked option without understanding unlimited-loss risk. Paper-trade any new structure before risking real capital.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter

A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient for a reversion trade. The scan actively rejects:

  • Structural breaks: fraud, going-concern, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement. These are not "oversold" — they are legitimately repricing lower.
  • Falling knives: drops of 50%+ in days with no stabilization. More likely to keep falling.
  • Buyout/merger pins: stocks pinned to a deal price don't revert to a technical mean.
  • Permanent regime changes: a new geopolitical reality (e.g., a decade of elevated defense spending) means the pre-shock price is not a valid "mean" to revert toward.

Names on today's list cleared this filter: their moves look like sentiment/positioning excess over sound businesses, near prior support/resistance levels — with the MRNA exception explicitly flagged as a new-paradigm binary, not a clean reversion.

Special-handling reminders
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Only trade when stretched UP (spike-fade). Never buy as "oversold" — structural roll decay grinds them lower indefinitely.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, UCO, BOIL): Volatility decay makes them unsuitable for multi-week holds. Short-term tactical only — prefer the unlevered fund for longer plays.
  • K-1 tax forms K-1: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL issue Schedule K-1, complicating tax filing. Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB for broad-commodity exposure without K-1.
  • Proxy funds proxy: GDX, GDXJ, GXE, XOP, URA, COPX track companies, not the commodity directly. They carry stock-market beta and can diverge from the underlying during broad market moves.
  • Earnings landmines: Any earnings within 30 days makes the technical setup secondary. A surprise can gap right through any technical level. Always check your brokerage's earnings calendar before entering.
