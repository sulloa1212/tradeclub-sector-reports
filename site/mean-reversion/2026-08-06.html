7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — how structures are selected by direction and IV. The actual setup-specific readings and "why this read" live in the tables above.

Indicator definitions (what each measure means):
RSI (14) — 0–100 momentum gauge. <30 = oversold (selling exhausted); >70 = overbought (buying stretched). <20 or >80 = extreme.
Bollinger %B (20,2) — Price position inside the band. <0 = below lower band (oversold); >1 = above upper band (overbought).
% from 50-day / 200-day SMA — How far price has stretched from its recent average. Bigger % = bigger rubber-band tension.
Z-Score vs 50-day mean — How many standard deviations (SDs) the price is from its average. Z >2 or <−2 is statistically unusual.
Direction + IV → Defined-risk structure map:
Oversold + Elevated IV → Put Credit Spread (sell lower put, buy even lower put) or Cash-Secured Put. Premium is rich — sell it. Max loss is defined by the spread width.
Overbought + Elevated IV → Call Credit Spread (sell upper call, buy even higher call). Premium is rich on the upside — sell it. Max loss defined.
Oversold + Low IV → Bull Call Spread (buy ATM call, sell OTM call). Debit structure — pay a fixed premium, risk only what you pay.
Overbought + Low IV → Bear Put Spread (buy ATM put, sell lower OTM put). Debit structure — fixed cost, defined max loss.
High IV + binary event → Iron Condor / Short Strangle (advanced, requires broker approval). Sell both sides to collect premium; max loss if price moves beyond your strikes. Paper trade first.
IV definition: Implied Volatility (IV) is the option market's expectation of future price swings. High IV = expensive options (favor selling). Low IV = cheap options (favor buying). "IV Rank" compares current IV to 52-week range.

Always defined-risk first. A credit spread limits max loss to the width of the spread minus the premium received. A debit spread limits max loss to what you paid. Never enter a short naked option (unlimited loss) before understanding the risk and receiving broker approval. Paper trade every structure before using real money.

8Guardrails — Reversion vs. Ruin, Caveats & Verify Before Trading

The most important filter in this whole report: a low RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Here's how to tell a reversion setup from a falling knife.

Reversion-vs-Ruin filter — reject these, keep the rest:
  • Structural breaks: Fraud, going-concern doubt, dividend cut, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, debt blowup, accounting restatement → these are NOT reversion setups. The mean has moved permanently.
  • Falling knives: A -50%+ move in days with no stabilization candle, no prior support, and no clear catalyst end point. SPCX today (lockup expiry) is borderline — wait for the dust to settle.
  • Buyout/merger repricing: Stock pinned to a deal price, not to its historical mean. Drop these immediately.
  • Permanent regime change: A business model that has been disrupted structurally (e.g., AI competitor eating margins). Earnings-miss + guide-down + analyst downgrades = possible regime change, not a bounce.

Keep names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business, ideally near prior support/resistance.

Special handling — Vol, Leveraged & Futures ETFs:
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY) — spikes only. Trade ONLY when stretched UP; they revert down as the spike fades. A low RSI on VXX is just roll-decay drift — not a buy signal.
  • Inverse vol (SVXY, SVIX). Grind up in calm markets, crater on vol spikes. Don't fade their up-moves as mean reversion.
  • Leveraged/futures ETFs (SCO, UCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, TBT, 2x/3x anything). Volatility decay + contango/backwardation = diverge from any long-run mean. Short-term tactical only (1–5 days). Prefer unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy for multi-week holds.
  • K-1 tax flag. USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, CPER issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — a complex partnership tax form. Prefer "no K-1" alternatives: PDBC (broad commodities), BCI, COMB. Check with your tax advisor.
  • Proxy vs. spot. GDX/GDXJ track gold miner stocks, not gold. URA tracks uranium companies, not uranium. XOP, COPX, MOO are equity baskets. They carry stock-market beta and can diverge sharply from the underlying commodity, especially in risk-off moves.
Composite-stretch rule — 3+ measures beats RSI alone:

A name extreme on RSI (14) + Bollinger %B + % from 50-day SMA = three confirmations. Adding Z-score and consecutive down-day count makes it stronger. A name with RSI 28 but normal Bollinger and flat relative to its 50-day is a weaker setup than one extreme on all three. Quality beats quantity — the shorter this list, the more conviction belongs in each setup.
