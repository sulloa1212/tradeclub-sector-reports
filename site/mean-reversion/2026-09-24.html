7Options Structures — Educational Legend

This section explains what each structure is (the method). The specific candidates and why each fits are in Sections 2–5 above.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling

  • Cash-secured put: Sell a put option, hold enough cash to buy 100 shares if assigned. You collect premium; max loss = stock goes to zero. Best when you'd be OK owning the stock cheaper.
  • Bull put spread: Sell a put at strike A, buy a lower put at strike B. Net credit received; max loss is the spread width minus credit. Defined risk. Ideal for oversold + high IV — you collect inflated premium with a capped downside.
  • Bull call spread (debit): Buy a call at strike A, sell a higher call at strike B. Net debit paid; max gain = spread width minus debit. Best for oversold + low IV — you pay less for the long call.

Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling

  • Call credit spread: Sell a call at strike A, buy a higher call at strike B. Net credit received; max loss = spread width minus credit. Defines the risk against a stock that might keep running up.
  • Bear put spread (debit): Buy a put at strike A, sell a lower put at strike B. Net debit paid; max gain = spread width minus debit. Best for overbought + lower IV — directional bet on a pullback.

How the indicators work

  • RSI (0–100 overbought/oversold gauge): Below 30 = oversold (selling pressure extreme). Above 70 = overbought (buying pressure extreme). Extremes are signals, not guarantees.
  • Bollinger %B: Measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands. Above 1 = above the upper band (stretched up). Below 0 = below the lower band (stretched down).
  • Z-score: How many standard deviations (units of typical daily variability) price is away from its 50-day mean. ±2 = notable; ±3 = extreme.
  • % from 50/200-day SMA: How far price has drifted from its average — the "mean" it may revert toward.
  • IV (implied volatility): The market's expectation of future price swings baked into option prices. High IV = expensive options → favors selling premium. Low IV = cheap options → favors buying.

8How to Read This Report · Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter. A low RSI is not a buy signal by itself. Before acting on any oversold name, ask: Is this a sentiment flush or a fundamental break? Reject any name showing: fraud or going-concern doubt; bankruptcy/restructuring risk; failed drug trial with no pipeline; dividend cut as a signal of distress; or a stock down 50%+ in days with no stabilization. The names in Section 2 passed this filter — but re-verify the thesis holds at the time you trade.

Special handling rules (non-negotiable)

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY) — UPSIDE FADES ONLY. These lose value every day due to VIX-futures roll decay (contango). A low RSI on VXX/UVXY is not an oversold buy signal — it's structural decay. Only trade them when they spike UP sharply on a fear event, as a fade. That setup is not active today.
  • Inverse-vol (SVXY, SVIX): They grind higher in calm markets and crash on VIX spikes. Don't fade their uptrends; the setup is recovery after a vol event, not a reversion play.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT): Volatility decay + roll cost = they deviate from any stable mean. Use for short-term tactical trades only (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, use the unleveraged fund or the equity-sector proxy.
  • K-1 tax forms: USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, and most commodity partnerships issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time — complex, can arrive late, may cause AMT issues. Prefer no-K-1 alternatives: PDBC (broad commodity), BCI/COMB. Flagged in the tables with K-1.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, GDXJ, XOP, URA, COPX, SIL, MOO): Track companies (miners, producers), NOT the underlying commodity. They carry equity beta, management risk, and can diverge widely from the spot commodity. Flagged with proxy.
  • Earnings/binary events: Any setup with a confirmed earnings date within ~30 days carries extra risk — the binary event can override the technical setup entirely. Always verify the next earnings date in your own brokerage before trading. RTX (Oct 27) is the confirmed near-term earnings name in today's scan.

Paper-trade first. If you've never traded options spreads before, practice in a simulator before risking real money. The mechanics of spreads — expiration, assignment, spread widening — can surprise beginners. Always start small, define your risk, and never risk more than you can afford to lose entirely.
