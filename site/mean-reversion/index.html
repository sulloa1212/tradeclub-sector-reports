7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method and terminology — the actual setups for each ticker are in Sections 2–5 above. Always paper-trade any new structure before using real money.

RSI(14) Runs 0–100. Below 30 = recent selling very fast (oversold). Above 70 = recent buying very fast (overbought). Below 20 / above 80 = extreme. It measures speed, not fundamental value — a stock can be oversold and keep falling.
Bollinger %B Measures where price sits relative to its 20-day moving average ± 2 standard deviations. <0 = below the lower band (stretched low); >1 = above the upper band (stretched high). Confirms RSI when both are extreme together.
Z-score How many standard deviations (σ) the price is from its 50-day average. ±2σ or beyond is statistically unusual — roughly 95% of observations are within ±2σ in a normal distribution. Real markets have fatter tails.
Stochastics A 0–100 oscillator comparing closing price to recent high-low range. <20 = oversold, >80 = overbought. When Stochastics and RSI are BOTH extreme, the signal is stronger (2 confirmations).
Bull call spread Bullish, defined risk. Buy a lower-strike call + sell a higher-strike call, same expiry. Max loss = net debit paid. Profits if stock rises. Good for oversold names with high IV (keeps debit lower).
Bear put spread Bearish, defined risk. Buy a higher-strike put + sell a lower-strike put, same expiry. Max loss = net debit paid. Profits if stock falls. Good for overbought names.
Put credit spread Bullish, premium-selling, defined risk. Sell a put at one strike + buy a lower-strike put. Collect a credit. Profits if stock stays above the short strike. Best when IV is elevated (you collect more premium).
Call credit spread Bearish, premium-selling, defined risk. Sell a call + buy a higher-strike call. Collect a credit. Profits if stock stays below the short strike. Best when IV is elevated on an overbought name.
IV note "IV elevated" means implied volatility (the market's estimate of future price swings, priced into options) is high relative to its recent average. High IV makes option premium more expensive — favors selling premium (credit spreads). Low IV favors buying options (debit spreads).
Reference mean The price level a "mean-reverting" asset might return to — typically the 50-day SMA (simple moving average, the average closing price over the last 50 trading days). It is not a price target or guarantee.
Oversold + High IV →
Put credit spread / Bull call spread / Cash-secured put (if willing to own the stock)
Oversold + Low IV →
Long call / Bull call spread (debit spread is cheaper when IV is low)
Overbought + High IV →
Call credit spread / Bear put spread
Overbought + Low IV →
Long put / Bear put spread

Always use defined-risk structures first. Never sell naked options until you deeply understand the unlimited-loss risk. Paper-trade every structure before using real capital.

8How to Read This Report & Guardrails

The most important filter: Reversion vs. Ruin
A low RSI is necessary but not sufficient for a mean-reversion trade. The question is: did the stock fall because of a temporary sentiment/positioning excess — or because of a permanent change to the business? Keep the first; reject the second.
Reject: Likely RuinKeep: Likely Reversion
Fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trial, bankruptcy, accounting restatement Sentiment/positioning excess — stock down sharply on macro/political fear, business fundamentally intact
−50%+ in days with no stabilization Near prior support/resistance; identifiable pre-shock "mean" to revert toward
Buyout/merger pin (stock locked to deal price, not its own mean) Oversold cluster: RSI + Stochastics + Williams %R + CCI all extreme together
Structural competitive loss of market share with no recovery path Catalyst is fading (a data print, a political event) rather than structural

Special ETF Handling — Read Before Trading

  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade only when RSI is stretched HIGH (a vol spike). Never buy them as "oversold" — structural roll decay (futures contango) grinds them lower over time. A low RSI is drift, not a setup.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, BOIL, TQQQ, SQQQ, TBT, any 2x/3x): Volatility decay and roll costs mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical trades (days, not weeks). For multi-week holds, use the unlevered equivalent or an equity-sector proxy.
  • Futures-based / K-1 ETFs (USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, COPER): These may issue a Schedule K-1 tax form, which complicates tax reporting. Prefer K-1-free alternatives (PDBC, BCI, COMB) where the exposure is equivalent.
