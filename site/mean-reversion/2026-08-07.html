7Options Structures Legend

This section explains the educational options structures mentioned in the tables. These are concepts, not trade recommendations — always paper trade first and confirm current IV levels in your own platform before acting.

📈 Oversold + High IV → Put Credit Spread
Sell a put at a lower strike, buy a put at an even lower strike. You collect premium if the stock stays above your short strike. Maximum loss is the spread width minus credit received. Best when IV is high (you collect more premium) and you expect the stock to stop falling or bounce. Defined risk — you cannot lose more than the spread width.
📈 Oversold + Low IV → Bull Call Spread
Buy a call at a lower strike, sell a call at a higher strike. You profit if the stock rises above your long strike. Cost is limited; max gain is the spread width minus what you paid. Best when IV is low (calls are cheaper). Defined risk — max loss is the premium paid.
📉 Overbought + High IV → Call Credit Spread
Sell a call at a lower strike, buy a call at a higher strike. You collect premium if the stock stays below your short strike. Best when IV is high (you collect more) and you expect a pullback or sideways move. Defined risk — max loss is spread width minus credit.
📉 Overbought + Low IV → Bear Put Spread
Buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. You profit if the stock falls below your long strike. Best when IV is low (puts are cheaper). Defined risk — max loss is premium paid.
RSI — What It Measures
RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a 0–100 gauge of how fast a stock has moved recently. Below 30 = recent selling has been intense ("oversold"). Above 70 = recent buying has been intense ("overbought"). Extreme readings (<20 or >80) are rarer and stronger signals. RSI alone is not enough — combine with %B, distance from moving averages, and a fundamental check.
Bollinger %B — What It Measures
%B measures where price sits within its Bollinger Bands (a volatility envelope around the 20-day moving average). %B < 0 means price is below the lower band (oversold extreme); %B > 1 means price is above the upper band (overbought extreme). Used alongside RSI to confirm a stretch.
Z-Score — What It Measures
Z-score measures how many standard deviations (units of typical volatility) a price is from its moving average. A Z-score below −2 means price is unusually far below its average (oversold by history). Above +2 means unusually far above (overbought). Helps quantify "how stretched is stretched."
IV (Implied Volatility) — Why It Matters
IV is the options market's forecast of future price swings (expressed as an annualized %). High IV = options are expensive; this favors selling premium (credit spreads). Low IV = options are cheap; this favors buying premium (debit spreads). For mean-reversion, high IV is ideal because you collect rich premium while betting the move will reverse.

8How to Read This Report / Guardrails

The one rule that beats all the others: An oversold RSI does not mean "buy now." It means "selling has been intense — look for a reason it might stop." Always complete the three-step check before acting on any setup.
Step 1 — Is it actually stretched?
Confirm RSI, %B, and distance from moving average in YOUR platform. Screener data is a starting point, not gospel.
Step 2 — Is it a reversion or a ruin?

Step 3 — Is there a binary coming?

Special caveats for this report

  • Volatility ETPs (VXX, UVXY, UVIX, VIXY): Only trade these when they are stretched UP (during a vol spike). Their structural decay makes low RSI readings meaningless as buy signals. Today: no spike = no fade trade.
  • Leveraged ETFs (TBT, UCO, TQQQ, BOIL, UNG): Daily rebalancing creates volatility decay. Only use for short-term tactical trades (1–5 days). For multi-week views, use the underlying unlevered fund or equity-sector proxy.
