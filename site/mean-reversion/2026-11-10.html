7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — which structure fits which situation. The specific setups appear in the tables above. All structures below are defined-risk (your maximum loss is known before you enter). Never use undefined-risk strategies (naked calls/puts) as a beginner.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling (Bullish)
Put Credit Spread: Sell a put below support, buy a lower put to cap risk. You collect premium upfront; profit if the stock stays above your short put.
Cash-Secured Put: Sell a put and hold enough cash to buy shares if assigned. Defined risk; works best when you're willing to own the stock at that price.
Bull Call Spread: Buy a call, sell a higher call. Defined cost and profit; works if the stock bounces to your target. Preferred when IV is so high that puts are too expensive.
Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling (Bearish)
Call Credit Spread: Sell a call above resistance, buy a higher call to cap risk. Collect premium upfront; profit if the stock stays below your short call.
Bear Put Spread: Buy a put, sell a lower put. Defined cost and profit; works if the stock declines. Best when IV is relatively low (puts are cheap).
Oversold + Low IV → Outright Buying (Bullish)
Long Call or Bull Call Spread: When IV is low, buying calls is relatively cheap — the edge shifts toward buying premium, not selling it. Use bull call spreads to reduce cost.
Overbought + Low IV → Outright Buying (Bearish)
Long Put or Bear Put Spread: When IV is low, buying puts is cheap. Use a bear put spread to reduce cost: buy a put at resistance, sell a lower put.
RSI (14-day)
0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = oversold (selling may be overdone); above 70 = overbought (buying may be extended); 20/80 = extreme readings.
Bollinger %B
Where price sits within its Bollinger Bands. Below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold); above 1 = outside the upper band (overbought). Confirms RSI signals.
Z-Score
How many standard deviations (σ) the price is from its 50-day average. ±2σ is unusual; ±3σ is rare. Bigger number = more stretched.
% from 50d / 200d SMA
How far the price is above or below its 50-day or 200-day simple moving average (a smoothed average price). Negative = below the mean; positive = above. The mean is what the price "reverts toward."
IV (Implied Volatility)
What options markets "imply" about future price swings. High IV = expensive options → selling premium has an edge. Low IV = cheap options → buying premium has an edge.
Composite Score
Names scoring on 3+ measures (RSI + %B + Z-score + % from moving average) rank higher than names extreme on RSI alone. Quality beats quantity.

8How to Read / Guardrails

The most important filter: Reversion vs. Ruin

A low RSI is necessary, but it is not sufficient. We reject "falling knives" and permanent re-ratings. The filter is simple to remember:

  • Keep names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business — ideally near a prior support level.
  • Reject names with structural breaks: fraud, going-concern doubt, failed drug trials, bankruptcy filing, massive accounting restatement, or buyout repricing (the stock is now pinned to a deal price, not its mean).
  • Reject names down 50%+ in days with no stabilization signal — these are likely falling further.
  • Flag (don't reject outright) names with company-specific risk layered on top of the technical setup — like NVDA's CEO probe. These can still bounce, but the binary risk is higher.
Special handling — Vol, Leveraged & Futures ETFs
  • Long-vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY, VIXY): Trade only when RSI is stretched UP on a spike. Never treat a low RSI on these as an oversold buy — structural roll decay means they drift lower over time. The clean trade is fading the spike.
  • Leveraged ETFs (UCO, SCO, TQQQ, etc.): Daily rebalancing and volatility decay mean they drift from any long-term mean. Use for short-term tactical holds only. For multi-week exposure, prefer the unlevered ETF.
  • Futures-based ETFs with K-1 (USO, UNG, UGA, etc.): These issue a Schedule K-1 at tax time (a more complex tax form). The alternative "No K-1" funds (PDBC for broad commodities; BCI/COMB where available) may be simpler for most accounts. Always confirm with your tax professional.
  • Proxy ETFs (GDX, XOP, URA): These hold company stocks, not the commodity itself. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying commodity price. GDX tracks gold miner stocks — not the same as tracking gold.
Dates are the #1 hallucination risk — always verify

Every earnings date in this report came from a live web search in this run. Even so, companies change reporting dates. Treat every earnings date as an estimate and confirm against your own brokerage, the company's IR page, or the SEC before structuring any options trade around it. A wrong earnings date can turn a defined-risk spread into a live binary surprise.
