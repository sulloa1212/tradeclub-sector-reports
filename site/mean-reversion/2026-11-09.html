7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the educational structures referenced in the tables above. All structures involve options — defined-risk means your maximum loss is known before you enter.

Oversold + High IV → Premium Selling

Cash-Secured Put: Sell a put below current price. You collect premium now; if the stock falls to that level, you buy it at the strike. Max risk = strike price × 100 (less premium received).

Bull Put Spread: Sell a lower put, buy an even lower put. Net credit received. Max loss is capped at the spread width minus credit. Defined risk — the safest way to express "I think the stock bounces."

Bull Call Spread: Buy a call, sell a higher call. Net debit paid. Benefits from a rally but limits cost (and risk) versus buying a naked call.

Overbought + High IV → Premium Selling (Short-Side)

Call Credit Spread (Bear Call Spread): Sell a call above current price, buy a higher call as a cap. Net credit. Profits if the stock stays below the lower strike. Max loss is the spread width minus credit received.

Bear Put Spread: Buy a put, sell a lower put. Net debit. Benefits from a price decline. Defined risk — maximum loss = net debit paid.

What the Indicators Mean

RSI (14): 0–100 momentum gauge. Below 30 = recent selling extreme; above 70 = recent buying extreme. It tells you how fast the move was, not that it's over.

Bollinger %B: Where price sits within its Bollinger Band. Below 0 = outside lower band (stretched down); above 1 = outside upper band (stretched up).

Z-score: How many standard deviations price is from its 50-day average. ±2 or beyond is a statistical outlier.

IV (Implied Volatility): How expensive options are. High IV = options cost more → premiums are worth selling. Low IV = options are cheap → buying options can be more attractive.

Proxy ETF: Tracks companies (like miners or drillers), not the commodity itself. Carries equity beta and may diverge from the spot price.

K-1 flag: Some ETFs (USO, UNG, DBC, etc.) issue a Schedule K-1 tax form instead of a 1099. This complicates tax filing. Prefer "No K-1" alternatives (PDBC, BITO) where available.

Leveraged/Futures ETFs: Volatility decay and contango/backwardation roll mean they deviate from any long-run mean. Use only for short-term tactical trades.

Long-Vol ETPs (VXX, UVXY): Structurally decay from contango. Only fade the spike — never buy the dip.
