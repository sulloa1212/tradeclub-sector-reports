7Options Structures Legend

This legend explains the generic method — what the indicators measure and which educational structures match which scenarios. The specific results for each name are in the tables above.

RSI (0–100 overbought/oversold gauge) Measures how fast a price moved recently vs. its own history. Below 30 = oversold (selling pressure extreme). Above 70 = overbought. Extreme: below 20 / above 80.
Bollinger %B (band position gauge) Shows where price sits within its 20-day band. Below 0 = outside the lower band (oversold extreme). Above 1 = outside the upper band (overbought extreme).
% from 50-day / 200-day SMA SMA (Simple Moving Average) = the mean price over that window. The % tells you how far price has stretched from that mean. A −10% reading means the price is 10% below its 50-day average.
Z-score (standard-deviation stretch) How many standard deviations price sits above/below its 50-day mean. Above +2 or below −2 = statistically extreme. Higher magnitude = stronger stretch.
Oversold + High IV → Selling premium Bull put credit spread (sell a put, buy a lower-strike put to cap risk). Cash-secured put (sell one put, hold cash to buy 100 shares). Goal: collect premium while waiting for a bounce. Risk is defined to the spread width.
Oversold + Low IV → Buying Bull call debit spread (buy a call, sell a higher-strike call to reduce cost). Or long call outright. Lower IV = cheaper options = buying makes more sense.
Overbought + High IV → Selling premium Bear call credit spread (sell a call, buy a higher-strike call to cap risk). Goal: collect premium while waiting for a pullback. Risk is defined to the spread width.
Overbought + Low IV → Buying puts Bear put debit spread (buy a put, sell a lower-strike put to reduce cost). Use when IV is low and you expect a fast reversal.
Defined risk always first. Every structure above has a pre-defined maximum loss — the spread width. Never sell naked (uncovered) options as a first step. Paper-trade all structures before using real money. IV (implied volatility — the market's forecast of future price swings) is high when options are expensive and low when they are cheap; selling premium is generally better when IV is high.

8How to Read / Guardrails

Reversion vs. Ruin — the most important filter. An oversold RSI is necessary but not sufficient. Reject names where the decline is driven by fraud, accounting restatements, bankruptcy, failed drug trials, or buyout re-pricing. Keep only names where the move looks like sentiment/positioning excess over an otherwise sound business. If a stock is down 50%+ in days with no stabilization, it is probably a falling knife — not a reversion setup.
Proxy flag GDX, XOP, XLE, OIH, COPX, URA, MOO track companies, not the raw commodity. They carry equity beta and can diverge from the underlying. A crude oil drop hits XOP harder if energy stocks also de-rate on valuation.
K-1 tax flag ⚠ USO, UNG, UGA, DBC, UCO, BOIL, certain CurrencyShares issue a Schedule K-1 (a complex partnership tax form). Prefer PDBC, BCI, or COMB for commodity exposure without K-1.
Leveraged / Futures ETF warning TQQQ, SQQQ, UCO, SCO, BOIL, KOLD, TBT, TMF — volatility decay and contango/backwardation roll mean these products deviate from any long-run mean. Use for short-term tactical plays only; for multi-week holds prefer the unlevered fund or equity proxy.
Earnings / event dates ⚠ A known binary event (earnings, FDA, Fed decision) can override any technical setup — the stock can move 10–30% regardless of RSI. Always verify the next earnings date in your brokerage before entering a position. Every date in this report that is flagged "verify" must be confirmed from a live source.
